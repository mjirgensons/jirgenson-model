- **Price −20%, i.e. €120 per member.** There are two readings of the variable costs:
  - **(a) Gold reading: variable costs stay 20% of revenue.** Revenue 14,400. Variable 2,880. Profit = 14,400 − 2,880 − 11,900 = **−€380**. For a 25% margin: revenue = 11,900 / 0.55 ≈ 21,636, which is ≈180 members (+50%).

Profit at 120 members = 120 × 90 − 11,900 = **−€1,100** (−7.6% of 14,400). Break-even = 11,900 / 90 = 132.2, so **133 members**. Today's profit needs (11,900 + 2,500) / 90 = **160 members**. Today's revenue needs **150 members**. A 25% margin needs 90n − 11,900 = 30n, so n = 198.3, i.e. **199 members (+79, +66%)**.
  - Reading (b) is the more literal reading of the task's wording. It is a different but correct route to the same conclusion: the cut turns profit into a loss, and the members needed for 25% rise far above any realistic growth. I accept either reading.
- **25% margin without the cut:** 120n − 11,900 = 37.5n, so n = 144.2, i.e. **145 members**. Or, at 120 members, the profit must be €4,500, which needs **€2,000/month less cost**.

  - Holding a 25% margin at the cut price: 1,000 / 60 = 16.7, so **17 members**.
- **The cut's cost:** 120 × €30 = **€3,600/month**. This equals the contribution of 30 members at €120. So the cut pays only if, without it, more than **25%** of members would leave.
- **Tax arrears:** €9,000 = 9,000 / 2,500 = **3.6 months** of profit, or **€750/month** over 12 months (30% of current profit).
- **Sensitivity check on Response 1's claim:** shift the variable/fixed split by 10%.
