## Step 1. Recomputed key numbers

- Revenue €18,000 from 120 members, so €150 per member. Costs €15,500, of which €3,600 are variable and €11,900 are fixed. Profit is €2,500/month and the margin is 2,500 / 18,000 = **13.9 %**.
- Variable cost per member: 3,600 / 120 = **€30**. That is also 20 % of revenue at today's price. Contribution at €150 is €120 per member. Break-even: 11,900 / 120 = 99.2, so about **100 members**.
- Price −20 % = €120. The two ways of reading the variable costs give different results:
  - **Per member** (the literal reading of "vary with the number of members"): revenue 14,400 − 11,900 − 3,600 = **−€1,100/month**. Contribution is €90 per member, so break-even is 11,900 / 90 = 132.2, about **133 members**. Keeping today's €2,500 profit needs 14,400 / 90 = **160 members** (+33 %). Profit at 150 members is 13,500 − 11,900 = **€1,600**. A 25 % margin needs 60n = 11,900, so n ≈ **198–200 members (+66 %)**.
  - **20 % of revenue** (the gold route): 14,400 − 11,900 − 2,880 = **−€380/month**. A 25 % margin needs revenue of 11,900 / 0.55 ≈ **€21,636**, about **180 members (+50 %)**.
  - Both routes show that goals 3 and 4 cannot both hold without roughly +50–66 % members. The per-member route is a correct, arguably more literal route, so I accept it as reaching the same conclusion.
- A 25 % margin at €150 needs 82.5n = 11,900, so n ≈ **144–145 members** (revenue ≈ €21,636–21,750).
- Each extra €1,000 of fixed cost needs extra members. At €150: 1,000/120 ≈ 8 to cover it, or 1,000/82.5 ≈ 12 to keep 25 %. At €120: 1,000/90 ≈ 11 to cover it, or 1,000/60 ≈ 17 to keep 25 %.
- Tax arrears: €9,000 = **3.6 months of current profit**. Paid over 12 months it is €750/month, 30 % of profit. It is an obligation, not an optional goal.
- Cash/reserve: **not given**.
- Price-cut scenarios at €120: 140 members gives €700 profit (4.2 %); 160 members gives €2,500 (13.0 %).
