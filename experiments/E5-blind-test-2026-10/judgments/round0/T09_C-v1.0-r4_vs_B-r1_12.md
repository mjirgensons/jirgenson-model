# Judgment — T09 (Newspaper kiosk in a small town), Response 1 vs Response 2

## Step 1 — Recomputed key numbers

- 2023→2024: 48,000 → 42,000 = −6,000 / 48,000 = **−12.5 %**.
- 2024→2025: 42,000 → 37,000 = −5,000 / 42,000 = **−11.9 %**.
- 2026 run-rate: 25,000 / 9 × 12 = **33,333 €** → vs 37,000 = **−9.9 %** (≈ −10 %). Monthly: 25,000/9 = 2,778 €/month vs 37,000/12 = 3,083 €/month in 2025.
- Third consecutive year of decline; population 8,000 → 6,000 = **−25 %** in five years; main factory closed 2023.
- +30 % on 33,333 = **43,333 €** (more than 2024's 42,000), i.e. ≈ +10,000 € of extra turnover against the trend.
- Profit/hour: 6,000 / (55 × 48 = 2,640 h) = **2.27 €/h**; with 52 weeks (2,860 h) = **2.10 €/h**. Either way ≈ 2 €/h.
- Total expenses 2025 = 37,000 − 6,000 = 31,000 € (≈ 2,580 €/month, but most of this is cost of goods that falls with sales; it is not a cash burn the reserve must cover).
- "Nothing changes" 2027: 33,333 × 0.88–0.90 ≈ 29,300–30,000 € → profit near zero to a few thousand depending on how much of the cost base is fixed.
- Lease expires in 8 months ≈ June 2027 (today is mid-October 2026). Reserve 4,000 €, no stated debts → the cost of a wrong exit is low.

## Step 2 — Mandatory elements

### Response 1
- **M1 = 1.** Latvian throughout, everyday words; none of "vector/parameter/zone/manoeuvre". "Red line" is ordinary language.
- **M2 = 1.** "–12.5% (2024), –12% (2025), and €25,000 in nine months means about €33,000 this year (–10%)". Correct.
- **M3 = 1.** "The town itself works against it: from 8,000 down to 6,000 residents and the biggest factory closed. That is stronger than any advertising." It also opens with "Turnover cannot be raised by 30% next year with marketing".
- **M4 = 1.** It does not deliver a +30 % plan: "Growth of 30% would be about €43,000, more than in 2024", set against the shrinking town. The main answer is the lease decision plus cheap tests.
- **M5 = 0.** The lease is named as the key decision ("the most important decision ... extending the lease", "around June 2027"). The owner's time is valued (≈ 2 €/h). There is a cheap-test-before-renewing path, and a condition for staying ("at least one service makes a profit"). The short-extension-with-exit-right request is a smart way to limit the damage. But the response never says that the cost of a wrong exit is low. It also never names an orderly exit with the 4,000 € intact; exit appears only as "another source of income". That core part of the element is missing.
- **M6 = 1.** Nothing changes: "in 2027 about €29,000–31,000, profit ... from about –€2,000 ... to about €5,000". Plan works: "illustratively ... 5–10% (a guess, not a promise), about €31,000–34,000". Both have numbers and the plan case is labelled. The plan case has no profit figure, but the turnover range and its labelling meet the element.
- **M7 = 1.** Measurable conditions: weekly revenue vs the same week last year plus the reserve balance; "If for three months in a row turnover is more than 12% lower ..., we make the decision about the lease earlier"; red line reserve < 2,600 €; a review on 15 January and a decision by March 2027. Exactly 3 questions (rent and fixed costs; whether the 4,000 € is the whole family reserve; other job or income). They differ from the key's examples, but the third partly covers what the owner would give up.
- **M8 = 1.** ≈ 554 whitespace tokens including headings and numerals, so under 600.

Total: 7/8.

### Response 2

- **M2 = 1.** "by 10–12% a year", "about €2,800 a month versus €3,100 last year", "about €33,000 (32–35 thousand)". Correct.
- **M3 = 0.** The environment is touched only in passing: "more than in 2024, when the town was bigger". The response never names the shrinking town (population −25 %, factory closure) as the strongest force, stronger than anything the owner can do. It says +30 % is "unlikely" and frames the cause as the business "keeps shrinking", not the town. That is a vague gesture, not the element.
- **M4 = 1.** No generic +30 % plan as the main answer. It shows the requirement concretely: 43,000 €, more than 2024 when the town was larger, ≈ +10,000 €, ≈ 30 € every working day. The main thrust is the lease decision plus cheap tests.
- **M5 = 0.** The lease is named as the biggest money decision, and "The marketing results need to be known before this decision" is strong. Options are explicit ("renew at a lower rent" vs "do not renew"), cheap tests come first, 3,500 € stays in reserve, and time is valued (≈ 2 €/h, "well below the minimum wage"). But the low cost of a wrong exit is never stated or weighed. Same gap as Response 1.
- **M6 = 1.** Nothing changes: 29–31 k €, profit 3,500–4,500 € (1.2–1.6 €/h). Plan works: 34–37 k €, profit 5,000–6,000 €. Full +30 %: 43 k €, 7,000–7,500 €. Assumptions are labelled (25–30 cents kept per extra euro, fixed costs unchanged).
- **M7 = 1.** Measurable conditions: weekly revenue vs last year; "if it shrinks to 5% or less, something is working"; check-points on 15 December and at the end of February ("if January and February are not better ... then ... comparing 'renew at a lower rent' and 'do not renew'"); a deadline two weeks before the notice date. Two question marks (three sub-questions), within the limit. The questions overlap with the "today" step, which already asks the owner to look up rent, notice term and category split.
- **M8 = 1.** ≈ 536 whitespace tokens, under 600.

Total: 6/8.

## Step 3 — Forbidden items and invented facts

### Response 1
- **Forbidden items: none.**
  - F1: not present. Facebook posts are one of three small tests capped at 300 €, not the answer.
  - F2: not present.
  - F3: not present. It does not declare the kiosk dead, and it tests niches (parcel pick-up, bill payment, delivery).
  - F4: not present. Priorities are proposed ("My suggestion ... if it is different for you, tell me") and the decision is left to the owner ("The decision is yours").
- **Invented facts: none.** All figures come from the task numbers or are labelled as proposals or guesses ("my suggestion", "a guess, not a promise"). One weakness, not an invented fact: it treats all 31,000 € of expenses as if they were monthly cash burn ("the €4,000 savings cover about one and a half months"). Its worst case ("costs do not fall") is labelled but implausible, because cost of goods falls with sales. The claim that the reserve would near 2,600 € by the lease end follows only from that worst case (−2,000 €/yr ≈ −1,330 € over 8 months). It is internally consistent but overstates the downside.

## Step 4 — Criteria

### Response 1
- **C1 = 8.** All core arithmetic is correct (−12.5 %, −12 %, 33,000 €, −10 %, 43,000 €, 2,860 h → ≈ 2 €/h, 31,000 € expenses) and every assumption is labelled. The "reserve covers 1.5 months of expenses" framing and the all-costs-fixed worst case are conceptually weak but labelled, and they do not change the advice.
- **C2 = 8.** It names the shrinking town as stronger than any advertising, and the lease expiry (≈ June 2027) as the decisive event that would hit the reserve. It does not weigh the low cost of exiting.
- **C3 = 8.** Steps are concrete and owner-doable: find the notice term; by 31 October ask in writing for a 6–12 month extension with a 1–3 month exit right or lower rent; log sales by hour and category for two weeks; run three sequenced tests by December within 300 €; decide by March. There is no "today" step, and "I will prepare the numbers" is an odd promise.
- **C4 = 8.** Latvian, plain words, within 600 words and 3 questions. It answers the real question honestly. The blunt opening refusal ("cannot") could feel abrupt, but it is justified.
- **C5 = 9.** Priorities are proposed with the choice left to the owner. The red line (reserve < 2,600 €) has a reason and is tested against the worst-case forecast. Weekly Monday metrics, a 15 January review and an early-decision trigger (3 months > 12 % down) are set. An explicit "do not" list covers long lease, loan, > 300 € on ads and new equipment.
- **C6 = 8.** No promises ("a guess, not a promise"); two forecasts with ranges and a stated condition; it says plainly that 43,000 € is out of reach. The plan-works scenario has no profit figure, and the very wide nothing-changes profit range (−2,000 to +5,000 €) is less informative.

### Response 2
- **C1 = 7.** Numbers are correct and the assumptions behind the forecasts are labelled. The marginal-margin model is more realistic than Response 1's. Two external facts are stated without being in the task (minimum wage, lottery ad rules; both broadly true), and the plan-works profit is overstated by ≈ 300 €.
- **C2 = 7.** Strong on the lease as the biggest money decision and on marketing results being needed before it. The ≈ 2 €/h finding is sharp. The shrinking town is barely named as the cause, and the low exit cost is not weighed.
- **C3 = 8.** Clear today / this week / this month structure with owner-doable actions: pull 24 months of category sales from the till, open the lease, ask the landlord about terms without committing, pick 2–3 tests and run them within 300–500 €. Test ideas are less prioritised and sequenced, and the lease ask is less sharp than Response 1's exit-right request.
- **C4 = 8.** Latvian, plain words, within 600 words and 2 question marks. It engages with the marketing request while reframing it. The questions duplicate the "today" lookups.
- **C5 = 6.** Good measurement design: weekly comparison, a ≤ 5 % "working" threshold, a 15 December check, an end-February decision condition and a deadline relative to the notice date; 3,500 € kept in reserve. No priority order is proposed, there is no explicit leave-untouched list, and the stop rule leads only to "compare", not to a red line.
- **C6 = 9.** "There are no guarantees". Three scenarios with ranges and labelled assumptions, each with €/hour, and "unlikely". The honest point that even a full +30 % leaves under 3 €/h is very useful for this owner.

## Step 6 — Preference

**Response 1 is better, confidence 0.65.**

Response 1 names the shrinking town as the force marketing cannot beat. It also asks for a short lease extension with an exit right, which is the move that most limits the damage of a wrong decision. It has a stronger control layer: proposed priorities, a reasoned red line tested against the forecast, an early-decision trigger and a do-not list.

Response 2 has a cleaner today/week/month sequence and a more realistic, more honest forecast model (€/h in every scenario, under 3 €/h even at +30 %). But it underplays the environment, proposes no priorities or red line, and both responses miss the explicit point that exiting is cheap.
