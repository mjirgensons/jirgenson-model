## Step 1. Recomputed key numbers

- Revenue €18,000 = 120 × €150. Costs €15,500, so profit is €2,500/month and margin is 2,500 / 18,000 = **13.9 %**.
- Variable €3,600, which is €30 per member (20 % of revenue at €150). Fixed costs: 15,500 − 3,600 = **€11,900**. Contribution per member at €150 is **€120**.
- Break-even at €150: 11,900 / 120 = 99.2, so **100 members**.
- Price −20 % (€120), same 120 members. Revenue is **€14,400**.
  - The task says the €3,600 "varies with the number of members", so variable stays €30/member (€3,600). Profit = 14,400 − 3,600 − 11,900 = **−€1,100/month**.
  - The answer key instead treats variable cost as 20 % of revenue (€2,880), which gives **−€380/month**.
  - Either way it is a loss. The per-member reading follows the task's wording more literally, so I accept it as a correct route.
- Break-even at €120 (per-member model): 11,900 / 90 = 132.2, so **133 members**.
- 25 % margin at €120 (per-member model): 90N − 11,900 = 0.25 × 120N, so 60N = 11,900 and N = 198.3, about **199 members (+66 %)**. The answer key's revenue-share model gives revenue of 11,900 / 0.55 ≈ €21,636, about **180 members (+50 %)**. Under both models the price cut and the 25 % margin cannot both hold without a large rise in members.
- 25 % margin at €150 (per-member model): 120N − 11,900 = 37.5N, so N = 144.2, about **145 members (+21 %)**.
- Revenue returns to €18,000 at €120 with **150 members**.
- Each €1,000 of extra monthly fixed cost needs this many extra members:
  - To keep a 25 % margin: 1,000 / 82.5 ≈ **12** at €150, and 1,000 / 60 ≈ **17** at €120.
  - Just to cover the cost: 1,000 / 120 ≈ **8** at €150, and 1,000 / 90 ≈ **11** at €120.
- Tax arrears: €9,000 / 12 = **€750/month = 30 % of current profit**, the same as 3.6 months of all current profit. It is an obligation, not a goal.
- Reserve/cash: **not given**.
