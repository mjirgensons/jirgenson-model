## Step 1. Recomputed key numbers

- Season month: 14,000 − 4,200 (30% parts) − 6,500 = **+3,300**.
- Winter month: 4,000 − 1,200 − 6,500 = **−3,700**. Winter outgoings per month = 6,500 + 1,200 = 7,700.
- Year: 7 × 3,300 − 5 × 3,700 = 23,100 − 18,500 = **+4,600**. Annual revenue 98,000 + 20,000 = 118,000, so the margin is 3.9%.
- Cash path (October counted as a season month): end Oct 12,300; Nov 8,600; Dec 4,900; Jan 1,200; Feb −2,500; Mar −6,200; Apr −2,900; May +400. Zero is reached about 1,200 / 3,700 ≈ 0.32 of a month into February, so **around 10 February**. The gap to reach April is 18,500 − 12,300 = **6,200**.
- E-bikes: 12,000 is more than the 9,000 held today. Paid after October they leave about 300, and cash hits zero in November. Paid in March: −6,200 − 12,000 = −18,200.
- Checks on other figures in the responses:
  - Payback in one season at 70%: 12,000 / 0.7 ≈ 17,100 (1,714 per bike).
  - Payback in two seasons: 6,000 / 0.7 ≈ 8,571 ≈ 8,600 per season.
  - Mechanic cut by a third for 5 months: 1,167 × 5 ≈ 5,800. Rent 500 × 5 = 2,500. Extra 1,000 of winter revenue × 0.7 × 5 = 3,500.
  - 9,000 / 6,500 ≈ 1.4 months. 12,000 / 4,600 ≈ 2.6 years.
  - A ±10% error in October takings moves cash by ±980 (after parts); in winter takings by ±1,400. Together that is ±2,380, so the gap ranges from about 3,800 to 8,600.
  - Loan of 12,000 over 3 years at 8%: about 376 a month. Five winter payments (≈1,875) take the gap to about 8,100.
  - Rent deferral 1,000 × 5 = 5,000 plus pay cut 750 × 5 = 3,750 gives 8,750, which is 2,550 above the 6,200 gap. The ±2,380 range gives about 200 to 4,900.
  - Plan path: monthly loss −3,700 + 1,000 + 750 = −1,950. End Nov 10,350; Dec 8,400; Jan 6,450, so it dips under 6,500 at the end of January.
  - Without the pay cut, keeping the same cushion needs 3,750 / 5 / 0.7 ≈ 1,070, about 1,100 a month of extra takings.
