# Judgment — T04, C-v1.0-r5 (Response 1) vs B-r2 (Response 2)

## Step 1. Recomputed key numbers

- Average monthly income: (6,100 + 6,900 + 6,500) / 3 = 19,500 / 3 = **€6,500**. Range ±€400 around the mean.
- Biggest client 55 % = **€3,575**; second client 15 % = **€975**; all others 30 % = **€1,950**.
- After the second client leaves: 6,500 − 975 = **€5,525**.
- Biggest client's share after the loss: 3,575 / 5,525 = 0.647 ≈ **65 %**.
- Gap to target: 10,000 − 5,525 = **€4,475**, i.e. 4,475 / 5,525 ≈ **+81 %** in 12 months (≈ €373 of added monthly income per month if linear).
- At €10,000 with the biggest client unchanged: 3,575 / 10,000 ≈ **36 %**. At €8,500 → 42 %; at €10,500 → 34 %.
- If the biggest client also left: ≈ **€1,950/month** remain.
- Not given: reserve/cash, monthly costs (including the employee), contract terms, pipeline, stop date of the second client.
- Budget: round 1 only, preliminary reading + ≤5 questions ranked by effect, ≤500 words, no full brief.

## Step 2. Mandatory elements

### Response 1
- **M1 = 1.** Opens with "What your numbers show": €6,500 average, €5,525 after the loss, "becomes about 65%". All correct.
- **M2 = 1.** "The biggest risk is the client at 55%, not the one leaving… Your second client's reason (an in-house AI tool) could apply to the biggest one as well." A numeric line is stated later ("aim for under 40% by month 12"). That line works more as a month-12 target than as a red line already crossed, so this is a borderline 1.
- **M3 = 1.** Safe actions are present: ask the leaving client for the stop date in writing, and have a normal check-in with the biggest client about plans and a fixed-volume agreement. They are buried in a full action plan rather than given as the single safe step for round 1.
- **M4 = 0.** There are four numbered items but six question marks, which is over the limit. The ranking is only partial ("Questions 1 and 3 could change the plan most", but Q3 sits third). And the questions come at the very end, after a full plan, so the response has already said far more than a round 1 should before it asks.
- **M5 = 0.** It never asks for the refusal order (income level, independence, hours, employee). "The order is your call" is about the order of actions, not about what to give up first or last.
- **M6 = 0.** Q1 asks for monthly costs and take-home pay, but nothing asks about reserve or cash in the account. The element needs both.
- **M7 = 1 (borderline).** The forecast labels "the market stays as it is" as an assumption, and the low end assumes "you lose another 10% of small clients to AI". "Pick… work where clients still pay for a human" points the same way. That is a labelled hypothesis that AI is eroding demand beyond these two clients, and the business is not declared doomed. It is not posed as a question to check, though.
- **M8 = 0.** About 680 words against a 500 limit, and it is in effect a full brief: diagnosis, risk, today/week/month plan, two forecasts, monitoring, review dates and questions.

## Step 3. Forbidden items and invented facts

### Response 1
- **F1 present.** It delivers the full plan before any answers: actions for today, this week and this month, a two-scenario forecast table, monthly and weekly metrics, review dates, and a rethink trigger.
- **F2 present.** Six question marks addressed to the person (Q2 and Q3 each have two), which is more than 5 by the counting rule used for H2. Borderline, because there are only four numbered items.
- F3 absent ("The order is your call"). F4 absent.
- **Invented facts:**
  - "Former clients are the cheapest to win back": a general claim stated as fact.
  - That clients still pay for a human in "legal or certified, medical, marketing, or rare language pairs": a market claim given as examples without a label.

  The forecast inputs (€500–1,000 per new client, 10% further AI loss, 1–2 months to stop) are labelled assumptions and do not count.

### Response 2
- No forbidden items. F1: only a preliminary reading and one safe step. F2: exactly 5. F3: the order is proposed "from your words" and open to change. F4: "I cannot promise that figure"; the cash cushion is treated as "unknown, not as safe".
- **Invented facts:**
  - "post-editing is a paid service of its own": an industry claim not in the task.
  - "You heard it early": no stop date is known, so this is unsupported.

## Step 4. Criteria

### Response 1
- **C1 = 7.** Every computed number is correct (3,575 / 975 / 1,950 / 5,525 / 65 % / 4,475 / 36 % / 34–42 %) and most assumptions are labelled. Against that: two unlabelled market claims, and a "plan works" range (€8,500–10,500) that does not follow from the stated "2–3 new clients at €500–1,000", which only gives about €6,500–8,500.
- **C2 = 7.** It finds the central issue: concentration, not the lost client, is the risk; AI could reach the biggest client; €1,950 probably does not cover two people; the two goals are really one. It never brings up reserve or cash runway, so it misses the nearest irreversible damage.
- **C3 = 7.** The steps are concrete, ordered today/week/month, and doable by the owner. Some are results rather than actions ("2–3 new regular clients"), and most are premature before the costs and stop date are known.
- **C4 = 3.** Several breaches. About 680 words against 500 (≈36 % over). A full plan and forecasts in a round where the person asked for questions in rounds. Six question marks against a limit of 5.
- **C5 = 7.** Good controls: share target under 40 % checked against the forecast (34–42 %), a 20 % volume-drop warning, monthly and weekly metrics, review at month-end and month 3 with a €6,000 trigger, and the action order left to the person. The refusal order is never asked, and nothing says what to leave untouched.
- **C6 = 8.** Two labelled forecasts with ranges, and "€10,000 is the top of the range, not the middle". No promises. The weakness is a 12-month forecast given before costs or cash are known.

### Response 2
- **C1 = 8.** Arithmetic is correct (5,500 ≈ 5,525; 65 %; +4,500 ≈ 80 %; €375/month; 36 %). Assumptions are labelled ("I am assuming… invoiced amounts… shares of the three-month average"). The rounded components (3,600 + 1,000 + 1,950) sum to 6,550, which is trivial. Two small unlabelled claims.
- **C2 = 8.** It names the danger (the biggest client leaving for the same AI reason), what that would hit (€1,950 against unseen costs and the employee), and that the cash cushion is unknown. It also sees that independence is the harder goal. It does not check the wider market environment.
- **C3 = 8.** One safe, reversible step with a time ("this week") that the owner does alone. Holding off on long contracts and hiring is explicit. That is the right amount for round 1.
- **C4 = 6.** It follows the round format: preliminary reading, danger, one safe step, 5 questions, then stops. The language is plain and it is in English. One breach: about 570 words against 500.
- **C5 = 8.** Two red lines are proposed with ownership ("mine, change them") and tested (55 % already over 50 %; 36 % at €10k against a one-third target). The refusal order is proposed and handed back. It says what to leave untouched (growth spending, hires, long commitments). No measurement frequency or review date yet, which is acceptable in round 1.
- **C6 = 8.** "I cannot promise that figure." It gives a range for current income, treats the cash as unknown rather than safe, and defers the forecast until the answers arrive. No guarantees.
