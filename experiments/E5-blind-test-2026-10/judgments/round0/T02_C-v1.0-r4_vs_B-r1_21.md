# Judgment — T02 (Food truck, 72-hour permit clock, EMERGENCY, EN)

## Step 1. Recomputed key numbers

- Today (day 0): €1,900 − €1,000 deposit = **€900**.
- Day 2 (repair day, the day after tomorrow): €900 − €1,400 balance (if due on completion) = **−€500**.
- Day 4: −€500 − €1,200 wages = **−€1,700**.
- Day 5: −€1,700 + €1,500 wedding deposit = **−€200**.
- Totals check: outflows €1,000 + €1,400 + €1,200 = €3,600; cash on hand plus inflow €1,900 + €1,500 = €3,400; net **−€200**.
- If the wedding client pays the €1,500 by day 2: €900 + €1,500 − €1,400 = €1,000 on day 2; €1,000 − €1,200 = −€200 on day 4.
- If nothing is done (no deposit, no repair): €1,900 → €700 after wages on day 4 → €2,200 on day 5 on paper. But the permit is suspended around day 2 and the €600 of stock is likely lost.
- Deadline: the inspection was yesterday (day −1), so 72 hours ends on day 2, the same day as the earliest repair slot. There is no slack.
- Wage split proposed in Response 2 (€900 on day 4, €300 on day 5): this works only if the repairer defers the €1,400. Day 4: €900 − €900 = 0. Day 5: 0 + €1,500 − €300 − €1,400 = −€200. The arithmetic is consistent.
- Stock at risk: about €600. Food held at 9°C may be unsafe.

## Step 4. Criteria

### Response 1
- **C1 = 8.** All the arithmetic is correct, and the assumptions are explicitly labelled (balance due on completion, wedding money on time, fuel and restocking excluded). "About €200 closes it" is slightly loose, given that the day-4 position is −€1,700.
- **C2 = 8.** It sees that "the timing is worse than the total", names the permit with no slack on day 2, and gives the full cash path, plus a no-change forecast showing stock loss and risk to the wedding job. However, the danger to people from food at 9°C is not treated as a first-order risk.
- **C3 = 8.** The steps are concrete, numbered and doable by the person: call the inspector with specific questions, pay the deposit, move the stock, ask the client and the repairer. They are layered as today, then by day this week, then this month. Food safety is ordered third.
- **C4 = 3.** Several breaches of the emergency mode:
  - Line one is analysis, not an action.
  - About 586 words against a 300-word budget.
  - A seven-section brief.
  - The answer to "what do I do today" is buried.
- **C5 = 6.** It has a good watch schedule with times (tomorrow morning, day 2 evening, day 4 morning, day 5) and a day-4 check of the balance against €1,200. It does not propose a priority order ("your call" without a proposal), sets no red line with a reason, and says nothing about what to leave untouched.
- **C6 = 9.** Two quantified forecasts with conditions, assumptions stated, unknown daily takings admitted, and a fallback with a range (€500–€1,700). No promises.

### Response 2
- **C1 = 7.** The numbers are correct and the timing of the balance is hedged ("probably"). There is one unlabelled overstatement ("only way to meet the 72 hours") and the loose "receive €3,400" wording.
- **C2 = 7.** It identifies the zero-slack repair day, the €900-versus-€1,400 timing problem, the residual −€200, the extra cost of wedding ingredients, and the permit as what enables all income. It does not quantify the −€1,700 low point before day 5.
- **C3 = 8.** The action comes first and every gap-closing step names who to contact today (inspector, repairer, wedding client, part-timers). The wage split has concrete figures (€900 and €300). There is little beyond today, which is acceptable in emergency mode.
- **C4 = 8.** The action is on line one, the reply is within the word budget, and the language is plain. The minor issue is that the second question bundles two sub-questions.
- **C5 = 7.** The priority order is proposed and explicitly left to the person, a red line is proposed with a reason and tested ("you are already below it"), there is a not-today list, and the next review has a time. It does not say what to measure at that review or how often.
- **C6 = 7.** There are two forecasts (nothing changes / plan works) with no guarantees and a "probably" hedge. The forecasts are terse and unquantified beyond −€200, and "only way" is slightly overconfident.
