## Step 1. Recomputed key numbers

- Account today: €1,900. After the €1,000 deposit: 1,900 − 1,000 = **€900**.
- Repair balance: 2,400 − 1,000 = €1,400, due on the repair day (day 2): 900 − 1,400 = **−€500**.
- Wages €1,200 on day 4: −500 − 1,200 = **−€1,700**.
- Wedding deposit +€1,500 on day 5: −1,700 + 1,500 = **−€200**.
- Same thing as totals: out before day 5 = 1,000 + 1,400 + 1,200 = €3,600 against €1,900 (gap €1,700). With the wedding money, in = €3,400 against €3,600 out (gap €200).
- Clocks: 72 h counted from yesterday ends the day after tomorrow, the same day as the earliest repair slot. Permit suspension is the nearest damage that cannot be undone. Wages are the second clock (day 4).
- Food safety: food held at 9°C may be unsafe. About €600 of stock is at risk.
- Alternative path if the €1,400 balance is moved to after day 5: 900 − 1,200 = −€300 on day 4, +1,500 = €1,200 on day 5, − 1,400 = −€200. The end result is the same.

## Step 4. Criteria

Moving dates cannot close the €200 structural gap, and the permit is named as the costliest loss. It does not spell out that the day-2 balance (−€500) itself puts the repair, and so the permit, at risk.
- C3 = 7. Concrete steps the person can do today, with who to call and exactly what to ask: card for the deposit, inspector questions in writing, staff agreement by text, an early-payment script for the client, a €1,000 backup. Food safety comes third, after two phone calls, and no step has a time attached.
- C4 = 3. Line one is right and the language is plain, but at about 662 words it more than doubles the emergency budget. A person in a crisis gets a long brief.
- C5 = 3. No priority order is offered for the person to choose, no red line is proposed or tested, there is no next review time, and there is no "leave untouched" list. Only the best-case cash path counts towards control.
- C6 = 6. Honest about the remaining €200 gap and the risk of suspension even with the repair, and it promises nothing. It gives only one (best-case) forecast and no "if nothing changes" path. It also suggests telling the client the repair is "an equipment upgrade", a mildly misleading pretext.

**Response 2**
- C1 = 6. €900, −€500, and −€200 are correct, and the 72-hour assumption is labelled. The "€300 short" figure silently assumes the €1,400 balance is deferred (an unlabelled assumption that understates the gap before day 5), and the two-hour discard rule comes from outside the task.
- C2 = 7. It names suspension the day after tomorrow as the greatest danger, sees that the slot falls on the deadline day, and sees that the balance due on repair day leaves the account €500 short and the permit at risk. It does not show the full ≈€1,700 hole before day 5.

Steps are in order and doable: deposit and written confirmation today, send to the inspector, cool boxes and discard, split the balance on the repairer call, ask the client tomorrow, check at 12:00. Missing: no step for talking to the two staff, no fallback if the repairer or client says no, and the client ask could have been today.
