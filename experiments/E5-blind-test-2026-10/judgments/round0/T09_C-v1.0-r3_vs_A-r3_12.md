# Judgment — T09 (Newspaper kiosk in a small town), Response 1 vs Response 2

## Step 1. Recomputed key numbers

- 2023 → 2024: (42,000 − 48,000) / 48,000 = **−12.5 %**.
- 2024 → 2025: (37,000 − 42,000) / 42,000 = **−11.9 %**.
- 2026 run-rate: 25,000 / 9 × 12 = **33,333 €**, which is **−9.9 %** against 37,000. So the decline is about 10–12 % a year for three periods in a row.
- 2023 → 2026: 48,000 → 33,333 = **−30.6 %** over three years. Population 8,000 → 6,000 = **−25 %** over five years. Revenue is therefore falling about twice as fast per year as the population (≈ −10 %/yr vs ≈ −5.6 %/yr).
- +30 % on 33,333 = **43,333 €**, which is **≈ +10,000 €** and slightly above the 2024 level (42,000). Hitting it means reversing about three years of decline in a town that keeps shrinking.
- Owner's hourly return: 6,000 / (55 × 48 = 2,640 h) = **€2.27/h**. Over 52 weeks (2,860 h) it is €2.10/h. So roughly €2 per hour.
- Implied total costs in 2025: 37,000 − 6,000 = 31,000 €/yr ≈ 2,583 €/month. This figure includes the goods bought for resale.
- "Nothing changes" for 2027: 33,333 × 0.88–0.90 ≈ **29,300–30,000 €**.
- The lease ends in 8 months from about October 2026, so **about June 2027**. The cost of a wrong exit is low: €4,000 reserve and no stated long-term liabilities.
- Calendar check: 16 Oct 2026 is a Friday, so 2 Nov 2026 is a **Monday**.

## Step 2. Mandatory elements

### Response 1
- **M1 = 1.** The reply is in Latvian, uses the polite plural "you" form and everyday words throughout ("red lines", "alarm", "empty hours"). It uses no "vector/parameter/zone/manoeuvre".
- **M2 = 1.** "this year about €33,000 (€25,000 in nine months …)" and "a decline of 10–12% a year". The assumption about the last three months is labelled, with a Christmas upside to 35,000.
- **M3 = 1.** "The town lost a quarter of its residents and its biggest employer, turnover is falling for the third year, and advertising here goes against the current." The environment is named as the dominant force, and the response says marketing will not reverse it.
- **M4 = 1.** It opens with "+30% (about €43,000) … is not a realistic target". It shows what +30 % would require (≈ 10,000 € more revenue) and what it would earn: 2,000–2,500 € at a labelled 20–25 c per euro, or 4,000 € at 40 c, "the conclusion is the same". It offers no generic marketing plan.
- **M5 = 1.** The lease end is dated ("around June 2027") and called "The biggest danger". What it would hit is named: a long renewal or a higher rent lands on the €4,000. The options are listed: extend on better terms, relocate, or a calm wind-down, decided by end of February. A cheap test comes before renewal (≤ €300, 3 h/week). The owner's time is valued at about €2/h. The reserve is the last thing to give up. One gap: it never says outright that the cost of a wrong exit is low. It conveys this only through the asymmetry (the danger is a long lease).
- **M6 = 1.** Two forecasts with numbers and conditions:
  - Nothing changes: 29,000–30,000 €, profit 4,000–4,600 €.
  - Plan works (decline stops, empty hours closed, new service): 32,000–34,000 €, profit 4,800–5,400 €.
  The profit ranges follow from its labelled 20–25 % assumption. They are higher than the key's "near zero", but they are correctly derived, and they still mean about €1.5–2/h.
- **M7 = 1.** It gives measurable conditions: reserve below €2,600, and revenue more than 10 % below the same month last year for three months running. A breach brings the decision forward, and the tests are compared with the same weeks last year. It asks exactly 3 questions: rent and the landlord's term, the category split and margins, and whether the kiosk is the only income. It handles "what would you give up last" by proposing an order and asking the owner to correct it.
- **M8 = 1.** About 580 whitespace-separated tokens. That count includes headings, split thousands ("48 000" = 2), € and % signs and list markers. The response is under 600.

### Response 2
- **M1 = 1.** The reply is in Latvian, in the informal singular "you" form, with everyday vocabulary. "Margin" is explained in plain words, and none of the banned words appear.
- **M2 = 1.** "by ~10-12% each year" and "about €33-35 thousand for the whole of 2026".
- **M3 = 1.** "This is not a problem of too little advertising but a shrinking customer base, and advertising does not bring it back." The environment is named as the cause, and the response says marketing will not reverse it. The supporting rate comparison is wrong (see C1), but the substance of the element is present.
- **M4 = 1.** "+30% … about €43-44 thousand, so back to the 2024 turnover, while the town has a quarter fewer people. You would have to turn −10% into +30%." This shows clearly what +30 % requires, and the response gives no generic plan as its main answer.
- **M5 = 1.** It covers most of the element:
  - "The lease term is your real clock."
  - "how to stop it in time and without losses" (an exit without losses).
  - Tests capped at 300–500 €, with the rest of the €4,000 kept as a cushion.
  - Don't renew, or renew only at a lower rent and a shorter term.
  - The owner's time is valued at about €2/h.
  The lease end is not dated, and the response does not say outright that the cost of a wrong exit is low.
- **M6 = 0.** Only one real forecast ("2027 will be about €30 thousand", with no profit). The "plan works" side is a goal ("A more realistic target … +5-10%"), not a forecast with conditions and numbers, and it gives no profit figure.
- **M7 = 0.** The stop or continue criterion has no number. "if … do not reach a specific level" leaves the owner to set the level, and "Whatever doesn't work after 6-8 weeks, drop it" has no measurable bar. It ends with four information requests (sales split, margin, rent and landlord, whether to continue), which is more than the ≤3 budget in spirit. None of them asks what has been tried or what is growing in the town.
- **M8 = 1.** About 523 whitespace-separated tokens (same counting method), so under 600.

## Step 3. Forbidden items and invented facts

### Response 1
- **Forbidden items:** none.
  - F1: no generic plan. The only marketing reference is "do not spend the savings on big advertising".
  - F3: it explicitly says "do not wind down the kiosk in a hurry: data are still missing" and tests niches first.
  - F4: the priority order is labelled "it is mine, not yours" and the owner is invited to change it.
- **Invented facts:** none.
  - The 31,000 € costs and the "1.5 months" reserve are derived from the task's numbers. The framing is slightly odd, because the costs include goods that sales pay for, but it is not invented.
  - The 20–25 c margin and the "last 3 months similar" assumption are labelled.

### Response 2
- **Forbidden items:** none.
  - F1: Facebook and a sign appear only as one of four low-cost test ideas, not as the answer.
  - F3: it asks whether the owner is open to ending and tests niches first.
  - F4: it proposes a "realistic goal" but asks whether the owner wants to continue.
- **Invented facts / unsupported claims:**
  1. "That is close to minimum-wage level for a very long working week." This is an external benchmark that is not in the task. It is also inaccurate: even 8,500 €/yr over about 2,640 h is about €3.2/h, well below a minimum hourly wage.
  2. "older customers, who are your most loyal." This is a claim about the customer base that is not in the task.
  3. "about €2 an hour before taxes." The task says profit is before the owner's pay, not "before taxes", so the response relabels the figure. This is minor.

  It also contains an analytical error: "Turnover is falling at roughly the same pace as the number of residents (about −30% in three years versus −25% of residents in five)". These are not the same pace; revenue is falling about twice as fast per year.

## Step 4. Criteria

### Response 1
- **C1 = 9.** Every number checks out: trend, run-rate, +30 % ≈ 43,000 (+10,000), €2/h, June 2027, Monday 2 Nov, and forecasts consistent with the stated margin. Assumptions are labelled. The only weak point is the "reserve = 1.5 months of total costs" framing.
- **C2 = 9.** It names the shrinking town as the driving force and the +30 % target as unrealistic. It names the nearest irreversible damage with its date and target: a long or more expensive lease renewal in about June 2027 hitting the €4,000. It stops short of stating that a wrong exit is cheap.
- **C3 = 9.** The steps are dated and in order:
  - This week: free hourly sales log.
  - End of November: lease talks without signing, with specific asks.
  - End of December: one capped test at a time.
  - End of February: decision.
  Fast and reversible steps come first, the contract caveats are noted, and the outcome of the talks with the landlord is kept explicitly open ("without signing anything").
- **C4 = 9.** It answers the real question in Latvian with everyday words, polite form, 3 questions, and stays within the word budget, though close to the cap and fairly dense.
- **C5 = 9.** It has all the control parts:
  - An explicit priority order, labelled as the adviser's, with the choice left to the owner.
  - Two red lines with reasons, tested against the forecast. One is honestly reported as already sounding, which is why growth spending waits.
  - A weekly measure and a first review on 2 Nov.
  - A "what not to do now" list.
  The basis of the first red line (total costs as burn) is a little artificial.
- **C6 = 9.** It makes no guarantees ("There are no guarantees; one good month does not yet prove anything"). It gives two conditional ranges with profit, and the test spending is capped.

### Response 2
- **C1 = 6.** The core arithmetic is right: trend, 33–35k, 43–44k, €2/h, and 2,500 € at a labelled 25 % margin. But it contains the wrong "same pace as population" comparison, an unsupported minimum-wage benchmark, an unsupported claim that older customers are the most loyal, and the "before taxes" relabelling. None of these changes the advice.
- **C2 = 7.** The diagnosis is strong: a shrinking customer base, "+30 % = back to 2024 with a quarter fewer people", and the lease as the real clock. It does not date the decision point or name exactly what a wrong renewal would hit.
- **C3 = 7.** The steps are sensible (data in 2–3 weeks, tests in 3–4 months, cut hours, lease decision "by month 5"). The timing is vague: "5th month" of what? Lease talks have no date. The idea list is broad (services, delivery, assortment, Facebook) rather than one test at a time.
- **C4 = 7.** Latvian, everyday words, comfortably within the word budget. It ends with four information requests instead of at most three, and a small generic-marketing item creeps in.
- **C5 = 4.** It has a spend ceiling (300–500 €) and keeps the reserve. It has no numeric threshold, no stated measurement frequency, no review date, and does not propose an order of priorities. The owner is told to write their own criterion.
- **C6 = 6.** It is hedged and makes no promises, and the margin assumption is labelled. But there is only one forecast (30k, without profit), and the second scenario is a goal, not a conditional forecast.
