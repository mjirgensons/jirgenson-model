# Judgment — T09 (Newspaper kiosk in a small town), Response 1 vs Response 2

## Step 1 — Recomputed key numbers

- 2023→2024: 48,000 → 42,000 = −6,000 / 48,000 = **−12.5 %**.
- 2024→2025: 42,000 → 37,000 = −5,000 / 42,000 = **−11.9 %**.
- 2026 run-rate: 25,000 / 9 × 12 = **33,333** → vs 37,000 = **−9.9 %**. Three periods of decline in a row.
- Population: 8,000 → 6,000 = **−25 %** in five years; the largest factory closed in 2023, so it was **not** running in 2024.
- +30 % on 33,333 = **43,333** (more than the 2024 level of 42,000). On a "nothing changes" path of about 30,000 in 2027 (33,333 × 0.9), that is about 44 % above trend.
- 2025 expenses implied: 37,000 − 6,000 = **31,000** (≈ 2,583 / month). The €4,000 reserve ≈ 1.5 months of expenses.
- Owner pay: 6,000 / (55 × 48 = 2,640 h) ≈ **€2.27/h**; with 55 × 52 = 2,860 h ≈ **€2.10/h** (≈ €500/month). Both are acceptable.
- 2026 profit: about 2,333 if all 31,000 of expenses are fixed; about 5,400 if expenses fall in proportion to sales (16.2 % × 33,333).
- 2027 if nothing changes: about 29,000–30,700 in turnover. Profit is near zero or negative if costs are mostly fixed.
- Lease ends in 8 months (≈ June 2027). That is the decision point. A wrong exit costs little: the reserve is €4,000, no long-term liabilities are stated, and current pay is about €2/h.

## Step 2 — Mandatory elements

### Response 1
- **M1 = 1.** Latvian, informal "you", everyday words. None of the banned words. "Trajectory" and "margin" are a little technical but acceptable.
- **M2 = 1.** "falling by about 12% a year", "about €33-35 thousand for the whole of 2026". The upper bound of 35k is tied to December, which is reasonable.
- **M3 = 1.** "The decline matches what happened to the town… Marketing… cannot create buyers in a town that is emptying out."
- **M4 = 1.** It does not deliver the +30 % plan as the main answer. It shows +30 % ≈ 44k, "about 40% above the trajectory", "almost the 2023 level" with a quarter fewer residents. It also adds the strong point that even a +30 % success would give only about €3/h.
- **M5 = 1.** The 8 months without commitments are framed as the window to decide ("decide with the numbers in hand", "If it cannot, leave without losses"). It gives options A (continue on new lease terms), B (convert to a service point if tests justify it) and C (wind down by lease end, keep the reserve, take a job). The owner's time is valued (≈ €2/h, €500/month). The low cost of exit is shown in substance: a minimum-wage job pays more than the kiosk.
- **M6 = 0.** "Nothing changes" gives turnover only (≈ 31k in 2027) and no profit. There is no "plan works" forecast for the proposed path (service point or tests). The only upside figure is the hypothetical +30 % case.
- **M7 = 0.** It has measurable conditions: a revenue target and deadline before each test ("If it doesn't, stop"), a minimum-wage-level pay goal, and a February decision. But it asks **4** questions, which breaks the ≤3 budget. The questions are also mostly data requests, not the decisive ones (what has been tried, what is growing in town, what the owner would give up last).
- **M8 = 0.** About 650 whitespace-separated words (excluding markdown markers). Even after removing every €, %, arrow and list numeral it is still about 605–610. Over 600.

**Total: 5/8.**

### Response 2
- **M1 = 1.** Latvian, formal "you", plain words ("red line" is a common everyday phrase). None of the banned words.
- **M2 = 1.** "falling by 10–12% a year", "this year about €33,000", with a ±10 % sensitivity note.
- **M3 = 1.** "the shrinking of the town is the strongest factor, and advertising will not stop it."
- **M4 = 1.** It does not deliver a marketing plan. +30 % ≈ 43,000, "more than in 2024", so it means reversing the decline (but see the factual slip in Step 3). Advertising is capped at €300 and 3 h/week.
- **M5 = 0.** The lease end (~June 2027) is clearly named as the decision point and the biggest threat ("do not sign a new long-term lease before March"). Time is valued at €2.10/h. But the response never says that a wrong exit costs little. Exit ("closing") appears only as one item in a fallback list. It does not lay out the orderly-exit-with-reserve-intact option against the alternatives. One core part of the element is missing.
- **M6 = 1.** "Nothing changes": 2027 turnover 29,000–30,500, loss of 500–2,000, reserve falls to about 2,000. "Plan works": 31,000–34,000, conditions stated, "an estimate, not a promise".
- **M7 = 1.** Measurable continue/stop conditions before the lease decision on 31 March: "more than 10% below last year's or the savings are below €2,600". Exactly 3 questions. The question about the owner's priorities is handled by a proposed order the owner can change.
- **M8 = 1.** About 578 whitespace-separated words, within the budget.

**Total: 7/8.**

## Step 3 — Forbidden items and invented facts

### Response 1
- **Forbidden:** none.
  - F1: a visibility bullet (Facebook post, sign, leaflets) appears as one of several cheap tests, not as the answer.
  - F3: it does not declare the kiosk dead and it tests niches.
  - F4: borderline but not counted. "So I would change the goal" imposes a minimum-wage-level pay goal without asking the owner's priorities. The final A/B/C choice is still left to the owner.
- **Invented facts:**
  - "the main product, newspapers, is shrinking across the whole country" (national press decline, not in the task).
  - "the minimum wage is about €4.5 an hour gross" and "Full-time work at the minimum wage gives about €780 a month gross". Both are external figures presented as fact, plausibly accurate for Latvia but not in the task, and they drive option C.
  - "The commissions are small" (parcel and bill-payment commissions) is an external claim.
  - "the landlord doesn't have many alternative tenants" is an inference stated as fact.
- **Minor numeric issues:**
  - "almost the 2023 level" is loose (44k vs 48k).
  - The "+10k → ~€3/h" calculation adds the extra margin to the 2025 profit base while measuring the +10k from 2026 turnover. It overstates slightly, but the conclusion does not change.

### Response 2
- **Forbidden:** none.
- **Invented or incorrect facts:**
  - "more than in 2024, when the factory was still working" contradicts the task, because the factory closed in 2023. It does not change the advice.
  - "considerably below the minimum wage" uses an external benchmark, with no number given.
- **Modelling note:**
  - The 2027 "nothing changes" loss assumes all €31,000 of expenses are fixed. It is only partly labelled ("with costs of about €31,000", "in the worst case"), which is inconsistent with the response's own 2026 range of 2,000–5,400. Question 2 does ask exactly this, which mitigates it.

## Step 4 — Criteria

### Response 1
- **C1 = 5.** The core arithmetic is right and the gross-margin assumption is clearly labelled. However, several external figures and claims are stated as fact (minimum wage €4.5/h and €780, national press decline, small commissions), and there are two small imprecisions.
- **C2 = 8.** Strong diagnosis: the decline tracks the town, marketing cannot fix it, and even a +30 % success leaves about €3/h. The lease window is used well. It does not name the most dangerous event (renewing on current terms while savings erode) together with what it would hit.
- **C3 = 7.** Phased steps the owner can do (Oct–Nov data, Dec–Feb tests with kill criteria, Feb decision, negotiate by March). Timing is by month blocks, not dates, and the test list is a menu.
- **C4 = 5.** It answers the real question in good everyday Latvian. But it breaks both budgets: about 650 words against ≤600, and 4 questions against ≤3.
- **C5 = 5.** There are per-test revenue thresholds, a €300–500 experiment cap and a February decision. Priorities are not made explicit, and the new goal is set for the owner rather than offered. There is no savings red line tested against a forecast and no review cadence.
- **C6 = 6.** No promises and the assumptions are labelled. It is honest about +30 %. But the "nothing changes" forecast has no profit figure and there is no "plan works" forecast for the proposed path.

### Response 2
- **C1 = 6.** Thorough and correct arithmetic (expenses 31k, €2.10/h, 1.5 months of reserve, 2026 profit range, 2027 range, red line). One factual slip about the factory in 2024, and a partly labelled all-fixed-cost assumption in the 2027 forecast.
- **C2 = 8.** It names the town shrinkage as the strongest force. It names the most dangerous event with its date: a multi-year lease at the current price around June 2027, hitting profit and the reserve. It also targets the right diagnostic (fewer customers or smaller baskets). It lacks R1's sharp "even +30 % doesn't pay" point.
- **C3 = 8.** Concrete, dated, owner-marked steps ("you"): this week, by 31 Oct, by 15 Nov, by 1 Feb. Cheap and reversible actions come first, and there is a cap on advertising money and time.
- **C4 = 8.** Within the word budget, exactly 3 questions, in Latvian, honestly reframing the request. The text is dense with numbers, but acceptable.
- **C5 = 9.** A priority order is proposed with an explicit invitation to change it. The red line (€2,600, one month of expenses) has a reason and is tested against the forecast. It states what to measure and how often (receipts, monthly YoY, weekly savings), gives a first review on 2 Nov and a decision by 31 Mar, and lists what not to do now.
- **C6 = 8.** "I cannot promise that", "an estimate, not a promise". Two forecasts with ranges and conditions, plus a ±10 % sensitivity note. The "nothing changes" path leans on the worst-case cost assumption.

## Step 6 — Preference

**Response 2, confidence 0.75.**

Response 2 stays within the word and question budgets. It gives dated steps the owner can do, a savings red line tested against a "nothing changes" forecast, two numeric forecasts, and a priority order the owner can change.

Response 1 has a sharper insight (even a +30 % success leaves about €3/h) and a clearer exit option. But it goes over both budgets, has no forecasts for profit or for the proposed plan, imposes a new goal instead of offering priorities, and relies on several unlabelled external figures.
