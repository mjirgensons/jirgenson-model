# Judgment — T09 (Newspaper kiosk in a small town), Response 1 vs Response 2

## Step 1. Recomputed key numbers

- 2023 → 2024: 48,000 → 42,000 = −6,000 / 48,000 = **−12.5 %**.
- 2024 → 2025: 42,000 → 37,000 = −5,000 / 42,000 = **−11.9 %**.
- 2026 run-rate: 25,000 / 9 × 12 = **≈ 33,333** → vs 37,000 = **−9.9 %**. If Q4 is stronger, up to ~35,000 (a labelled assumption).
- Cumulative 2023 → 2026: 48,000 → ~33,300 = **≈ −31 % in three years**; population 8,000 → 6,000 = **−25 % in five years**. Revenue is falling faster per year than population (roughly −10…−12 %/yr vs ≈ −5.6 %/yr).
- +30 % on 33,333 = **≈ 43,300** (≈ +10,000), i.e. back above the 2024 level (42,000) with a quarter fewer residents.
- Extra profit from +10,000 revenue at an assumed 20–25 % margin = **2,000–2,500 €/yr** (at 40 % = 4,000).
- Owner's pay: 6,000 / (55 h × 48–52 weeks = 2,640–2,860 h) = **≈ €2.1–2.3 per hour**.
- 2025 total costs: 37,000 − 6,000 = 31,000 €/yr ≈ 2,583 €/month; reserve 4,000 € ≈ 1.5 months of total costs.
- If the trend continues: 2027 ≈ 33,300 × 0.9 ≈ **30,000 €**.
- Lease ends in 8 months from ~October 2026 → **≈ June 2027**; a wrong exit is cheap (4,000 € reserve, no stated long-term liabilities); the costly, hard-to-reverse mistake is a long renewal on current terms.
- 2 November 2026 is a Monday (checked: 1 Jan 2026 is a Thursday).

## Step 2. Mandatory elements

### Response 1
- **M1 = 1.** Latvian throughout, informal "you", everyday words; none of "vector/parameter/zone/manoeuvre"; "margin" is explained in plain words.
- **M2 = 1.** "by ~10-12% each year"; "€25,000 ... means about €33-35 thousand for the whole of 2026".
- **M3 = 1.** "This is not a problem of too little advertising but a shrinking customer base, and advertising does not bring it back" — names the shrinking customer base as the dominant force and says advertising will not reverse it.
- **M4 = 1.** Main answer is not a marketing plan; shows +30 % = 43–44k, "back to the 2024 turnover, while the town has a quarter fewer people. You would have to turn −10% into +30%."
- **M5 = 1 (with a gap).** Lease is "your real clock"; options: don't renew / renew only on lower rent and shorter term / cheap tests with 300–500 € while keeping the reserve; time valued at "about €2 an hour". The low cost of a wrong exit is only implicit ("how to stop it in time and without losses"), not stated as a weighing of costs.
- **M6 = 1 (weak).** Nothing changes: "2027 will be about €30 thousand"; plan works: "add +5-10% with the new services" framed as a realistic target. No profit figures in either scenario.
- **M7 = 0.** The stop/continue criterion is left blank ("if ... do not reach a specific level"), and the "6-8 weeks" test cutoff has no threshold; it ends with four numbered information requests (sales mix, margin, rent/landlord, whether to continue), which exceeds the ≤3 budget in substance even without question marks.
- **M8 = 1.** ≈ 519 words.

### Response 2
- **M1 = 1.** Latvian, polite "you", everyday words ("red lines", "alarm", "cap"); no forbidden vocabulary.
- **M2 = 1.** "this year about €33,000 ... up to €35,000"; "a decline of 10–12% a year"; the Q4 assumption is labelled.
- **M3 = 1.** "The town lost a quarter of its residents ..., turnover is falling for the third year, and advertising here goes against the current."
- **M4 = 1.** "+30% (about €43,000) with marketing next year is not a realistic target"; shows that even if it worked, the extra ~10,000 € revenue gives only 2,000–2,500 € profit (with a sensitivity check at 40 %). It shows what +30 % would require less explicitly than Response 1 (it gives no "reverse −10 % into +30 %" statement), but the substance is there.
- **M5 = 1 (with a gap).** "The end of the lease around June 2027" named as the biggest danger; long renewal or a rent increase "the blow falls on your €4,000"; options by end of February: renew on better terms, relocate, calm closure; cheap test first; "about €2 an hour". Like Response 1, it does not say outright that a wrong exit is cheap. It warns against a hasty closure instead, but it does name the costly mistake correctly as the long renewal.
- **M6 = 1.** "If nothing changes: in 2027 turnover €29,000–30,000, profit €4,000–4,600. If the plan works (...): €32,000–34,000, profit €4,800–5,400." The profit figures follow from its labelled 20–25 % margin assumption (I checked: 6,000 − 7,000 × 0.20–0.25 ≈ 4,250–4,600). They are more optimistic than the key's "near zero", but the route is valid.
- **M7 = 1.** Two numerical red lines (reserve < 2,600 €; revenue three months running >10 % below the same month last year), tested against the forecast; weekly measurement; first check on Monday 2 November; lease decision by end of February; exactly three questions. Its questions differ from the key's examples (it asks nothing about what was tried or what is growing in the town), but it handles the owner's priorities through a proposed order that it invites the owner to change.
- **M8 = 1.** ≈ 580 words, close to the limit but within it.

## Step 4. Criteria

### Response 1
- **C1 = 6.** The core arithmetic is right (−10–12 %, 33–35k, 43–44k, ~2 €/h, 2,500 € at a labelled 25 % margin). However, it calls the revenue fall "at roughly the same pace" as the population, when −30 % in three years is about twice the annual pace of −25 % in five. It also has several small unlabelled claims and the "remaining €4,000" slip after spending 300–500 €.
- **C2 = 8.** It identifies the core issue: the request contradicts a shrinking customer base, the owner earns ~2 €/h, and the lease is the real clock. It names the lease without the date and only partly says what a bad renewal would hit.
- **C3 = 7.** The steps are sensible and the owner can do them (2–3 weeks of data, 3–4 months of tests capped at 300–500 €, cut hours, negotiate early). The hour cut has no date, the lease criterion is left to the owner to invent, and "by month 5" is ambiguous.
- **C4 = 7.** Latvian, plain words, ≈ 519 words, and it speaks to the marketing request through cheap tests. One clear breach: four numbered information requests against a ≤3 budget.
- **C5 = 5.** It has a test budget, a 6–8 week cutoff, an early lease decision and a question about whether to continue. It gives no concrete threshold, no measurement cadence, no review date and no proposed priority order, and the only thing it says to leave alone is the reserve.
- **C6 = 7.** No promises, ranges given, and the margin assumption is labelled. It has a nothing-changes revenue figure and a plan target of +5–10 %, but no profit scenarios, and the plan case is framed as a goal rather than a conditional forecast.

### Response 2
- **C1 = 8.** All arithmetic is correct and consistent (33–35k, 43k, 10,000 → 2,000–2,500, 2,600/month, 1.5 months, June 2027, Monday 2 November). The margin assumption is labelled and tested for sensitivity. Minor points: "largest employer" reinterprets the task, and the "months of costs" reserve measure includes cost of goods, which is conceptually loose.
- **C2 = 9.** It names the shrinking town, three years of decline and ~2 €/h. It identifies the most dangerous event with its date (lease end ≈ June 2027) and what it would hit: a long renewal or a rent rise falling on the 4,000 €.
- **C3 = 9.** The steps are dated (this week / end of November / end of December / end of February), each is the owner's own action, the free and reversible step comes first, and it sets caps (300 €, 3 h/week). The landlord-dependent step is protected by "without signing anything". A small tension between "one thing" and "one at a time" over several options.
- **C4 = 9.** Latvian, everyday words, three questions, ≈ 580 words (within budget but close). It answers the real question behind the request; there is little marketing content, which is right here.
- **C5 = 9.** It proposes a priority order, explicitly labelled as its own and open to change. It sets two red lines with reasons and tests them against the forecast (it admits the second is already ringing), names weekly metrics and the first review date, and lists what not to do now. The second red line being already triggered weakens it slightly as a threshold.
- **C6 = 9.** Two conditional forecasts with revenue and profit ranges, "There are no guarantees; one good month does not yet prove anything", and a sensitivity check. The nothing-changes profit (4,000–4,600) may be optimistic compared with the key, but it follows from the stated assumption.
