# Judgment — T09 (Newspaper kiosk in a small town), Response 1 vs Response 2

## Step 1. Gold facts recomputed

- Turnover decline: 48,000 → 42,000 = −6,000 / 48,000 = **−12.5 %**; 42,000 → 37,000 = −5,000 / 42,000 = **−11.9 %**.
- 2026 run-rate: 25,000 / 9 × 12 = **33,333 €**, i.e. −3,667 / 37,000 = **−9.9 %** against 2025. Since 2023: 48,000 → 33,333 = −30.6 % ("almost a third").
- Population: 8,000 → 6,000 = **−25 %** in five years; the main plant closed in 2023.
- +30 % on 33,333 = **43,333 €** (+10,000 €; ≈ 27 €/day if open 365 days). +30 % on 2025 (37,000) = 48,100 € = the 2023 level. 43,333 is above the 2024 level (42,000).
- Owner's time: 55 h × 48 weeks = 2,640 h → 6,000 / 2,640 = **€2.27/h**; 55 × 52 = 2,860 h → **€2.10/h**. So ≈ €2/h either way.
- Average profit margin 2025: 6,000 / 37,000 = 16.2 %. +10,000 € turnover at 16 % = +1,620 € → +0.57 €/h.
- Savings 4,000 € ≈ 8 months of current profit (6,000 / 12 = 500 €/month).
- Lease ends in 8 months: today is 16 Oct 2026 → about June 2027. A natural decision point; the cost of a wrong (orderly) exit is low: 4,000 € reserve, no stated long-term liabilities.
- "Nothing changes" 2027: 33,333 × 0.88–0.90 ≈ 29,300–30,000 €; profit falls at least proportionally and faster if costs are fixed.
- Niche (parcel point, bills, services, other format): unknown, so it needs questions or cheap tests.

## Step 2. Mandatory elements

### Response 1
- **M1 = 1.** The reply is in natural Latvian with everyday words ("headwind", "red line", "receipts"). It does not use "vector", "parameter", "zone" or "manoeuvre". One small oddity is "we no longer spend on it" (first-person plural).
- **M2 = 1.** "falling for the second year in a row by ~12% (€48 → 42 → 37 thousand), and this year it is about 10% lower: … roughly €33,000 for the year". The trend and the full-year estimate are both correct, and the assumption is labelled ("I assume the last three months are average…").
- **M3 = 1.** "The town has a quarter fewer people… That is your strongest headwind, and advertising does not turn it around." This names the shrinking town as the strongest force and says advertising will not reverse it.
- **M4 = 1.** It gives no generic marketing plan. It shows what +30 % requires: about 43,000 €, +10,000 €, ≈ 27 €/day, or the 2023 level if counted from last year. It adds that even success would add only ≈ 0.6 €/h. The main answer is redirected to what the hours are worth and to the lease.
- **M5 = 0.** The lease end (around June 2027) is named as the biggest threat, with dates for talks (January) and the decision (March). The owner's time is valued at ≈ €2/h, and a cheap test (≤ 500 €) is proposed. But the response never says that the cost of a wrong exit is low. It frames losing the spot as a danger ("you may lose the place"). It does not offer an orderly exit with the 4,000 € intact as one of the options.
- **M6 = 1.** It gives two numbered forecasts. Nothing changes: 29–30k €, profit 4,500–5,000 € "if it follows turnover", below €2/h. Plan works: 33–37k € "if the new services outweigh the further decline… This is not a promise". The downside profit is probably optimistic (the gold expects near zero), but it is labelled as conditional.
- **M7 = 0.** There are measurable stop conditions with dates: review on 30 Nov, stop if the new service brings < 50 €/week, stop experiments if savings fall below 2,500 €. There are 2 questions, within budget. But the conditions govern the experiments, not the renew-or-exit choice at the lease. The questions (rent; product mix and margin) miss what has been tried and what is growing in the town. The "what to give up last" topic is covered only by a proposed order the owner may correct, not by a question.
- **M8 = 1.** About 560 whitespace-separated tokens, including symbols. That is within 600.

### Response 2

- **M2 = 1.** "€25,000 for nine months means about €33,000–35,000 this year (−5 to −10%…)" and "a business that is falling by about 10% a year". This gives the trend and the estimate. It does not show the −12.5 % and −11.9 % yearly figures. The 35k / −5 % upper end has no support, though it is labelled ("I don't know that").
- **M3 = 0.** The environment appears only in passing ("while the town keeps shrinking"). "Marketing does not remove this risk" refers to the lease lock-in, not to the town's decline. The response never names the shrinking town (−25 %, plant closure) as the strongest force, stronger than the owner's means. This is a gesture, not a statement.
- **M4 = 1.** The main answer is not a marketing plan. It shows that +30 % means 43–45k €, "so more than in 2024, while the town keeps shrinking. Nobody can promise that", and that even success gives only about 2.5–3 €/h.
- **M5 = 0.** It names lease renewal as the biggest risk (lock-in to a business falling ≈ 10 % a year). "Today: open the lease and write down by what date you must give notice…" is a strong step. It proposes a short 6–12-month extension and values time at ≈ €2/h. But it does not state the 8-month date and says nothing about the low cost of a wrong exit. An orderly exit is only implied ("Whether to continue… you decide").
- **M6 = 1.** Three forecasts with numbers. Nothing changes: 30–33k €, profit 3,500–5,000 €. Plan works (+30 %): 43–45k €, profit 7,000–8,300 €. Partial ("a more realistic outcome"): 34–37k €, profit 4,500–5,700 €. The "plan works" case simply adopts the +30 % target rather than a labelled range for a new niche, but the realistic range is present.
- **M7 = 0.** It sets a measurable condition: monthly year-on-year comparison, with a drop to ≤ 5 % meaning the steps work, checked at the end of January and in March when the lease is decided. That condition is well tied to the lease. But it has no explicit stop side, and its single question (rent; seasonality) misses what has been tried, what is growing in the town, and what the owner would give up last.
- **M8 = 1.** About 368 words.

## Step 3. Forbidden items and invented facts

### Response 1
- **Forbidden:** none. It gives no generic marketing plan; the "tell local employers, school and council about the new service" step is targeted and free. It does not declare the kiosk dead and it explores niches (parcels, bills, tickets). The priority order is proposed, not imposed: "I suggest this order… If the kiosk matters more to you than the hours, tell me".
- **Invented facts:** none. "≈ 27 €/day" assumes 365 open days, which is derived and minor. "A rent deposit" and "the decision must be made by March" are presented as risks or recommendations, not facts.

### Response 2
- **Forbidden:** none counted. F1 is borderline: the cheap-test examples include a loyalty discount card, a post in a local group and a window sign, which are exactly the generic items. They are one option among several with a 300–500 € cap, not the answer, so F1 is not triggered. They still dilute the advice.
- **Invented facts:** one minor item. "about €2 an hour before taxes": the task says the profit is before the owner's pay, not "before taxes". The 25–35 cents per euro margin is labelled as an assumption.

## Step 4. Criteria

### Response 1
- **C1 = 9.** Every number checks out (12 %, 10 %, 33k, 43k, +10k, 27 €/day, 48k, 2,860 h, ≈ €2/h, 16 %, 1,600 €, 0.6 €/h, June 2027, 500 €/month, 8 and 5 months, 29–30k), and the assumptions are labelled. The only quibble is that the downside profit is taken as proportional, although the response itself notes the marginal margin is higher.

- **C3 = 8.** The steps are dated and owner-doable, cheap and reversible first: receipts by hour 19 Oct–2 Nov; 2–3 providers by 15 Nov, start with one, ≤ 500 €; landlord in January with a concrete ask. It misses checking the lease's notice deadline now, which could fall before January.
- **C4 = 8.** It answers the question actually asked by explaining why +30 % is the wrong target, in Latvian, about 560 words, with 2 questions and everyday words. It is dense with numbers, with one slightly odd phrasing.
- **C5 = 9.** Control is strong. It proposes an explicit priority order and invites correction. The 2,500 € red line has a reason and is tested against the forecast. It sets weekly measures, a review on 30 Nov, a < 50 €/week stop rule and a "don't do now" list. It has no explicit renew-or-exit criterion.
- **C6 = 8.** No promises ("This is not a promise"). Two conditional range forecasts; the plan-works range (33–37k) is modest rather than +30 %. The downside profit is likely too rosy.

### Response 2
- **C1 = 8.** The arithmetic is correct and consistent under its labelled 25–35 c/€ assumption: profit ranges, 2.5–3 €/h, 43–45k. It relabels the profit as "before taxes", and the 35k / −5 % upper end has no support beyond "year-end may be stronger".
- **C2 = 6.** It names lease lock-in as the biggest risk and shows that +30 % pays little per hour. But it barely names the shrinking town as the dominant force, gives no lease date, and does not weigh the low cost of exit.
- **C3 = 7.** The today / this week / this month order works, and "open the lease today and note the notice deadline" is the best single step in either response. The monthly tests are an open menu mixed with generic marketing, with no single starting choice or dates.
- **C4 = 8.** Latvian, about 368 words, 1 question, everyday words; compact and fits the QUICK mode. One minor slip: "euro".
- **C5 = 5.** It sets a monthly y/y measure, a ≤ 5 % threshold, reviews in January and March tied to the lease, and a 300–500 € cap. It makes no priorities explicit (only "you decide"), sets no savings red line, and has no "leave untouched" list.
- **C6 = 7.** It promises nothing ("Nobody can promise that") and gives three ranges with a "more realistic" label. But presenting +30 % (43–45k, 7–8.3k profit) as the "plan works" scenario partly legitimises the target it just called unpromisable.

## Step 6. Preference

Response 1 is better, with confidence 0.7. It names the shrinking town as the decisive force and turns +30 % into what it means per day and per hour of the owner's time. It gives dated, owner-doable steps with a savings red line tested against the forecast, an explicit proposed priority order and clear stop rules. Response 2 is shorter and has the valuable "check the lease notice deadline today" step. But it mixes in generic marketing items, uses +30 % as a forecast scenario, and leaves priorities and red lines out. Neither says that an orderly exit is cheap, and neither asks what is growing in the town.
