# Judgment — T08 (Print shop: "profitable" but cash falling) — Response 1 vs Response 2

## Step 1 — Recomputed key numbers

- Cash: €22,000 → €13,000 = −€9,000 over 6 months = **−€1,500/month**.
- Receivables: €8,000 → €21,000 = **+€13,000** (≈ +€2,167/month).
- Paper profit: €3,000 × 6 = €18,000. If €13,000 of it is stuck in receivables, cash should have risen ≈ €5,000. It fell €9,000, so ≈ **€14,000 is unexplained** (≈ €2,333/month). Possible causes: drawings, loan principal, equipment, tax/VAT, stock, or an overstated profit figure. None of these can be stated as fact.
- Reserve: €13,000 / €26,000 = **0.5 months**. That is red against a proposed 1-month line.
- Months to zero at −€1,500/month: 13,000 / 1,500 ≈ **8.7 months**.
- 40 % customer: if receivables are spread in proportion to sales, ≈ 0.4 × €21,000 ≈ **€8,400** is at risk. In practice it is likely more, because this customer is the late payer.
- Response 2's own figures, checked:
  - Sales ≈ 26,000 + 3,000 = 29,000 (labelled assumption); 40 % = 11,600.
  - Moving from 30 to 90 days ties up 2 × 11,600 ≈ 23,200 extra; minus the 13,000 already visible leaves ≈ 10,000 more.
  - €14,000 / 6 ≈ €2,300/month; 3,000 − 2,333 ≈ €700.
  - €8,000 ≈ 9.2 days of costs.
  - No-change forecast for end of January (≈ 3.5 months): 13,000 − 5,250 ≈ €7,750 ≈ €8,000.
  - Worst case: receivables grow €10,000 instead of the trend's ≈ €7,600, so ≈ €5,300 ≈ €5,000; the €8,000 trigger is reached at ≈ 2.3 months, i.e. late December.
  - Plan-works forecast for 31 December: 13,000 + 6,500…13,000 + ≈ 1,700 ≈ €21,000–€28,000.
  - All of these hold under a coherent reading. The phrase "adds the further €10,000" is loosely worded: the €10,000 replaces the trend's receivable growth rather than adding to the drift.
