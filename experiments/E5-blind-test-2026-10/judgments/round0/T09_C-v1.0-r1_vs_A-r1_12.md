# Judgment: T09, Response 1 vs Response 2

## Step 1. Recomputed key numbers

- Decline: 48,000 → 42,000 = −12.5 %; 42,000 → 37,000 = −11.9 %.
- 2026 run-rate: 25,000 / 9 × 12 = 33,333 (−9.9 % vs 37,000). Trend ≈ −10…−12 %/year.
- +30 % on 33,333 = 43,333 (above 2024's 42,000 and near the pre-decline path). On 37,000 it would be 48,100.
- Nothing-changes 2027: 33,333 × 0.87…0.92 ≈ 29,000–30,700.
- Hourly pay: 6,000 / (55 × 48 = 2,640 h) = €2.27/h; using 55 × 52 = 2,860 h it is €2.10/h. 6,000 / 12 = €500/month.
- Implied total costs last year: 37,000 − 6,000 = 31,000 (€2,583/month). Reserve 4,000 ≈ 1.5 months of that.
- 2026 profit range: all costs fixed → 33,333 − 31,000 ≈ 2,300; costs proportional (16.2 % margin) → ≈ 5,400.
- Population 8,000 → 6,000 = −25 % in five years. Factory closed in 2023 (so it was not working in 2024).
- Lease ends in 8 months ≈ June 2027 = the decision point. A wrong exit is cheap (€4,000 reserve, no stated long-term liabilities).
- Reference only (not in the task): Latvian minimum wage 2026 is €780/month gross, ≈ €4.5/h.

Word-count method: I counted lexical words. A number written with a thousands space ("48 000") counts as one word. Standalone symbols (%, €, →, ×) and list markers count as zero. A raw whitespace count is higher for both: about 590 for Response 1 and about 650+ for Response 2.

- **M5 = 0.** Partly present:
  - Lease end (June 2027) is named as the biggest threat, with the lease decision by 31 March.
  - Cheap tests come before renewal: new goods, a parcel point, ads ≤ €300, and no long lease before March.
  - The owner's time is valued (€2.10/h).

  What is missing: it never says that a wrong exit costs little. It never sets out an orderly exit with the €4,000 intact as one of the main options. Closure appears only as the last word of a fallback list ("another place …, fewer hours, or closing").
- **M6 = 1.** Both forecasts are there with numbers:
  - Nothing changes: 29,000–30,500 €, a loss of 500–2,000 €, and the reserve down to 2,000.
  - Plan works: 31,000–34,000 €, with stated conditions, labelled "an estimate, not a promise".
- **M7 = 1.** The stop/continue condition is measurable and dated: by 31 March, if revenue is still more than 10 % below last year or the reserve is below 2,600 €, then relocate, cut hours or close. There are exactly 3 questions. The "what would you give up last" point is handled as a proposed order the owner can change.
- **M8 = 1.** About 515 words, within the 600-word budget.

- **M8 = 0.** About 605 lexical words, and about 650+ with any standard counter. Over the 600-word budget.

## Step 3. Forbidden items and invented facts

### Response 1
- **Forbidden:** none.
  - F1: no generic plan.
  - F3: it does not declare the kiosk dead; it proposes tests.
  - F4: the priority order is explicitly proposed, with "If your order is different, tell me".
- **Invented or incorrect facts:**
  - "more than in 2024, when the factory was still working" contradicts the task, which says the factory closed in 2023.
  - "considerably below the minimum wage" relies on an external rule. No figure is given, and the claim is true.
- **Assumption, stated as a condition:** the 2027 nothing-changes loss assumes all 31,000 € of costs stay fixed ("with costs of about €31,000"). That is the pessimistic case, even though the response itself shows that costs may partly fall with sales.

### Response 2
- **Forbidden:** none.
  - F1: marketing ("Visibility") is one test among several, not the answer.
  - F4 is borderline but not counted. It sets "at least minimum-wage pay for your time" as the new goal without asking the owner's priorities ("I would change the goal"). This is framed as the adviser's view, and the final A/B/C choice stays with the owner.
- **Invented facts:**
  - "newspapers, is shrinking across the whole country" is an external cause stated as fact.
  - "the minimum wage is about €4.5 an hour gross" is an external figure.
  - "Full-time work at the minimum wage gives about €780 a month gross" is an external figure. It is correct, and it also assumes a job is available in a town whose main factory closed.
  - "The commissions are small" is an unsupported claim about parcel and payment services.
  - "the landlord doesn't have many alternative tenants" is an unlabelled inference stated as fact.
- **Minor arithmetic issues:**
  - The "≈ €3/h" figure adds +2,000–2,500 € to last year's 6,000 €, but the 10k increment is measured from this year's ≈ 34k, so the bases are mixed. The error is small and makes its conclusion stronger, not weaker.
  - "almost the 2023 level" overstates the closeness: 44k vs 48k.
- **Labelled assumption (fine):** a gross margin of 20–25 %.

## Step 4. Criteria

### Response 1
- **C1 = 6.** The numbers are all correct and derived from the task: decline rate, run-rate, €2.10/h, 31,000 costs, the 2,000–5,400 profit range, 1.5 months of reserve, the red line, and the 2027 range. There is one factual slip that contradicts the task (factory in 2024) and an unquantified minimum-wage reference. Neither changes the advice.
- **C2 = 8.** It names the shrinking town as the dominant force. It names the nearest irreversible damage precisely: signing a multi-year lease in June 2027 locks in costs while revenue falls, and the reserve path breaches the red line. It also sets up a test of whether customers or basket size are vanishing. It does not frame the low cost of a wrong exit.
- **C3 = 9.** Every step has an owner ("you") and a date:
  - this week: hourly receipt log;
  - by 31 Oct: landlord terms;
  - by 15 Nov: 10 regulars and courier firms;
  - by 1 Feb: drop unprofitable hours.

  Fast, cheap and reversible steps come first, and the ad spend is capped at €300.
- **C4 = 9.** It is in Latvian, within budget (≈ 515 words), asks exactly 3 questions and uses plain words. It answers the real question by explaining why +30 % is the wrong target.
- **C5 = 9.** All the control parts are present and coherent:
  - The priority order is proposed and open to change.
  - The 2,600 € red line has a reason (one month of costs) and is tested against the forecast (the worst case falls to 2,000).
  - Measurement is set: hourly receipts, monthly revenue vs the same month last year, and the reserve weekly.
  - Review dates are set: 2 Nov, then monthly, then the lease decision by 31 Mar.
  - The "do not do now" list is explicit.
- **C6 = 9.** "I cannot promise that", and "This is an estimate, not a promise". It gives ranges with conditions and two numeric forecasts.

### Response 2
- **C1 = 5.** The core arithmetic is right (decline, 33–35k, 44k, €2/h), and the margin assumption is labelled. But it states several external facts as fact without labels (minimum wage figures, national press decline, small commissions, few alternative tenants) and mixes bases in the ≈ €3/h figure.
- **C2 = 8.** Its reframe is sharp: even a successful +30 % leaves pay at about €3/h, below minimum wage. It names the lease-free 8 months as leverage and flags auto-renewal or notice terms. It does not quantify the reserve path or what a bad renewal would hit.
- **C3 = 7.** The steps are right and doable: sales by group, fixed costs, hourly log, lease terms, 2–3 cheap tests, a February decision, negotiation by March. But they are phased by month ranges rather than dated, and some tests (home delivery, service point) need set-up that is not detailed.
- **C4 = 5.** It is in Latvian and largely plain, but it breaks two budgets: over 600 words, and 4 questions against a limit of 3. It ends with an offer of further work.
- **C5 = 5.** It has a goal threshold (minimum-wage pay), an experiment budget of €300–500 and a February decision point. It lacks:
  - a proposed priority order for the owner to choose from;
  - a red line on the reserve tested against a forecast;
  - a review cadence.

  Per-test stop thresholds are delegated to the owner.
- **C6 = 6.** It makes no promises ("unlikely to work") and labels its margin assumption. It lacks a two-forecast frame for its own plan, its nothing-changes forecast is a single revenue point, and option C assumes a minimum-wage job is available without conditions.

## Step 6. Preference

**Response 1, confidence 0.75.** Response 1 gives this owner a dated, self-run plan for the next eight months, within budget. It includes a reasoned reserve red line tested against a numeric nothing-changes forecast, a proposed but changeable priority order, and a measurable stop/continue rule before the lease decision. Response 2 has the sharper insight that even +30 % would not fix the hourly pay, and it frames exit as cheap more clearly. But it breaks both the word and question budgets, lacks a two-forecast frame for its own plan, and leans on several unlabelled outside facts.
