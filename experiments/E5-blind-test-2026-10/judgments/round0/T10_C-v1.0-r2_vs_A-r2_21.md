# Judgment — T10 (Response 1 vs Response 2)

## Step 1. Recomputed key numbers

- Rent shortfall: 1 100 − 450 = **€650**.
- Credit-line cost: 650 × 12 % / 12 ≈ **€6.5/month**; for an €800 draw (650 + 150 buffer), 800 × 1 %/month = **€8/month**. Cheap, fast, reversible.
- After drawing €800 and paying rent: 450 + 800 − 1 100 = **€150** left on the account; **€1 200** left on the line.
- If the client keeps not paying, the next rent (1 100) needs 950 more from the line: line debt 800 + 950 = **€1 750**, **€250** left. So the line covers this rent and the next one, and then it is gone (about 1.5–2 months of runway).
- Client debt 3 800 / 1 100 ≈ **3.5 months of rent**. The bigger danger is that the client defaults or pays very late, not this month's rent.
- Food and children are covered by the wife's salary. Housing is the thing to protect first.

## Step 3. Forbidden items and invented facts

### Response 1

This is an immediate suspension on day 0, at the same moment it gives the client 5 working days. It is softened by "First check the contract" and by written notice in the same letter. Under M4's own framing ("without written notice") I treat it as the permitted form, but it is aggressive towards the main client and I penalise it under C3/C5.

  3. "Suspension in case of late payment is usually allowed" (a legal claim).

### Response 2

- **Invented facts:**

## Step 4. Criteria

### Response 1
- **C1 = 6.** All the arithmetic is correct (650, 800, €8/month, €1 200 left, "a month and a half" is a fair rough figure). But it brings in three or four external legal rules and figures not in the task, hedged but stated as fact.
- **C2 = 8.** It finds the real problem: the rent is just arithmetic, the danger is the client not paying, and the line buys only about 1.5 months. It adds a solvency check. It does not quantify the 3.5 months, and it plays down the near deadline rather than dating it.
- **C3 = 7.** The steps are concrete and doable by the person (check line terms, draw early, call accounting, formal letter with 5 working days, registry check, invoice other clients, template letter). But there are too many for an emergency, the timing beyond "today" is loose, and immediate suspension of work is a premature escalation.
- **C4 = 3.** It ignores the emergency format: the first line is not an action, and at about 555 words it is almost twice the budget. Language and question count are fine.
- **C5 = 4.** It has a "don't do" list and an escalation timeline. But it proposes no priority order for the person to confirm, no red line or threshold, and no single review date tied to a measurement.
- **C6 = 6.** It is cautious about the law ("The details depend… check") and makes no promises. It gives no scenario forecast (client pays vs keeps silent) and names no riskiest assumption.

### Response 2
- **C1 = 7.** All numbers are correct (650, 800, 150, €8/month, €1 750 debt, €250 left), and the assumption is labelled ("Assumption: there are no other large payments…"). It loses points for one external legal rule (the €40 per invoice) and for the unlabelled assumption that one invoice ≥ €650.
- **C2 = 5.** It names the nearest damage precisely (rent in 6 days, €650 gap, reversible through the line). But it calls the rent "the most dangerous thing" and misses that the client's default is the larger danger. The forecast hints at it without naming it.
- **C3 = 7.** The order is clear and timed (call now → letter today with a 3-day deadline → day 3 trigger → draw €800), fast and reversible moves come first, and the person can do all of it alone. Nothing addresses the default risk (no solvency check, no pipeline), and "send a written demand" on day 3 slightly duplicates the letter already sent.
- **C4 = 9.** It fits the emergency mode: the action is in line one, about 287 words, two questions in one, in Russian, in plain words.
- **C5 = 8.** It proposes a priority order with "correct me if yours is different" and a red line with its reason, tested against the forecast ("You are already below it… you will be at minus €650"). It sets a 3-day check with a trigger and gives what not to touch now. What to measure is only implicit (money arrived or not).
- **C6 = 8.** It gives two forecasts (client pays one invoice / client stays silent), labels its assumption and makes no guarantees. The "the line will not be needed" claim is one soft, invoice-size-dependent statement.
