# Evaluation — T05 (dog-grooming salon, two turns), Response 1 vs Response 2

## Step 1. Recomputed key numbers

- Profit: 7,200 − 6,900 = **€300/month**; 300 / 7,200 = **4.2 %**.
- Reserve: 2,500 / 6,900 = **0.36 months** (about 11 days). Below any sensible red line (1 month = €6,900). Gap to the line: 6,900 − 2,500 = €4,400.
- Reserve against costs excluding owner pay: 2,500 / 5,100 = 0.49 months (about 2 weeks).
- Owner's hourly pay: 60 h × 4.33 weeks ≈ 260 h; 1,800 / 260 ≈ **€6.9/h (about €7)**.
- Booked revenue if every appointment were served: 7,200 / 0.8 = **€9,000**, so no-shows cost about **€1,800/month**. This assumes empty slots are not refilled. If half are refilled, the loss is about €900.
- Halving no-shows (20 % → 10 %): served = 9,000 × 0.9 = 8,100, so **+€900/month** and profit about €1,200. At 14 % no-shows: 9,000 × 0.86 = 7,740, so +€540 and profit €840.
- Nothing changes: 4,400 / 300 ≈ **14.7 months (about 15)** to reach 1 month of reserve. After 6 months the reserve is 2,500 + 1,800 = €4,300.
- Plan works: at a steady +€900 it takes 4,400 / 1,200 ≈ **3.7 months (about 4)**. With a two-month linear ramp it takes about 4.4 months at the 10 % end and about 5.9 months at the 14 % end.
- A 5 % price rise is worth 7,200 × 0.05 = **€360** (or about €405 on €8,100 after the no-show fix), if no client leaves.
- A 10 % revenue drop costs €720, so profit goes from 300 to **−€420**.
- Calendar check: 16 Oct 2026 is a Friday, 18 Oct a Sunday, 19 Oct a Monday and 16 Nov a Monday. These dates are internally consistent.

## Step 3. Forbidden items and invented facts

### Response 1
- Forbidden: none. It explicitly says "No hiring, equipment, loans", and quotes no benchmarks or unexplained changes.
- Invented facts: none. The deposit amount and the 5 % price rise are labelled as illustrations, the refill assumption is labelled, and the 4.3 weeks per month is a standard conversion. One loose claim is "The picture holds if any number here is off by 10%". This is not an invented fact, but it is shaky, because the response itself shows that a 10 % revenue shortfall turns profit negative.

### Response 2
- Forbidden: **F4**. The price-rise value moves from €360 to €400 between turns with no explanation.
- Invented facts:
  1. "That's well below what you'd earn employed elsewhere": a wage comparison that is not in the task.
  2. "A few repeat clients often cause most of them": a general pattern stated as fact.
  3. "Most booking tools can do this": a product claim that is not in the task.
- Minor arithmetic issue: 300 + 900 + 400 = 1,600, yet turn 2 says "roughly €1,500". There is no explanation in turn 2, although turn 1 mentioned slightly higher supply costs.
- Unlabelled assumption: the price rise assumes no client is lost.

## Step 4. Criteria

### Response 1
- **C1 = 8.** All core numbers are correct and derived from the task, with assumptions and illustrations labelled. There are two small weaknesses: the 5–7-month range is somewhat conservative, and the "picture holds if off by 10%" claim is loose.
- **C2 = 8.** It finds the real issues: no-shows larger than the whole profit, the reserve at 0.36 months, the €7/h pay, and the trade-off between the owner's time and pay. The danger is named as a weak month ("quiet January", a sick week), but the quantified example is absorbable, and the case where the owner is ill is not pushed to its consequence.
- **C3 = 9.** Every step is dated, has an owner ("you or your groomer") and is ordered fast and reversible first: confirmations and reminders today, then a targeted deposit by Sunday, then a price review by mid-November.
- **C4 = 9.** Both turns are within the word and question budgets, in English and in everyday words. Turn 2 respects "no questions" and gives a clean final answer. Turn 1 is near the cap for a "quick check".
- **C5 = 9.** It proposes a priority order and leaves the choice to the owner. It sets a red line and tests it against both forecasts. It gives weekly measures, a review date, triggers for changing the plan and a list of what not to do. The reason given for the red line is brief.
- **C6 = 9.** There are no promises. It uses ranges with conditions, gives two forecasts including a worst case ("if deposits drive clients away"), and names the riskiest assumption in turn 2 with its effect.

### Response 2
- **C1 = 5.** The core arithmetic (€300, 11 days, €7/h, €1,800, €900, €360) is right. However, the €1,500 vs €1,600 sum, the unexplained change from €360 to €400, the unlabelled no-client-loss assumption and three unsupported general claims add up to more than one minor issue.
- **C2 = 6.** It sees that no-shows are the biggest leak, that the pay is very low and that the cushion is thin. The VAT and tax check is a sharp point that could reverse the picture. But the danger is one unquantified line with no date, and turn 2 drops it.
- **C3 = 7.** The steps are concrete and ordered (today: no-show rule; reminders and waitlist; check repeat offenders by name; price rise). Turn 2 stacks a deposit on every booking and a price rise in the same week. These are two client-facing changes at once, which are less reversible, and no owner or dates are given beyond "this week".
- **C4 = 9.** It is concise and within budget in both turns (3 questions, then 0), in plain English, and acknowledges the "no more data" request gracefully.
- **C5 = 4.** It sets a reserve target and a rule of €500 a month into the reserve, says not to change the groomer's hours, and has one 8-week checkpoint. There is no priority order, no red line, no weekly measures and no review date.
- **C6 = 5.** The no-show figure is labelled as an "upper estimate", and turn 2 offers a "half works" variant. But there is no "nothing changes" forecast, the risk of losing clients from the price rise and deposits is not mentioned, and turn 2 does not name the riskiest assumption.
