# Judgment — T09 (Newspaper kiosk in a small town), Response 1 vs Response 2

## Step 1. Key numbers I recomputed

- Turnover decline: 48,000 → 42,000 = −6,000 / 48,000 = **−12.5 %**; 42,000 → 37,000 = −5,000 / 42,000 = **−11.9 %**.
- 2026 run-rate: 25,000 / 9 × 12 = **33,333**; vs 37,000 = −3,667 / 37,000 = **−9.9 % (≈ −10 %)**.
- +30 % on 33,333 = **43,333**, which is +10,000 a year. From the 2025 base: 37,000 × 1.3 = **48,100**, which is above even 2023.
- Nothing-changes 2027: 33,333 × 0.9 ≈ **30,000**.
- Population 8,000 → 6,000 = **−25 % over five years**, about −5.6 % a year. Turnover is falling about twice as fast per year.
- Net margin 2025: 6,000 / 37,000 = **16.2 %**.
- Owner's hours: 55 × 48 = 2,640 h, or 55 × 52 = 2,860 h. 6,000 / 2,640–2,860 = **€2.1–2.3 per hour**, about €500 a month.
- The lease ends 8 months after 16 Oct 2026, so **around mid-June 2027**. Any notice period would fall earlier. Reserve €4,000; no stated debts, so a wrong exit costs little.

## Step 2. Mandatory elements

### Response 1
- **M1 = 1.** The reply is in Latvian, uses everyday words and has none of the banned terms. "Red line" and "cap" are ordinary phrases.
- **M2 = 1.** It says "decreasing … by roughly 12% a year" and "2026 is heading for about €33,000 (25,000 ÷ 9 × 12; 10% less than last year)". The calculation is shown and correct. Saying ~12 % for all three years slightly overstates the last year, which is ~10 %.
- **M3 = 1.** "With marketing alone, +30% next year is not realistic: the town is shrinking, and advertising will not bring back those who have left". It also says the town has lost a quarter of its people and that the shrinkage is not the owner's fault. The environment is named as the force that marketing cannot reverse.
- **M4 = 1.** It gives no generic plan. It works out +30 % = 43,000, which is 10,000 more a year (48,100 from the 2025 base). It says "+30% ... that needs something that adds new customers to the kiosk, not just advertising, and I cannot promise that." Marketing is put last in the order: "the lease, working hours and the offer, only then marketing".
- **M5 = 0.** Several parts are there:
  - The lease end is named as the biggest threat and dated (mid-June), with a warning about notice periods and auto-renewal.
  - It suggests testing new services (parcels, bills, tickets) for at most 500 € before lease talks.
  - It values the owner's time at about €2/h.

  But it never says that a wrong exit would cost little. An orderly exit with the reserve intact is not offered as a main option. Exit appears only as a fallback if a trigger fires ("smaller premises or ending it"). The options lean towards staying on a short lease.
- **M6 = 1.** It gives two forecasts:
  - Nothing changes: 2027 about 30,000 (28,000–32,000), profit 3,900–4,600 €, worst case about 3,300 €.
  - Plan works: 33,000–36,000, labelled "my estimate, not a calculation", profit 4,800–5,800 €.

  Both rest on a labelled assumption of 20–30 cents profit per euro of turnover. Its "nothing changes" profit is higher than the key's "near zero", but it follows correctly from the stated assumption.
- **M7 = 1.** The stop triggers are measurable: turnover more than 15 % below last year for three months running, or reserve below 3,000 €. Either leads to smaller premises or closing, which falls before the lease decision. It asks 2 questions (rent and lease terms; turnover split). It also asks the owner to say whether the kiosk matters more than income.
- **M8 = 1.** About 577 words.

Total: 7/8.

### Response 2
- **M1 = 1.** The reply is in Latvian and plain. "Margin" and "fixed costs" are mild business words but easy to understand.
- **M2 = 1.** It says "has been falling by roughly 10–12% a year for the third year now" and puts 2026 at 33–35 thousand €. The range includes the 33,333 run-rate. The upper end rests on an unstated assumption about how strong the fourth quarter is.
- **M3 = 1.** "So, most likely, what is missing is not advertising but buyers"; "you would have to find €13–14 thousand of extra turnover in a town that is losing residents"; "Advertising raises demand for newspapers and lotteries only a little."
- **M4 = 1.** It shows +30 % = 44,000, "higher than in 2024", and 13–14 thousand above the trend. It also points out that even if the target were reached, the owner would earn only about +1 € an hour. It gives no generic plan.
- **M5 = 1.**
  - The plan is built around the lease: "Plan until the end of the lease", with the decision due by March, before renewal.
  - Options: test one repeat-visit service in January–February; negotiate a shorter or turnover-linked lease; or exit with the reserve intact ("The €4,000 savings are a safety cushion for the exit").
  - It values time at about €2/h.
  - It frames a low-cost exit: "Closing the kiosk is also a decision, not a failure: a kiosk earning about €2 an hour does not have to be saved at any cost."
- **M6 = 0.** The only nothing-changes forecast is for turnover (2027 about 30–31 thousand €), with no profit figure. The plan-works case ("you hold on to about €34 thousand") is a target, not a labelled forecast range, and also has no profit figure. There are no two numeric forecasts.
- **M7 = 0.** The continue/stop rule uses placeholders: "at least €X and profit per hour at least €Y". These are not measurable conditions. It also asks for four pieces of information ("write: (1)…(4)") plus one direct question. In substance that is more than the 3-question budget.
- **M8 = 1.** About 530 words.

Total: 6/8.

## Step 3. Forbidden items and invented facts

**Response 1.** No forbidden items. The Facebook-group and door notice is a minor step, not the answer, so F1 does not apply. It does not declare the kiosk dead (no F3). It proposes a priority order and leaves the choice open ("This order is suggested by me; if the kiosk matters more to you than the income, tell me"), so F4 does not apply. **No invented facts:**
- The cents-per-euro margin is labelled as an assumption.
- The plan-works range is labelled as an estimate.
- The dates are planning dates, not facts.

**Response 2.** No forbidden items:
- The retention ideas (press subscription delivery, school, library, Facebook) are one step inside a reframed plan, not the answer, so F1 does not apply.
- Closing is presented as a legitimate option after niche tests, not as a verdict, so F3 does not apply.
- It asks whether the owner wants to keep the kiosk and leaves Y to them, so F4 does not apply.

**Invented facts:** the comparison to the minimum wage ("well below the minimum wage") is an outside benchmark not in the task. It is correct in direction but presented as fact. Separately, "on the same scale as the fall in the number of residents" is an inaccurate comparison. The population falls about 5–6 % a year, turnover 10–12 %. I treat this under C1, not as an invented fact. "Advertising raises demand only a little" is a general claim stated without a hedge.

## Step 4. Criteria

### Response 1
- **C1 = 9.** Every figure is correct and derived from the person's own numbers:
  - run-rate 33,333;
  - +30 % = 43,000, or 10,000 more;
  - 48,100 from the 2025 base;
  - about €2/h and €500 a month;
  - both forecasts follow from the labelled 20–30 % assumption;
  - the reserve arithmetic (4,000 − 500 = 3,500).

  The only flaw is calling all three years ~12 %.
- **C2 = 8.** It finds the real problem: a shrinking town, a falling income and owner pay of €2/h. It names the most dangerous event precisely: signing a long lease at the same rent, with a notice or auto-renewal deadline that could come in spring and with the reserve as the only cushion. It does not say that a wrong exit costs little, and it explores niches only lightly.
- **C3 = 9.** The steps are dated (22 Oct, 15 Nov, 15 Jan) and the owner can do all of them. The fast, reversible ones come first: read the lease, log receipts by hour, close dead hours ("reversible"). Spending is capped at 500 € and 3 hours a week.
- **C4 = 9.** It answers the request directly, explaining why +30 % is not realistic and what to do instead. It is in Latvian with everyday words, about 577 words and 2 questions, all within budget. It is dense with numbers but readable.
- **C5 = 10.**
  - The priority order is proposed and left to the owner.
  - The red line (3,000 €, no debt) comes with a reason and is checked against the forecast: "the forecast reaches this only with lower rent".
  - It says what to measure weekly and monthly, sets the next review for 2 November, and gives explicit triggers.
  - It lists what not to do now: no long lease, no loan, no spending above 500 €, no extra hours.
- **C6 = 9.** It makes no promises ("I cannot promise that"). Both forecasts are ranges with conditions, the estimate is labelled, and the margin assumption is named along with what it would change. Start-up costs of the new services are only capped, not costed.

### Response 2
- **C1 = 7.** Most arithmetic is correct:
  - +30 % of 34 thousand ≈ 44 thousand;
  - a 13–14 thousand gap to the trend;
  - margin 16 %;
  - about €2/h;
  - +2–3 thousand a year ≈ +1 €/h;
  - a 5 % discount eating about a third of profit.

  But "same scale as the population decline" is wrong. The 33–35 thousand upper end rests on an unstated seasonality assumption. The minimum-wage benchmark comes from outside the task.
- **C2 = 7.** The diagnosis is strong: there are too few buyers, not too little advertising; pay per hour is the core problem; fixed costs make profit fall faster than turnover. It names the lease and warns against investing before renewal. It does not identify the nearest point of no return (the notice or renewal deadline) or say what that would hit.
- **C3 = 7.** The phases are sensible and doable: two weeks of measuring, November–December, January–February, a decision by March, and fewer hours. The timing is loose, the lease negotiation has no date, and some steps rely on other parties (school, library, pensioners' association).
- **C4 = 7.** It reframes the request well, in Latvian, at about 530 words. But the four numbered requests for information plus one direct question exceed the 3-question budget in substance, and "margin" and "fixed costs" are slightly managerial.
- **C5 = 6.**
  - The decision rule uses X/Y placeholders.
  - There is no savings red line beyond "cushion for the exit".
  - It sets no measurement frequency or review date, except "Decision by March".
  - It does say what to avoid: discounts, renovations, spending savings on ads.
  - It replaces the owner's goal with its own ("Not +30 %, but…"), although it asks whether the owner wants to keep the kiosk.
- **C6 = 7.** It is honest and hedged, with no promises, and tells the owner to check payback per transaction before starting a service. It lacks the two numeric forecasts: there is a trend for turnover only, no profit in either scenario, and the "plan works" case is a target, not a range.
