# Judgment — T08 (Response 1 vs Response 2)

## Step 1. Recomputed key numbers

- Cash: €22,000 → €13,000 = −€9,000 over 6 months = **−€1,500/month**.
- Receivables: €8,000 → €21,000 = **+€13,000** (≈ +€2,167/month).
- Paper profit: 6 × €3,000 = **€18,000**. Gap between profit and cash change: 18,000 − (−9,000) = **€27,000**. Receivables explain €13,000 of it, which leaves **≈ €14,000 unexplained**. Possible causes: drawings, loan principal, tax/VAT, equipment, stock, paying suppliers down, or a wrong profit figure. This should be asked about, not asserted.
- Reserve: 13,000 / 26,000 = **0.5 months**. Six months ago it was 22,000 / 26,000 = 0.85 months. A proposed red line of 1 month (€26,000) puts the shop in the red now.
- Months to zero at −€1,500/month: 13,000 / 1,500 ≈ **8.7 months**.
- Big customer: if sales ≈ expenses + profit ≈ €29,000/month (an assumption), 40% ≈ **€11,600/month**. One more month of delay would leave ≈ 13,000 − 11,600 ≈ **€1,400**. A proportional 40% share of the €21,000 receivables is ≈ €8,400.
- A working alarm of €8,000 is reached at −€1,500/month after (13,000 − 8,000) / 1,500 ≈ 3.3 months, i.e. late January. €8,000 ≈ 9.2 days of expenses (26,000 / 30 ≈ 867 per day).
- If the plan works: 13,000 + 10,000 collected = €23,000 ≈ 0.88 months. 13,000 + 13,000 = €26,000 = 1.0 month.

The gap is handled as an open item: it lists possible causes as "usually comes from", tells the person to ask the accountant for a reconciliation, and invites them to send the bank outflows. That counts as asking about it.
- **M4 = 0.** The danger is mentioned only in passing: "the big customer slipping further, could leave you short on payroll". It gives no amount at risk and no effect on months-to-zero.
- **M5 = 1.** Collection and terms levers: an owner-to-owner call, a dated payment, "oldest invoice paid first, or a deposit" (in effect a credit limit), chasing all overdue invoices, 30–50% deposits, enforcing terms, late-payment terms, and invoice financing as a last resort "because it costs real money". There is no "sell more".
- **M6 = 0.** There are no forecasts. It recommends that the person build a 13-week forecast, but it gives neither a nothing-changes path nor a plan-works path with numbers.
- **M7 = 0.** There is no weekly measurement list and no review date. The ageing report is pulled once.
- **M8 = 1.** About 454 words and 0 questions.

## Step 3. Forbidden items and invented facts

  - F1: it does not accept "profitable" and push growth. Reducing reliance on one customer is listed as a long-term point.
  - F2: the causes of the €14,000 are given only as possibilities ("usually comes from").
  - F3: it asks 0 questions.
  - F4: it makes no explicit promise, but see the reassurances below.
- **Invented facts:**
  1. The EU rule: "B2B suppliers can generally claim statutory interest and a fixed fee (about €40)". This rule is not in the task. It is accurate and hedged ("check the rules in your country"), so this is minor.
  2. "It's a timing problem, not a loss problem." This is a diagnosis stated as fact. It contradicts the response's own statement that "you can't tell whether the problem is only late payment or also a structural leak" while €14,000 is unexplained.

### Response 2

  - F2 is borderline: "The other €14,000 left for things profit does not show: your own withdrawals, loan repayments, equipment, tax". This states the category, which follows from the accounting identity if profit is roughly right, and the response defends it with "This holds even if profit is 10% off". It names no single cause and goes on to ask the question, so F2 is not counted.
  - F3: exactly 3 questions.
  - F4: the plan-works forecast is explicitly conditional.
- **Invented facts:**
  1. The euro-area rule: "late-payment interest and a fixed fee of at least €40 per invoice". This rule is not in the task. It is accurate and hedged ("check national rules"), so this is minor.
  - The sales figure of €29,000 is a labelled assumption, not an invented fact.

## Step 4. Criteria

### Response 1
- **C1 = 7.** Every number is correct (18k, −9k, 27k, 13k, 14k, 0.5 months, 1,500/month) and the steady-profit assumption is labelled. However, "It's a timing problem, not a loss problem" is an unsupported conclusion that contradicts the response's own point about the unexplained €14,000, and the EU rule is outside the task.
- **C2 = 6.** It finds the profit/cash contradiction and isolates the €14,000 gap well. The most dangerous event is only gestured at: no amount, no date, no effect on runway. "Looks survivable" and "timing problem" then soften the diagnosis.
- **C3 = 7.** The steps are concrete, doable by the owner and ordered ("today", "this week", "next few weeks"). The first action, the call, is fast and reversible. There are no specific dates, and the 13-week forecast is extra work handed to a person who asked for a quick answer.
- **C4 = 8.** It answers "what's going on / what this week" in about 454 words with no questions. A little jargon ("P&L", "receivables ageing report", "discretionary", "B2B") is acceptable for a business owner.
- **C5 = 3.** It has no red line or threshold, no explicit priority order left to the person, no weekly measurement list and no review date. The only control-like point is to hold discretionary spending and withdrawals.
- **C6 = 5.** It makes no promises, but there are no forecasts at all, and "The average drain… looks survivable" and "It's a timing problem, not a loss problem" are soft reassurances that the data do not support.

### Response 2
- **C1 = 8.** All the arithmetic checks: 27k, 18k, 11.6k (from a labelled assumption), 0.5 and 0.85 months, 9 days, €1,400, late January, 23k ≈ 0.9 months. The euro-area rule is outside the task, though hedged. The plan-works figure ignores continuing outflows, though it is conditioned on the €14,000 being curbed.
- **C2 = 8.** It names the contradiction, the €14,000 gap, the reserve falling from 0.85 to 0.5 months, receivables exceeding cash, and the most dangerous event with its amount and impact ("about €1,400, inside 30 days"). It does not raise the possibility that the profit figure is substantially wrong, and it gives no zero date.
- **C3 = 9.** Almost every step has an owner and a deadline ("You, by Friday", "Your accountant, by Wednesday 21 October", "You, this month"), plus dated triggers (23 October → prepayment-only; under €8,000 → call the bank and pay wages first). The fast, reversible call comes first, and conditions outside the person's control are kept separate ("It needs the customer to keep the dates").
- **C4 = 7.** It stays within budget (about 582 words, 3 questions) and in plain English. It is near the limit for a request phrased as "quick analysis", and the section structure is fairly heavy, with some awkward phrasing ("last to give up first", "working alarm").
- **C5 = 9.** The priority order is proposed and left to the person ("tell me if yours differs"; "If the customer matters more to you than cash, tell me"). The red line and working alarm come with reasons and are tested against the forecast (€8,000 crossed around end of January). It sets Monday measurements, a review date and a "what not to do now" list. Days-to-payment is the only thing missing.
- **C6 = 9.** It makes no promises and gives two numeric forecasts with ranges and conditions, plus a worst case. It states openly that the plan depends on the customer keeping its dates and on the €14,000 outflow being explained.
