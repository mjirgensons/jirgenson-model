## Step 1. Recomputed key numbers

- Turnover decline: 48,000 → 42,000 = −12.5 %; 42,000 → 37,000 = −11.9 %; 2026 run-rate 25,000 / 9 × 12 = 33,333 (−9.9 % vs 37,000). Cumulative 2023 → 2026 ≈ −30.6 % (≈ −31 %).
- Population 8,000 → 6,000 = −25 % in five years ≈ −5.6 % a year compounded (0.75^0.2 = 0.944).
- +30 % on 33,333 = 43,333, which is more than the 2024 figure (42,000), i.e. undoing roughly three years of decline in a town that is still shrinking.
- "Nothing changes" 2027: 33,333 × 0.88–0.90 = 29,300–30,000.
- Profit 6,000 / 37,000 = 16.2 % net margin; implied costs 31,000 a year ≈ 2,583 a month; the €4,000 reserve ≈ 1.5 months of all costs.
- Hours: 55 × 48 = 2,640 h → €2.27/h; 55 × 52 = 2,860 h → €2.10/h. Either way ≈ €2 an hour.
- Weekly till in 2026: 25,000 / 39 weeks ≈ €641.
- Seasonal check: if the last quarter is 20 % better, 25,000 + 8,333 × 1.2 = 35,000, still below 2025.
- Test payback at the 16 % margin: €500 / 0.16 = €3,125 of extra turnover.
- Lease: if "now" is about mid-October 2026, it ends about June 2027. That is the natural decision point. A wrong exit costs little: the €4,000 reserve stays intact, there are no stated long-term liabilities, and the profit forgone is about €400–500 a month at about €2 an hour.
- Note on the gold's "profit near zero" in the nothing-changes case: the 2027 profit level depends on the share of fixed costs, which is unknown. A constant margin gives about €4,900; with fixed rent it would be lower (about €3,500–4,500). A forecast of about €3,800–4,900 (≈ €1.3–1.9/h) is effectively near-zero pay for 55 h/week, so I accept it as consistent with the gold's intent.
