## Step 1. Recomputed key numbers

- Revenue €18,000 = 120 × €150. Costs €15,500 = €3,600 variable + €11,900 fixed. Profit €2,500/month; margin 2,500 / 18,000 = 13.9 % (≈14 %).
- How to model the variable cost: the owner says the €3,600 "vary with the number of members". The literal reading is therefore €30 per member, and that cost stays the same when the price changes. The gold key instead treats variable cost as 20 % of revenue. Under the per-member reading:
  - Price −20 % (€120), 120 members: revenue €14,400; costs 11,900 + 3,600 = €15,500. Result: **−€1,100/month**. The gold's revenue-proportional route gives −€380. Both routes give a loss, so the conclusion is the same.
  - Break-even: at €150, 11,900 / 120 = 99.2, so **100 members**. At €120, 11,900 / 90 = 132.2, so **133 members**.
  - 25 % margin at €150: 120n − 11,900 = 37.5n, so n = 144.2, i.e. **≈145 members (+25)**.
  - 25 % margin at €120: 90n − 11,900 = 30n, so n = 198.3, i.e. **≈199 members (+79, +66 %)**. The gold's route gives ≈180 (+50 %). Either way, goals 3 and 4 cannot both hold without a very large rise in members.
  - Getting back to €18,000 revenue at €120 takes **150 members**.
  - Each €1,000 of added fixed cost needs 1,000 / 120 = 8.3 extra members at €150, or 1,000 / 90 = 11.1 at €120, just to cover the cost. Keeping the 25 % margin as well needs ≈12 and ≈17.
- Tax arrears: €9,000 / €2,500 = **3.6 months of all current profit**. Over 12 months that is €750/month, which is 30 % of today's profit. The arrears are an obligation, not a goal.
- Reserve or cash position: **not given**.

I accept the per-member route (−€1,100; 199 members) as correct, and arguably more faithful to the task wording than the gold key. Neither response is penalised for differing from −€380 or 180.

## Step 6. Preference

Both responses get the arithmetic right and are within budget. Each misses one mandatory element, and the misses are different:

- **Response 1** ranks the tax arrears third instead of in the top two. In return, it explicitly ties the expansion items to the unknown cash balance and asks for that balance, along with room capacity.
- **Response 2** puts the arrears first as a non-negotiable bill, and its checks are better sequenced and timed, including the useful step of asking leavers whether price is really the reason. It does not ask for the cash position needed to judge the spending items.

For this owner this week, Response 2's order (pay down the legal obligation first, then test the price threat before cutting) is slightly more useful. The gap is small.
