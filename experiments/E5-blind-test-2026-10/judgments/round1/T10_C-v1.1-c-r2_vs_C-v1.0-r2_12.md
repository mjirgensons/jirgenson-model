# Judgment — T10, C-v1.1-c-r2 vs C-v1.0-r2 (pair 12)

- Rent shortfall: 1 100 − 450 = **€650**.

- Client debt: 3 800 / 1 100 ≈ **3.45 months of rent**. A default or a long delay is the bigger danger, not this month's rent.

## Step 3. Forbidden items and invented facts

**Response 1**
- No forbidden items. "Do not hand over new work to this client without payment" is a mild protective step and is limited by "do not switch anything off … until you have read the contract". It is not advice to stop work immediately, so it is not F4.
- Invented facts: one external legal rule stated as fact. It says that in most EU countries, late B2B payment carries statutory interest and a fixed compensation. The rule is hedged with "check this for your country". It is accurate in substance, but it is not in the task, and it assumes the person is in the EU without saying so. "21/22 October" and "22 November" come from today's date plus the task's 6 days, so they are not invented.

**Response 2**
- No forbidden items. "Do not start new work without prepayment" is about new work, not stopping work. It warns against threats rather than making them.
- Invented facts: the same EU late-payment rule, with an added figure, "no less than €40 per invoice". It is hedged with "check the rule of your country". Separately, there is an unlabelled assumption: "If the client pays at least one invoice, the line will not be needed". This assumes each invoice is at least €650, but the split of the 3 800 is unknown. The response itself asks for that split at the end.

## Step 4. Criteria

### Response 1
- **C1 = 8.** All arithmetic is correct (650; 800 → about €8; 2 450 → 1 350 → 250; consistent dates), and "only rent counted" is labelled. Two points off: the external EU rule is hedged but unlabelled, and "Only this step can bring money before the rent" overstates slightly, since the line is also money before rent.
- **C2 = 8.** It names the nearest danger (rent on 22 October, €650 short) and the bigger one (a silent client draining the cushion, red zone 22 November). It also adds a bankruptcy check. It never puts the 3 800 debt in months of rent.
- **C3 = 8.** The steps are concrete and timed: call plus written demand today, accounts and register check, reply check tomorrow at 12:00, line decision on 21 October, a fixed draw of 800. The 21 October draw date is one day before rent. That is tight, and it sits oddly next to the response's own "drawing in advance is almost free".
- **C4 = 8.** One action comes first, it is in Russian, about 279 words, with two useful questions. The paragraph on the red and yellow zones is dense and a bit technical for an emergency reply.
- **C5 = 9.** The priority order is proposed and open to correction ("my proposal, correct me"), including what to give up first. The red line is defined with a reason and tested against the forecast (yellow after October, red on 22 November). There are dated checks and a list of what not to do.
- **C6 = 9.** No promises. It gives two paths (client pays → the line stays in reserve; client stays silent → red zone on 22 November), with a caveat that taxes and other expenses bring the date closer.

### Response 2
- **C1 = 7.** The arithmetic is correct (650; 800 → about €8; 1 750 / 250), and the no-other-payments assumption is labelled. Points off for the external EU rule with its €40 figure, and for the unsupported claim that paying one invoice removes the need for the line.
- **C2 = 6.** It sees the immediate gap clearly, and correctly says it is still reversible. The red line it defines is already crossed, so it diagnoses nothing new. The client's default is not identified as the larger danger, and no date or impact is given for it.
- **C3 = 7.** The steps are doable and well timed: call the signer or accounts today, letter with a 3-day deadline, draw 800 on day 3 with a good buffer, don't draw all 2 000. Two weaknesses. The demand asks for only one invoice, which may not cover the rent. And the day-3 step "send a written demand" repeats or confuses the letter already sent today.
- **C4 = 8.** In Russian, about 288 words, one question mark, and the action comes first. The opening "call, don't write" followed by "send a letter" is a little contradictory. The invoice-split question is less useful than a question about pipeline or contract.
- **C5 = 7.** The priority order is proposed and open to correction. There is a check in 3 days with a trigger, a list of what not to do, and a cap on the draw. The red line is not tested against the forecast in a meaningful way, and the forecast's endpoint (250 left on the line at the next rent) is not tied to a date or a threshold.
- **C6 = 7.** It gives two forecasts and labels an assumption. But "the line will not be needed" if one invoice is paid is a soft promise that the person's own unknown invoice split could falsify.

## Step 6. Preference

**Response 1** is better, with confidence 0.7.

Both responses get the immediate fix right (€650 short, draw 800 at about €8 a month, protect housing). Response 1 also names the real larger danger: a silent client draining the cushion, with a red zone on 22 November and an insolvency check.
