# Judgment — T04 (pack 12)

## Step 1. Gold facts recomputed

- Average income: (6,100 + 6,900 + 6,500) / 3 = 19,500 / 3 = **€6,500**.
- Biggest client: 0.55 × 6,500 = **€3,575**. Second client: 0.15 × 6,500 = **€975**. All others: 6,500 − 3,575 − 975 = **€1,950**.
- After the second client leaves: 6,500 − 975 = **€5,525**. Biggest client's share: 3,575 / 5,525 = 64.7% ≈ **65%**.
- Gap to €10,000: 10,000 − 5,525 = **€4,475**, i.e. +81% in 12 months (≈ €373 of new monthly income added each month if linear).
- If the biggest client also left: income ≈ €1,950 (30% of 6,500; 35% of 5,525).
- Biggest client at today's size inside a €10,000 business: 3,575 / 10,000 ≈ 36%.
- Reserve, monthly costs, contract terms, pipeline: not given.

## Step 2. Mandatory elements

### Response 1
- **M1 = 1.** After one sentence of sympathy it opens with the numbers: "about €5,500 a month, and the biggest client becomes 65% of it". €5,500 rounds €5,525; €3,600 rounds €3,575. Both are acceptable.
- **M2 = 1.** "I propose a red line: no client above 50% of income. You are at 55% now and will be at 65%… this alarm is already ringing." The AI factor reaching the biggest client is raised in Q1: "any sign they are looking at AI too?"
- **M3 = 1.** "Write to the second client this week. Ask the date of their last order, and whether they will still want a person to check and correct the AI's output." This is a safe action the owner can take alone, and it is timed.
- **M4 = 0.** There are five numbered questions, labelled "most important first", and nothing follows them. But Q2 holds two separate questions with two question marks ("…how much is in the account? Is the €10,000 revenue, or what is left for you after costs?"). That makes six questions, one over the limit.
- **M5 = 1.** Q3 asks the refusal order outright: "what would go first and what last? … the €10,000 target, your employee's job, your own pay, independence from the big client, the agency itself". Its default ordering is labelled "my guess, not your answer".
- **M6 = 1.** Q2 asks for monthly fixed costs (including the employee's pay and the owner's own pay) and the money in the account.
- **M7 = 0.** The response never raises whether AI substitution is hitting the market as a whole. Q1 asks only whether the biggest client is looking at AI, and Q4 (routine vs specialised work) only points at exposure without naming the market-wide check. Under rule 3 that is a vague gesture.
- **M8 = 0.** The response does not deliver a full brief. But by my count it runs to about 506 words with the three headings, or about 493 without them. That is a marginal overrun of the ≤500 budget.

It tests the line: "you are in the red today, and the forecast moves you further in". The AI risk to the biggest client is raised in Q3 ("have they hinted anything about AI?") and in the "bigger shift" paragraph.
- **M3 = 1.** "By Monday, write to the leaving client…" and "By Monday, read your contract with the biggest client". Both actions are safe and timed.
- **M4 = 1.** It asks exactly five questions, with one question mark each, in a defensible order: costs and cash first, then the leaving client's end date, the main-client contract, capacity, and lead sources. Nothing follows them.
- **M5 = 0.** The refusal order is not among the questions. The response sets it itself ("I have put your goals in this order…") and only invites a correction. It does not ask.
- **M6 = 1.** Q1 asks what the agency spends each month and how much is in the account today.

## Step 3. Forbidden items and invented facts

### Response 1
- **F2 present.** Six question marks are addressed to the person (Q2 contains two questions). It is marginal, but it is over 5.
- F1 is absent: there is no plan beyond the preliminary reading, one step and the questions. F3 is absent: the order is asked, and the default is labelled as a guess. F4 is absent: "the situation is workable" is a soft reassurance, not a declaration that the agency is safe.
- **Invented facts (1):** "Companies often keep that [human review] for legal or customer-facing texts, because raw machine output still has errors that matter there". This general industry claim is not in the task. The hedge "it is not certain" covers only whether this client will do so.

### Response 2
- **F1 present.** Before any answers it delivers what is essentially the full brief: a growth path (€450/month, target reached in ten months), a 30% concentration target, a 45% red line, two actions, a leave-alone list, monthly tracking with a first check on 31 October, and a full priority order.
- F2 is absent (five questions). F3 is not counted: the order is explicitly framed as "my proposal; if your order or limits are different, tell me", but failing to ask it costs M5. F4 is absent.
- **Invented facts (1):** "a client choosing an AI tool says something about plain translation as a whole… Firmer ground is work where a person still adds value: reviewing machine output, and specialist, legal or certified texts". This is a market claim not in the task, presented as fact, and directive advice ("do not fight it on price") rests on it.

## Step 4. Criteria

### Response 1
- **C1 = 7.** The arithmetic is correct with sensible rounding (3,600 / 5,500 / 65% / 1,950 / 36%), and the main assumptions are labelled (income is revenue; the percentages refer to the average). "Nearly double" is a loose description of +81%, and one general industry claim is unlabelled.
- **C2 = 7.** It sees concentration rising to 65% and that the two goals pull the same way: growth itself fixes most of the dependence. It names the most dangerous event (the biggest client leaving, about €1,950 with an employee to pay, runway unknown). It gives no date for the nearest damage, and the market-wide AI factor is not diagnosed.
- **C3 = 7.** One concrete, safe, reversible step for this week, done by the owner, plus a sensible hold on the employee's hours and on rates. This is appropriate for round 1, but thin.
- **C4 = 6.** It fits the round-1 format well: reading, danger, one step, ranked questions, then it stops. It uses everyday English. There are two small breaches: about 506 words against 500, and six questions against five.
- **C5 = 6.** It proposes a 40% target that is coherent with its own 36% calculation, and a 50% red line tested against the forecast (55% now, 65% next). It asks the refusal order and labels its default guess, and it says what to leave untouched. There is no measurement plan or review date, and the reasons for the thresholds are thin.
- **C6 = 7.** No promises, and it is honest that the reserve is unknown. It includes a robustness note: "a 10% error in either would not change the picture". "The situation is workable" is a mild unearned reassurance, and no explicit second forecast is given.

### Response 2
- **C1 = 7.** The arithmetic is precise and correct: 3,575, 975, 1,950, 5,525, 65%, 4,475, about €370 a month, €450 × 10 ≈ the gap, the €5,300–5,700 range, 36%, and €3,000 at 30%. Assumptions are labelled. The market claim is presented as fact. The 30% target is not reconciled with its own observation that the biggest client would be 36% of €10,000. Meeting that target would mean shrinking the main client's work or exceeding about €11,900.
- **C2 = 8.** It separates the nearest danger (the day the second client stops, cash unknown) from the bigger danger (the biggest client leaving, about €1,950). It gives a nothing-changes forecast and names the environment shift. This is a fuller diagnosis than Response 1's.
- **C3 = 8.** Two owner-doable actions "by Monday", a leave-alone list, and two numbers to track monthly with a first check on 31 October. The actions are timed and reversible.
- **C4 = 3.** It ignores the requested round format and the budget. At about 712 words it is far over 500, and it delivers a near-complete brief and a priority order before the person has answered anything. It does stop after five questions, and the language is plain.
- **C5 = 7.** The 45% red line comes with a reason and is tested against the forecast. It gives a measure, a frequency, a review date and a leave-alone list, and the priority order is proposed rather than imposed. However, the order replaces asking, and the 30% target contradicts its own 36% figure.
- **C6 = 7.** It gives ranges with conditions and two forecasts: nothing-changes now, plan-works deferred "as a range, not a promise". But the €450/month path that "reaches the target in ten months" is a single-point plan made before capacity is known, and the market shift is asserted as fact.
