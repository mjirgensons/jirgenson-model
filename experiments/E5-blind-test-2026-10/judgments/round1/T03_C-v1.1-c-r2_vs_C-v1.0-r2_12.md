## Step 1. Recomputed key numbers

- Revenue €18,000 (120 × €150); costs €15,500; profit €2,500/month; margin 2,500 / 18,000 = **13.9 %**. Fixed €11,900, variable €3,600 (= €30 per member = 20 % of revenue at today's price). Annual profit €30,000.
- Two readings of the variable cost after a price cut are possible:
  - (a) Gold reading, variable = 20 % of revenue: revenue 14,400, profit 14,400 − 11,900 − 2,880 = **−€380/month**; 25 % margin needs 11,900 / 0.55 ≈ €21,636 ≈ **180 members (+50 %)**.
  - (b) Per-member reading, variable = €30 per member. This is closer to the owner's words ("vary with the number of members"): contribution at €120 is €90. Profit at 120 members = 120×90 − 11,900 = **−€1,100/month**. Break-even 11,900/90 = 132.2 → **133**. To get today's €2,500 back: 14,400/90 = **160 (+33 %)**. For a 25 % margin: 90n − 11,900 ≥ 30n → n ≥ 198.3 → **199 (+66 %)**. Revenue back to €18,000 at **150 members**, where profit = 13,500 − 11,900 = **€1,600 (8.9 %)**.
  - Both readings show the same thing: the cut makes a loss at today's membership, and the 25 % margin then needs about 50–66 % more members. Both responses use reading (b) correctly, which the procedure allows as a different but correct route.
- At €150, a 25 % margin needs 120n − 11,900 ≥ 37.5n → n ≥ 144.2 → **145 members**, or €2,000/month less cost at 120 members (profit 4,500 vs 2,500).
- The price cut gives away 120 × €30 = **€3,600/month = €43,200/year**, more than the current €30,000/year profit.
- Each extra €1,000/month of fixed cost needs 1,000/120 = 8.3 (→9) members at €150, or 1,000/90 = 11.1 (→12) at €120.
- Tax arrears €9,000 = **3.6 months** of all profit = €750/month over 12 months = **30 %** of current profit. This is an obligation, not a goal.
- Sensitivity: if total costs are 10 % lower (€13,950), profit at €120 × 120 = €450, which is near zero. If the variable/fixed split is off by 10 % (variable 3,240–3,960), break-even at €120 is 131.8–132.6 members, a shift of less than one member.
