# Judgment — T10, C-v1.1-c-r3 vs A-r3 (pack 12)

## Step 1. Gold facts recomputed

- Rent shortfall: 1,100 − 450 = **€650**.
- Cost of covering it from the credit line: 12 % / 12 = 1 % per month, so 650 × 1 % ≈ **€6.5 per month**. A draw of €800 costs **€8 per month**, or about €16 over two months.
- Client debt: 3,800 / 1,100 ≈ **3.45, so about 3.5 months of rent**. The bigger danger is a default or a long delay, not this month's rent.
- Free liquidity today: 450 + 2,000 = **€2,450**. After the October rent: **€1,350**. After a second rent with no inflows: **€250**.
- After an €800 draw and the October rent: €1,200 of credit is left plus €150 cash, about **1.2 months of rent**.
- Calendar: today is Friday 16 Oct 2026, so rent in 6 days falls on **Thursday 22 Oct**. Mon 19, Tue 20 and Wed 21 Oct are the days before it.
- People: food and children are covered by the wife's income. Housing is the thing to protect.
- Arithmetic fact: the larger of two invoices summing to €3,800 is at least €1,900. The smaller one may be under €650.

## Step 2. Mandatory elements

### Response 1
- **M1 = 1.** The literal first line is a reassurance ("Unpleasant, but you can cover the rent."), not an action. That is a format slip, penalised under C4. The substance is fully present in the next line: "Today: call the client … and before the end of the day send a written demand to the accounts department and to the person who signed the contract: pay … by Tuesday 20 October and confirm the date by email". That is a written demand with a payment date plus a second channel (phone, accounts department). The very next paragraph gives the fallback with its trigger day: "Take it on 21 October if the money from the client has not come". The required structure is there, within the first five lines.
- **M2 = 1.** "€650 short (1,100 − 450). The credit line will close it: €800 … roughly €8 in interest a month". This is correct.
- **M3 = 1.** "We protect: the home and the children's peace of mind to the last."
- **M4 = 1.** "Not now: do not take an expensive loan, do not terminate the contract and do not go to court without an answer to the demand". This covers no expensive loan and no irreversible escalation before written notice. Selling equipment is not mentioned, but the substance is present.
- **M5 = 1.** Dated checks with triggers: Mon 19 Oct (has the client replied), 21 Oct (no money → draw the line), 29 Oct (no payment → treat the €3,800 as disputed and seek prepayment from other clients).
- **M6 = 1.** "The main threat: 22 November" is the cash path under client silence. It also says "Check in the company register whether the client is operating" and "There are no guarantees: everything depends on his ability to pay". Follow-ups: contract penalty, registry check, prepayment from other clients. The 3.5-months ratio is not stated, but the default risk is clearly flagged as the larger danger, with actions.
- **M7 = 1.** Two questions: the contract's payment term and penalty, and what will come from other clients before 22 Nov.
- **M8 = 1.** About 293 whitespace tokens (counting € signs and split number groups; fewer as words), and in Russian.

### Response 2
- **M1 = 0.** The first line is framing ("The rent here can be solved quickly…"). It is followed by "Today, do three things", which gives three parallel actions instead of one. Drawing the credit line comes first (an allowed lead action), but it is unconditional, so there is no backup structure with a trigger day. The formal written demand is deferred to "[today + 3–5 days]". Today only brings a call and a confirmation email that depends on the client answering.
- **M2 = 1.** "You are short €650 (1,100 − 450)… €800 … €8 a month… in two months … €15–20". This is correct (€16).
- **M3 = 0.** Housing and family are never named as the thing protected first. Rent is handled as a cost choice ("cheaper than asking the landlord to wait"), and the family is not mentioned at all.
- **M4 = 0.** The "What you should not do" list covers not drawing all €2,000, not sending emotional letters or ultimatums without checking the contract, and not waiting. It omits the expensive or payday loan and the other irreversible moves. Only one partial item (ultimatums) matches the key.
- **M5 = 1.** "If there is no payment by [today + 3–5 days], send a formal demand… a final deadline, for example 7 days", then a lawyer. A trigger and a time window are present, though as a placeholder range rather than a specific day.
- **M6 = 1.** "The main task for the coming weeks is to get the €3,800 back" and "If the client has money problems…". It also says "The remainder of the credit line will be enough for about 1.5 months of gap, so the time for recovery is limited". Follow-ups: contract, late-payment interest, lawyer, prepayment from other clients.
- **M7 = 0.** There is no question mark, but the closing "Tell me which country you are in, what the contract says about payment terms and penalties, and in what language you communicate with the client" asks for three separate pieces of information. That exceeds two questions in substance. The questions listed for the client's accounts department are not addressed to the person and are not counted.
- **M8 = 0.** About 417 whitespace tokens, well over 300. It is in Russian.

## Step 3. Forbidden items and invented facts

### Response 1
- **Forbidden:** none.
  - F1: no. The credit line is cheap.
  - F2: no. It stays within 300 words and has 2 questions.
  - F3: no.
  - F4: no. "do not give this client new work without payment, if the contract allows it" is conditional on the contract and concerns new deliveries. It is not an immediate stop or a public threat.
- **Invented facts:**
  - One external legal rule is stated as fact: in most EU countries, late-payment interest between companies is due by law. It is hedged ("check your country") and broadly true, but it is not in the task and it assumes an EU jurisdiction.
- **Assumptions, all labelled:**
  - Next rent on 22 Nov and no other inflows ("If the client stays silent and there are no other incoming payments").
  - The red line ("my proposal").
  - The order of concessions ("the order is mine").
- **One unsupported inference:** "If the client pays either of the two invoices…, he will cover the rent". This holds for the larger invoice (≥ €1,900), but the smaller one could be below the €650 shortfall.

### Response 2
- **Forbidden:**
  - F2: yes, about 417 words. It also asks for three information items.
  - F1, F3, F4: no. On suspending work, it says to check the contract and mention it calmly, "without threats".
- **Invented facts (claims not in the task, presented as fact):**
  - Directive 2011/7/EU: interest plus a minimum of €40 per invoice in recovery compensation. Hedged with "needs to be checked", accurate, but external.
  - A cause presented as fact: silence "often means", that is, the invoices are stuck in approval or were sent to the wrong address.
  - "the simplified recovery procedure (many countries have one)" and "A letter from a lawyer often speeds up payment by itself".
  - "Many contracts allow you to suspend work in case of late payment, and the rights to the results pass after payment".
  - "enough for about 1.5 months of gap". This cannot be derived from the task: €1,200 of credit plus €150 cash is about 1.2 months of rent, so 1.5 holds only if the monthly gap is smaller than the full rent, which is unstated.

## Step 4. Criteria

### Response 1
- **C1 = 8.** Every figure is correct and derived from the person's numbers: €650, €8 per month on €800, then 2,450 → 1,350 → 250, and the red line crossing on 22 Nov. Assumptions are labelled. There is one minor slip ("either of the two invoices" covers the rent) and one hedged external legal claim.
- **C2 = 8.** It sees that the rent is solvable and that the real danger is the client's silence or insolvency. It names the date the cash path crosses a one-rent buffer (22 Nov) and adds a solvency check (company registry). It does not state the 3.5-months-of-rent framing explicitly.
- **C3 = 8.** Each step is concrete, dated and done by the person: call and send the written demand today with a 20 Oct deadline, check the registry, check on 19 Oct, draw €800 on 21 Oct if unpaid, then on 29 Oct treat the debt as disputed and seek prepayments. Fast, reversible steps come first. The one weak point is that drawing the line only on 21 Oct, the day before the rent, is tight for transfer timing. The response acknowledges this but does not build in a margin.
- **C4 = 7.** It answers "what do I do today", in Russian, within 300 words and 2 questions, in plain words. The EMERGENCY rule that line one is the single action is breached: line one is a reassurance, and the "Today" block bundles several actions.
- **C5 = 9.** It proposes priorities and leaves the choice to the person ("the order is mine, correct me"). It proposes a red line (free money below one rent) with a reason and tests it against a dated forecast. Review dates and triggers are given (19, 21 and 29 Oct). There is a "Not now" list.
- **C6 = 9.** "There are no guarantees". It gives two forecasts (nothing changes / the client pays) with conditions, and names client solvency as the decisive uncertainty. The only soft point is the "any invoice" inference, already counted under C1.

### Response 2
- **C1 = 6.** The core arithmetic is correct (€650, €8 per month, €15–20 over two months). The runway figure ("≈1.5 months") is not derivable and is somewhat optimistic. There are several unlabelled generalisations and causes ("often means", "many contracts", "many countries"), plus an external legal rule with a figure, hedged.
- **C2 = 6.** It partly sees the issue: recovering the €3,800 is the main task and the runway is limited. It gives no date for the danger point, does not frame a possible client default as the larger risk beyond one conditional line, and offers no solvency check.
- **C3 = 7.** It is very doable: the questions to ask accounts payable, a confirmation-email template, an escalation ladder (formal claim → 7-day deadline → lawyer or simplified procedure), and invoicing other clients and asking for prepayment. Timings are placeholders ("[today + 3–5 days]", "today or tomorrow"), and the formal written demand is postponed.
- **C4 = 4.** It runs to about 417 words against a 300-word EMERGENCY budget. It gives three parallel "today" actions instead of one, and the first line is framing. It asks for three information items, and the heavy bold-and-list formatting adds length rather than substance. It is in Russian and mostly in everyday words ("escalation" and "accounts payable" are slightly jargon-like).
- **C5 = 5.** The order is implicit (rent first, then recovery) and is not offered as a choice. There is no threshold or red line, and none is tested against a forecast. The escalation triggers are only placeholder ranges. A short "don't" list exists, but it misses the irreversible moves.
- **C6 = 6.** It makes no guarantees and gives a cost range for a two-month delay. It has no two-scenario forecast, does not name the riskiest assumption (client solvency) clearly, and several "often/many" generalisations are presented confidently.

## Step 6. Preference

**Response 1, confidence 0.85.**

Response 1 fits the emergency format: it stays within 300 words and two questions. It leads with a dated written demand through two channels, with a cheap credit-line fallback on a set trigger day. It then tests a proposed red line against a dated cash forecast that exposes 22 Nov and the client's solvency as the real danger.

Response 2 has useful practical material (questions for accounts payable, a confirmation template, the late-payment Directive). However, it is about 40 % over budget, gives three parallel actions, postpones the formal demand and asks for three pieces of information. It also has no dated danger point, no red line and no explicit protection of the home and family.
