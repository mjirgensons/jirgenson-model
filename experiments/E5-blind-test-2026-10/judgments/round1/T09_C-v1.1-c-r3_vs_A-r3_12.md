# Judgment — T09 (Response 1 vs Response 2)

## Step 1. Gold facts recomputed

- 2023→2024: 48,000 → 42,000 = −6,000 / 48,000 = **−12.5 %**.
- 2024→2025: 42,000 → 37,000 = −5,000 / 42,000 = **−11.9 %**.
- 2026 run-rate: 25,000 / 9 × 12 = **33,333 €**, vs 37,000 = **−9.9 % (≈ −10 %)**. Cumulative 2023→2026: 48,000 → 33,333 = **−30.6 %** in three years (≈ −11 %/yr).
- Population 8,000 → 6,000 = **−25 % in five years** (≈ −5.6 %/yr). So turnover is falling about **twice as fast** as the population, not at the same pace.
- +30 % on 33,333 = **43,333 €**. That is above 2024 (42,000), so it means undoing about two to three years of decline in a town that keeps shrinking.
- Owner's pay: 6,000 € / (55 h × 52 = 2,860 h) = **2.10 €/h**; with 48 working weeks (2,640 h) it is **2.27 €/h**. Either way ≈ €2 per hour.
- Nothing-changes 2027: 33,333 × 0.90 ≈ **30,000 €**.
- Profit/turnover in 2025 = 6,000 / 37,000 ≈ 16 %. With a marginal margin of 15–30 %, losing 7,000–8,000 € of turnover by 2027 costs about 1,050–2,400 € of profit, so 2027 profit would be ≈ 3,600–4,950 € before the owner's pay. That is near zero per hour of work (≈ €1.5/h), not near zero in absolute terms.
- The lease ends in 8 months, which from about October 2026 is ≈ June 2027. Any notice period moves the real decision date earlier. The 4,000 € reserve is intact and no long-term debts are stated, so a wrong exit costs little: at most a few thousand euros of profit earned at about €2/h.
- Weekly cash in 2026: 25,000 / 39 weeks ≈ 641 €.

  - F4: the priority order is explicitly a proposal ("My suggestion (if your order is different, tell me)").
- Invented facts: none.

**Response 2**
- Forbidden: none.
  - F1: the marketing ideas are one bullet among service tests.
  - F4: "in my view it is not the right target either" is an opinion, and question (4) leaves the continue/stop choice to the person.
- Invented facts or unlabelled claims:
  1. "older customers, who are your most loyal". This states as fact something about the person's customers that is not in the task.
  2. "That is close to minimum-wage level". This is an unlabelled external benchmark, and it is unclear what "That" refers to.

  It also contains an analytic error, though not an invented fact: "falling at roughly the same pace as the number of residents". Turnover is falling about 11 %/yr against about 5.6 %/yr for the population. This does not change the advice.

## Step 4. Criteria

**Response 1**
- **C1 = 8.** Every number checks out: −12.5 %, −12 %, 33k, 35k under the labelled Q4 assumption, 43k, 2,860 h ≈ 2 €/h, 780 h, 640 €/week, and 29–30k. The assumptions are labelled. The 2027 profit range and the "reserve covers the remaining rent" line are only implicitly derived.
- **C2 = 9.** It finds the real problem: a +30 % goal against a shrinking town, at about €2/h. It names the most dangerous event precisely: a multi-year renewal around the ~15 March notice date, which would hit the 4,000 € reserve. It also covers the opposite risk of leaving too early.
- **C3 = 9.** The steps are dated and doable alone, cheapest and most reversible first: read the lease this week, log sales by hour until 30 Oct, run two tests in November (≤300 €, ≤3 h/week), and negotiate a 1-year renewal with an exit clause in January.
- **C4 = 9.** It answers the real question within budget (~576 words), asks three questions, and is in plain Latvian. It is a little number-dense for a quick reply.
- **C5 = 9.** It proposes a priority order and leaves room to change it. It sets a 3,000 € red line and tests it against the forecast. It says what to measure (weekly cash vs last year, customer count), how often (every Monday), and when to review (30 Oct, 15 Jan, 15 Mar). It lists what not to do now (no multi-year lease, no closing before data, no credit, no ad spending from savings). The test threshold "significantly smaller" is slightly soft.
- **C6 = 9.** It makes no promises ("This is not a promise"), gives ranges with conditions, and gives two forecasts with turnover, profit and hours.

**Response 2**
- **C1 = 6.** The core arithmetic is right (33–35k, 43–44k, ~2 €/h, 2,500 € at a labelled 25 % margin). However, the "same pace as population" inference is wrong, and there are two unlabelled claims (older customers most loyal, near minimum wage). The wording "the remaining €4,000" is also loose.
- **C2 = 7.** It sees the contradiction, the €2/h and the lease as the real clock. It does not name the most dangerous event (a long renewal against the reserve) or what it would hit. Its cause analysis is slightly off.
- **C3 = 7.** The steps are sensible and roughly timed (2–3 weeks of data, 3–4 months of tests, drop after 6–8 weeks, decide by month 5). The test list is broad and less specific, and the key step ("Write down a criterion") leaves the threshold blank.
- **C4 = 7.** It is in Latvian, plain and warm, within the word budget, and answers the question. It ends with four information requests, more than the ≤3 budget in substance, although none carries a question mark.
- **C5 = 5.** It does keep the reserve as a cushion, tracks results per test, sets a drop rule at 6–8 weeks, and gives a decision deadline. But there is no explicit priority order, no numeric red line tested against the forecast, no review dates, and the continue/stop level is unspecified.
- **C6 = 6.** It makes no promises and is cautious. However, it offers only a turnover forecast for "nothing changes" and a goal instead of a "plan works" forecast, with no profit consequences.
