# Judgment — T09 (Newspaper kiosk in a small town), Response 1 vs Response 2

## Step 1. Recomputed key numbers

- Year-on-year change: 48,000 → 42,000 = −12.5 %; 42,000 → 37,000 = −11.9 %.
- 2026 run-rate: 25,000 / 9 × 12 = 33,333 (≈ −9.9 % vs 2025). Cumulative 2023 → 2026 ≈ −30.6 %.
- Population: 8,000 → 6,000 = −25 % over five years (≈ −5.6 % a year), so turnover is falling faster than the population.
- +30 % on 33,333 = 43,333 (more than 2024's 42,000). On 34,000 it is 44,200.
- Nothing changes 2027: 33,333 × 0.9 ≈ 30,000.
- Hours: 55 × 48 = 2,640 h, or 55 × 52 = 2,860 h. Profit/hour: 6,000 / 2,640 ≈ €2.27, or 6,000 / 2,860 ≈ €2.10. So about €2 per hour, and about €500 a month.
- 2025 margin: 6,000 / 37,000 = 16.2 %. 2025 costs: 31,000, about €2,583 a month.
- 2026 profit if all costs are fixed: 33,333 − 31,000 ≈ 2,300. If all costs are variable: 16.2 % × 33,333 ≈ 5,400.
- 2027 nothing changes, all costs fixed: 30,000 − 31,000 ≈ −1,000 (at 29,000 it is ≈ −2,000).
- Reserve: 4,000 / 2,583 ≈ 1.5 months of costs.
- Lease ends 8 months from mid-October 2026, around mid-June 2027. A 31 March decision leaves about 2.5 months.
- A 5 % discount at a 16 % margin uses up 5/16 ≈ 31 % of profit, about a third.

Exit options are present: the €4,000 "is a safety cushion for the exit", and "closing the kiosk is a decision, not a failure: about €2 an hour…". The cheap test costs €300–500, and continuing depends on thresholds. The owner's time is valued at about €2/h.
- **M6 = 0.** "Nothing changes" gives turnover only (30–31k in 2027) and no profit figure. "Plan works" is a target ("hold on to about €34 thousand") rather than a labelled forecast range for the new format or niche. This is a partial gesture only.
- **M7 = 0.** The continue/stop rule is a template ("at least €X … at least €Y"), and no threshold is filled in. There is one question mark, but the closing list asks for four more items (product groups, rent and fixed costs, what else is sold, whether to keep the kiosk). That makes about five asks, which is over the ≤3 budget in substance. It never asks what has been tried or what is growing in the town.
- **M8 = 1.** About 535 words.

- **M3 = 1.** Turnover −31 % against population −25 %. "the town will not get back the residents who have left". "+30% … with marketing alone is not achievable". It also checks that the conclusion still holds at 35k.

## Step 4. Criteria

### Response 1
- **C1 = 7.** All arithmetic is correct: about €2/h, a 16 % margin, a discount eating about a third of profit, +2–3k at a 16–25 % margin, and 44k > 2024. The 25 % is labelled optimistic. There is one unlabelled general claim about advertising, a loose population comparison, and a slightly wide top of the 2026 range.
- **C2 = 7.** It sees the real problem: no buyers, €2/h, and +30 % would not fix it even if reached. It treats the lease as the decision point. It does not spell out the most dangerous event (locking into a multi-year lease, or the reserve running out) or what that would hit.
- **C3 = 7.** The steps are ordered and owned by the person, with time windows (two weeks, Nov–Dec, Jan–Feb, by March). The idea of cutting quiet hours is useful. There is no "today" action, the lease step has no date, and the X/Y thresholds leave work undone.
- **C4 = 7.** Language, register and length (about 535 words) fit, and it reframes the request well. The soft breach is the question budget: one question plus a four-item information request is more than the ≤3 allowed.
- **C5 = 6.** Leaving Y to the owner is good, and "leave untouched" is covered (no renovations, reserve not for ads). There is no numeric red line, nothing tested against a forecast, no measurement cadence and no review date beyond "by March".
- **C6 = 7.** No promises, and it is hedged with "most likely" and ranges. The two-forecast structure is incomplete: there is no 2027 profit figure for "nothing changes" and no labelled "plan works" range for a new format.

### Response 2
- **C1 = 8.** Every derived number checks out:
  - 31 % cumulative decline and 43k > 2024;
  - about 2,900 h and €2/h;
  - 2026 profit 2–5k by cost behaviour, and monthly costs of €2,600;
  - 1.5 months of reserve;
  - a worst case of about −2k that crosses the line around autumn;
  - mid-June expiry, and 31 March leaving 2.5 months.

  Assumptions are labelled ("I do not know", "my assumption", "I suggest"). The minor issues are the population timeframe and the "landlords usually give way" generalisation.
- **C2 = 9.** It names the central issue (a shrinking market makes +30 % unattainable) and the nearest irreversible damage precisely: a multi-year renewal or a silent auto-renewal at current rent. It gives a date and shows what that would hit (profit of 2–5k, the reserve crossing the line).
- **C3 = 9.** Each step has an owner and a date: read the lease today (notice period, auto-renewal), get product-group data by 23 October, one 4–6-week test in Nov–Dec, a written landlord request by 31 December, then weekly and monthly checks. The fast, reversible steps come first. The landlord's answer is kept separate as a condition. The tests are not costed, and it does not suggest cutting hours.
- **C4 = 8.** Language, register and length (about 584 words, inside 600) fit, with three asks across two question marks. It is dense with numbers and somewhat compressed ("you are at 1.5"), which is a bit heavy for a QUICK reply but still in plain words.
- **C5 = 9.** All the parts are there and fit together:
  - the priority order is proposed, not imposed;
  - the €2,600 red line has a reason and is tested against the worst-case forecast;
  - the measures are weekly and monthly, with a next review on 16 November;
  - "what not to do" is stated (no multi-year lease, ads capped).

  The one gap is a turnover or test-result threshold for continuing.
- **C6 = 9.** It gives two labelled forecasts with ranges and conditions, plus "This is not a promise". The profit range follows from a stated uncertainty, and the landlord claim is caveated. The opening "not achievable" is slightly categorical.

## Step 6. Preference

**Response 2 is better (confidence 0.75).** Response 2 treats the lease as a dated threat and gives the owner a "today" action: check the notice period and auto-renewal. Its reserve red line is tested against a worst-case forecast, it gives both forecasts with numbers, and it sets review dates and stop rules. That is everything this owner needs to decide before the lease runs out. Response 1 has sharp points (the €2/h framing, the discount arithmetic, cutting quiet hours), but its thresholds are left as X/Y, its forecasts are incomplete and it asks for more information than the budget allows.
