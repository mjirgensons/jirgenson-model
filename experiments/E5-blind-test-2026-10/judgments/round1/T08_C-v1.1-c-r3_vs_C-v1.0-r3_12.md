## Step 3. Forbidden items and invented facts

**Response 1**
- Forbidden: none. It does not push growth. The causes of the €14,000 are given as "usual suspects", not as fact. It asks 2 questions. The forecasts are conditional.
- Invented facts: one, the EU late-payment rule ("late-payment interest plus a fixed fee per invoice (at least €40 under EU rules)"). It is accurate and softened with "check yours", but it is not in the task. The note that loan capital is not a cost is a definition used to explain a possible cause, not a fact about this business, so it is not counted.
- Minor error: "near zero if ... they stop paying" over six months. Full non-payment of about €11,600/month would bring zero within roughly 1–2 months, so this understates the danger. The advice does not change.

**Response 2**
- Forbidden: none. The €14,000 causes are given with "perhaps". It asks 2 questions. It says "Neither is a promise."
- Invented facts: one, the same EU rule ("EU rules normally give you late-payment interest and at least €40 per invoice"). It is accurate and softened with "normally" and "If your invoices say 30 days".
- Minor error: "€26,000 by the end of April (about three months of cushion)". €26,000 is one month of expenses, as the response itself says in its red line. This is an internal contradiction. It does not change the advice.

## Step 4. Criteria

**Response 1**
- C1 = 8. Every core number is correct, and the assumptions are labelled ("I am assuming most of it is your biggest customer", the 10% sensitivity check). Points off for the external EU rule and for understating the effect of the customer stopping payment.
- C2 = 7. It finds the real problem (cash tied in receivables plus a €14,000 non-cost outflow) and names default by the 40% customer as the danger, with amounts. The danger has no date or months-to-zero, and the default arithmetic ("you reach zero") is loose.
- C3 = 8. Steps have owners and deadlines (you by 23 Oct, you or the bookkeeper by 20 Oct, the accountant by 23 Oct, the bank now), and there is a concrete €6,500 collection ask. Points off: the steps are not ordered today / this week / this month, the deposit step has no date, and the profit-to-bank reconciliation is handed to the accountant instead of something the owner can do at once.
- C4 = 8. It answers both "what's going on" and "what do I do this week", in plain English, within 600 words and with 2 questions. It is close to the word limit for a "quick" request, and "bridge from profit to bank balance" is a little technical.
- C5 = 8. It proposes a priority order, leaves the choice to the owner ("yours to change") and spells out the trade-off of ranking the customer above the cushion. It has a red line with a reason, a weekly check, a review date and triggers, and a list of what to hold off on. Missing: days to pay. The €10,000 trigger has no stated reason.
- C6 = 8. Two forecasts with conditions and ranges. The plan assumes only €9,000 of the €13,000 comes in (a deliberate margin). No promises. It does not state outright that the forecasts are not guarantees, but it never overclaims.

**Response 2**
- C1 = 7. The forecast ranges, the dates and the €14,000 are all correct, and the assumptions are named at the end with a 10% sensitivity check. Points off for the "about three months of cushion" contradiction and the external EU rule.
- C2 = 8. It finds the central issue and the €14,000 gap. - **Response 1:** H1 true (≈586 ≤ 600). H2 true (2). H3 true (English). H4 true (no internal labels). H5 true (not a family task, and the vocabulary is plain). H6 true. The €4,000 and €7,000 outcomes are explicitly different conditions, not the same quantity.

## Step 6. Preference

Response 2 serves the owner slightly better this week. Its actions are ordered by time and the owner can do them all himself, including finding the €14,000 from his own bank statements. Its danger and thresholds are dated and tested against the forecast, and it tracks days to pay, which is the metric that matters for a late payer. Response 1 is close behind and has real strengths: an explicit −1,500/month burn rate, a concrete €6,500 collection ask, a thoughtful deposit-versus-relationship trade-off and a direct mention of default. It loses on undated danger, a looser time order and a missing days-to-pay measure. Response 2's "three months of cushion" slip is a visible but minor flaw.
