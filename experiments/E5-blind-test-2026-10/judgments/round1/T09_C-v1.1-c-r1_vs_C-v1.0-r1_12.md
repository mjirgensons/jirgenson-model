# Judgment — T09 (Newspaper kiosk in a small town), Response 1 vs Response 2

## Step 1. Recomputed key numbers

- Turnover decline: 48,000 → 42,000 = −12.5 %; 42,000 → 37,000 = −11.9 %. 2026 run-rate 25,000 / 9 × 12 = 33,333 (−9.9 % vs 2025). Cumulative 48,000 → 33,333 = −31 % (about a third). Population 8,000 → 6,000 = −25 %.
- +30 % on 33,333 = 43,333 (above 2024's 42,000). If 2026 ended at 36,000 (strong Q4), +30 % = 46,800.
- Owner's hourly income: 6,000 / (55 × 48 = 2,640 h) ≈ €2.27/h; with 52 weeks (2,860 h) ≈ €2.10/h; ≈ €500/month.
- 2025 expenses implied: 37,000 − 6,000 = 31,000 (≈ €2,583/month). Reserve 4,000 ≈ 1.55 months of expenses, or 8 months of the owner's €500/month income.
- 2026 profit: if costs fixed ≈ 33,333 − 31,000 = 2,333; if costs proportional (16.2 % margin) ≈ 5,400.
- 2027, nothing changes (−8…−13 %): turnover ≈ 29,000–30,700. Profit: fixed costs ≈ −2,000…−300 (near zero or a loss); proportional costs ≈ 4,700–5,000.
- Lease expires 8 months from mid-October 2026 → mid-June 2027. The cost of a wrong exit is low: the €4,000 reserve, no stated long-term liabilities, and the kiosk pays the owner about €2/h.

Exit appears only as "start preparing an exit" under the red line, so the cost-of-exit weighing is missing in substance.

Exactly 3 questions, all decision-relevant (rent and extension terms, margin by product line, Q4 2025 turnover). Priorities are put to the owner ("If your order is different, tell me").

## Step 3. Forbidden items and invented facts

**Response 1**
- Forbidden: none. The 8-week test (≤ €200, reserved papers, a post in a local group, a poster) is a small capped experiment, not the main answer, so F1 does not apply. No F3: it does not declare the kiosk dead. No F4: priorities are proposed ("my suggestion").
- Invented or unlabelled claims:
  - "many leases require 1–3 months' notice", a general claim about notice periods (hedged, but presented as fact).
  - "a spot that is hard to get back", an unsupported claim that the location is hard to regain.
  - "Where there are fewer residents, it is usually not easier for the landlord to find another tenant", a general claim about the landlord's market.

**Response 2**
- Forbidden: none. The ad cap of €300 is a limit, not a plan. Priorities are proposed ("I suggest this order").
- Invented or wrong facts:
  - "more than in 2024, when the factory was still working" contradicts the task, which says the factory closed in 2023. The point is rhetorical and the advice does not depend on it.
  - "considerably below the minimum wage" is an external fact not in the task. It is true for Latvia (€500/month for 55 h/week is far below the full-time minimum wage).

## Step 4. Criteria

**Response 1**
- C1 = 4. Most of the arithmetic is right: 33,000; 10–12 %; 43,000/47,000; €2/h; 8 months of reserve; 2,500 × 12 = 30,000. The exception is the nothing-changes profit: "profit about €4,000–5,000 (if costs do not fall along with turnover)". With costs that do not fall, 29,000–30,000 against 31,000 of costs is a loss of about 1,000–2,000. So the stated condition is inverted and the forecast that matters most for the lease decision looks rosier than it is. Add the "you lose €6,000 a year" exit framing and three unlabelled general claims.
- C2 = 7. It sees the town as the driver and the lease end (mid-June 2027) as the danger, including what a long lease would hit (fixed rent against falling turnover) and the notice-period trap. But it frames leaving as the costly mistake, which partly misses the key insight that the wrong-exit cost is low.
- C3 = 8. Steps are dated and doable by the owner: this week log hourly turnover for 4 weeks, then drop weak hours; pull monthly turnover since 2023 for the lease talk; by December an 8-week test capped at €200 and 3 h/week, plus evaluating parcel pick-up or bill payments; in January ask for a 6–12 month or turnover-linked lease. Fast, reversible steps come first.
- C4 = 8. Answers the real question, in Latvian and plain words, with 3 questions, just under the word budget.
- C5 = 8. All parts are present and coherent. Priorities are proposed and left to the owner. The red line (€2,500/month, two months running) comes with a reason and is tested against the forecast ("reaches this line close to the end of the lease"). It sets weekly and monthly measures and a review on 16 November, and says what not to do. A raw monthly turnover threshold is exposed to seasonal false alarms.
- C6 = 7. No promises ("a forecast is not a promise"; +30 % "I do not consider … a credible outcome"), and it gives ranges and two forecasts. But the nothing-changes profit range is misleadingly optimistic under its own stated condition.

**Response 2**
- C1 = 6. The arithmetic is correct and richer: expenses 31,000; €2.10/h; reserve ≈ 1.5 months of expenses; red line 2,600 = one month; 1,400 headroom; 2026 profit 2,000–5,400 depending on cost behaviour (computed 2,333–5,405); 2027 loss 500–2,000 with expenses held at 31,000 (labelled). Assumptions are labelled. One factual misreading (the factory "still working" in 2024) and one unlabelled external fact (minimum wage). Neither changes the advice.
- C2 = 8. It names town shrinkage as the strongest force, and the lease end (~June 2027) as the biggest threat, with what it hits: fixed costs against falling turnover, and a rent rise wiping out profit. It shows the owner earns far below minimum wage and the nothing-changes path ends in a loss. It identifies the cost structure (fixed vs variable) as decisive and builds a diagnostic for lost customers vs smaller baskets.
- C3 = 8. Every step names the owner ("(you)") and a date: receipts by hour for two weeks; ask the landlord about terms by 31 October; ask 10 regulars what they now buy elsewhere and add one or two items by 15 November; check whether couriers pay for a parcel point; drop unprofitable hours by 1 February; ad spend capped. The steps are cheap and reversible.
- C4 = 8. Answers the real question, in Latvian and plain words, with 3 questions, within budget.
- C5 = 9. Priorities are proposed and left to the owner, with an offer to recompute. The reserve red line comes with a reason and is tested against the worst-case forecast (it is breached). It measures receipts, monthly turnover against the same month last year (which handles seasonality) and savings weekly. First review is 2 November, the lease decision is due 31 March with explicit conditions, and it lists clear don'ts (no long lease before March, no credit, no spending below €2,600).
- C6 = 9. "This is an estimate, not a promise." It gives ranges with conditions (cost behaviour; new goods or the parcel point actually bringing customers; the rent not rising), two forecasts, and names the key uncertainty (fixed vs variable costs) as a question.

  - H1: about 590 tokens, which is ≤ 600.
  - H2: 3 question marks.

  - H6: numbers are consistent within the response. The 6,000 is last year's profit and the 4,000–5,000 is a future profit, so they are different quantities. The inverted cost condition is an accuracy error, not a two-value inconsistency.
- **Response 2:** all six pass.
  - H1: about 582 tokens.
  - H2: 3 question marks.
  - H3: Latvian.
  - H4: no internal labels.

## Step 6. Preference

Response 2 serves this owner better this week. Its arithmetic is correct and its nothing-changes forecast shows the real risk: profit near zero or a loss, with the reserve falling below the red line. That correctly frames the lease decision as a low-cost exit-or-test choice. Response 1 says the same scenario still yields €4,000–5,000 under an inverted cost condition, and presents leaving as the costly mistake. Both responses share the same overall shape (decline the +30 % plan, short lease, red line, cheap tests). Response 2's steps also have clear dates and owners and start with diagnostics (customers vs basket, cost structure, what regulars buy elsewhere), and its only flaw is a rhetorical misstatement about the factory.
