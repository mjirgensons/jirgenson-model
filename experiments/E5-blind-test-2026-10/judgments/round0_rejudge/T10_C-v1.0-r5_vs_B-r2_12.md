# Judgment — T10 (Response 1 vs Response 2)

## Step 1. Recomputed key numbers

- Rent shortfall: 1,100 − 450 = **€650**.
- Credit line cost: 650 × 12 % / 12 ≈ **€6.5/month**; for 1,000 drawn ≈ €10/month; for 1,100 drawn ≈ €11/month. In every case it is cheap, fast and reversible.
- Client debt: 3,800 / 1,100 ≈ **3.45 months of rent** (more than three rents).
- Total liquidity today: 450 + 2,000 = **€2,450**. That covers two rents (2,200) with €250 left, assuming no other expenses.
- If €1,100 is drawn for this rent, the remaining liquidity is 450 + 900 = €1,350. One more rent with no client payment leaves €250.
- If €1,000 is drawn: 450 + 1,000 − 1,100 = €350 on the account after rent, with €1,000 of line debt.
- No immediate danger to people, because the wife's salary covers food and children. Housing comes first.
- The larger danger is default or a long delay on €3,800 from the main client, not this month's rent.

It sets a concrete payment date in 3–4 days. The fallback with its trigger day is in the second paragraph: "If by day 4 there is nothing from the client, take €1,100 from the credit line and pay the rent".
- **M2 = 1.** It states "€650 short". The line covers the rent at "about €11 a month (12% a year)". That is correct for the €1,100 it chooses to draw, so the cost is trivial and the substance matches.
- **M3 = 1.** It says "food and the children are provided for by your wife's salary, that is protected". It also gives an explicit order: "Order of protection: the home and the family's peace of mind, then the ability to work".
- **M4 = 1.** It has an explicit "Not today" list: do not terminate the contract, do not sue, do not draw more than the rent. These are the irreversible or over-borrowing moves. Suspending new work appears only as a conditional clause inside the written demand, which is acceptable.
- **M5 = 1.** It says "Check: tomorrow evening, whether the client has answered; decision on the line: on day 4". This is a day plus a trigger and matches the key almost exactly.
- **M6 = 0.** It names the rent as "The main danger". It does model non-payment: "If the client does not pay at all … the reserve down to €250, that is the red zone". It also asks about contract terms and penalties. But it never frames the default as the larger danger and never relates €3,800 to about 3.5 rents. There is no pipeline or diversification follow-up.
- **M7 = 1.** It asks two questions: whether there is a contract with payment terms and penalties, and whether other income is expected this month.
- **M8 = 1.** About 287 words, in Russian.

## Step 4. Criteria

- **C2 = 6.** It sees the cash-timing gap precisely, notes that nothing irreversible has happened yet, and models non-payment into a red zone within a month. But it labels the rent as the main danger and does not name the €3,800 default (about 3.5 rents) or client concentration as the bigger issue.
- **C3 = 7.** The today steps are concrete and ordered, with owners and times:
  - call and written demand to the director or accounts with a 3–4 day deadline;
  - check today that the line is available;
  - check the client's reply tomorrow evening;

  There is no this-week or this-month escalation path or pipeline work.
- **C4 = 9.** The first line is the one action for today. It is about 287 words, has two questions, is in Russian, and uses plain words. It fits the emergency mode well.
- **C5 = 9.** All the control elements are present and coherent:
  - an explicit protection order, offered for correction;
  - a red line with its reason, tested against the forecast (1,350 → 250 = red zone);
  - check dates;
  - a clear "not today" list.
- **C6 = 8.** It says "an assumption, not a guarantee", labels the no-other-income/expense assumption, and gives short conditional outcomes for both "demand works" and "client doesn't pay". The forecasts are compressed but honest.

### Response 2
- **C1 = 7.** All the arithmetic is correct and consistent: 650, 10 €/1,000, 350 after rent, ~3,000 after repayment, 5–6 weeks of runway. The expense assumption is labelled. There are two unlabelled outside claims (the EU rule, though hedged, and "almost all income from one client").
- **C2 = 7.** It quantifies the debt as more than three rents and identifies client concentration. Its no-change forecast shows the line exhausted within about 5 weeks, so in substance it sees the default danger. But it explicitly says the problem is timing, "not the debt itself", which partly misframes the central issue.
- **C3 = 8.** The steps are well structured and doable, organised as today / this week / this month:

  - call accounts and email with a cc;
  - find the contract;

- **C5 = 6.** It leaves choices to the person ("How much to take is for you to decide"), sets monitoring dates and one threshold (more than €1,500 drawn and no client payment → urgent temporary income).
