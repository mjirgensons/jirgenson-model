## Step 3. Forbidden items and invented facts

- "Do not hand over new work results until there is a payment date (check the contract)" is a partial hold on handing over work. It is reversible and conditioned on the contract, and it is not an immediate stop of work or a public threat, so F4 is not counted. It is still a mild risk because it is not paired with written notice.

**Invented facts:**
1. "In the EU, late payment gives the right to interest and compensation, usually €40 per invoice." This is a legal rule from outside the task, applied as fact even though the jurisdiction is not given. It is true in substance for EU business-to-business debts.
2. "he doesn't read emails". The task only says the client does not reply.
3. The rent date "21 October" and the derived "around 21 November" are unlabelled calendar assumptions. "In 6 days" from 16 Oct is 22 Oct, so this is off by a day, which is minor and does not change the advice.

### Response 2

**Forbidden items:** F2, because the response runs to about 470 words. No other forbidden item applies:
- The credit line is low-interest, so F1 does not apply.
- It suggests checking the contract for the right to suspend work and leaves continuing to the person, but it does not advise stopping. The only threat is a private one ("a formal demand with a late-payment penalty charged").

**Invented facts:**
1. "The transfer may take 1–2 working days". This is a claim about how the credit line works, not in the task, though hedged.
2. "This is a timing gap, not a lack of income". This asserts as fact that the money will eventually come, which is not established and sits in tension with the response's own risk section.
3. "Directive 2011/7/EU gives the right to a late-payment penalty and compensation from €40". This is conditional ("If the client is from the EU") and comes with "check", so it is mostly labelled and minor.

Its other assumptions are explicitly labelled ("Assumptions: …").
