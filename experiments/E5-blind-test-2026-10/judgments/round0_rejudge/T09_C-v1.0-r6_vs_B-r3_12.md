# Judgment — T09 (Newspaper kiosk in a small town), pair C-v1.0-r6 vs B-r3 (#12)

## Step 1. Gold facts, recomputed

- Revenue 2023→2024: 48,000 → 42,000 = −12.5 %. 2024→2025: 42,000 → 37,000 = −11.9 %.
- 2026 run-rate: 25,000 / 9 = 2,778 €/month; × 12 = 33,333 € (−9.9 % vs 2025). Cumulative 2023→2026e: −30.6 %.
- Population 8,000 → 6,000 over five years = −25 % (factory closed 2023).
- +30 % on 33,333 = 43,333 €, which is more than 2024 (42,000). Monthly pace for +30 % = 3,611 €.
- Nothing changes (another ≈ −10 %): 2027 ≈ 30,000 €; gap to the target ≈ 13,300 €.
- Owner's pay: 6,000 € / (55 h × 52 = 2,860 h) = €2.10/h. With 48 working weeks (2,640 h) it is €2.27/h. Either way about €2–2.3/h, or about 500 €/month.
- Reserve 4,000 € ≈ 8 months of last year's profit (500 €/month), or 9–10 months of this year's lower profit.
- Lease ends in 8 months (about June 2027, assuming it is now about October 2026). That is the decision point. A wrong exit costs little: there is a 4,000 € reserve and no stated long-term liabilities.
- The task gives no cost structure. Any profit forecast therefore needs a labelled assumption about fixed costs or margin.

## Step 2. Mandatory elements

### Response 1
- **M1 = 1.** The whole reply is in Latvian, in the informal singular "you" form. The words are everyday ones ("red line" is explained in place), and none of the banned terms appear.
- **M2 = 1.** "Turnover is falling for the third year: about 12% a year, this year about 10%". It also gives "€2,780 a month; for the year it will come to about €33,000" and labels its assumption about the remaining three months.
- **M3 = 1.** "The town has shrunk by a quarter, the factory is closed: that holds things back the most, and advertising does not overcome it, it only slows the decline."
- **M4 = 1.** It opens with "With marketing alone I do not promise +30% next year". It shows that 43,000 is more than the 2024 level and that the gap is about 13,000 against a 30,000 trend. It gives no generic plan.
- **M5 = 1.** It names the lease ending in June as the main decision ("This winter's most important decision is the lease"). The options include:
  - a short lease with an exit clause;
  - relocation;
  - closure ("a short term, moving or closing");
  - a cheap test of a new service (≤500 € from profit) before renewing;
  - staying only if a measurable condition holds.

  The owner's time is valued at €2.10/h, and it proposes cutting hours to ≤45 a week. The point that a wrong exit costs little is only implicit: the 4,000 € reserve, "do not sign a new multi-year lease", and closure listed as a normal option. Its substance is there, though it is not stated in so many words.
- **M6 = 1.** Nothing changes: about 30,000 € (28–32k), profit about 4,800 €. Plan works: 31–35k, with the 2–3k contribution from the new service labelled as an assumption. It also says "€43,000 is outside both ranges". The profit forecast is not "near zero" because the response uses a constant-margin assumption. That is noted under C1; the element itself is met.
- **M7 = 1.** It gives an explicit, measurable stop rule before the lease decision: "If at the end of January the weekly takings are still more than 10% below last year's, then we drop a long lease extension…". There are review dates (1 Dec; lease decision by end of February). It asks exactly 3 questions (rent and owner of the premises; revenue mix and commission; how much the owner needs per month). The third question touches the owner's priorities, and the priority ordering is covered separately.
- **M8 = 1.** About 590 words counted as whitespace tokens (fewer if "2 780"-type numbers and €/% symbols are not counted as words). That is within 600, but only just.

**Response 1**
- Forbidden: none.
  - F1: no marketing plan. The only promotional step is to announce the new service on the door and in the local group.
  - F3: not declared dead. It tests a niche and asks questions.
  - F4: the priority order is proposed ("I suggest this order… If the kiosk is more important to you than your time, tell me").
- Invented facts: none. The assumptions are labelled: "I assume average last three months", "if profit stays at about 16%", "this is an assumption" on the 2–3k from the service, and the 15 % worst case is conditional. "advertising does not overcome it" is an analytical judgement grounded in the task data, not an invented figure.

**Response 2**
- Forbidden: none. The local-group post is one of three optional cheap tests, not the answer. Priorities are not chosen for the owner ("Which two, decide yourself").
- Invented facts: none in the strict sense. Two claims are mildly unlabelled:
  - "A new lease is your biggest money commitment" (the rent is unknown);
  - the causal claim "Most of it is explained by people leaving", which compares a 2023–2026 revenue fall with a five-year population fall (mismatched periods).

  Both are reasonable inferences rather than fabricated facts.

## Step 4. Criteria

**Response 1**
- **C1 = 8.** Every figure checks out: 2,780/month, 33k, −12 %/−10 %, €2.10/h, 43k > 2024, 30k trend, 13k gap, reserve ≈ 9 months, 2,000 € ≈ 4.5 months, 3,600/month for +30 %. The labelled constant 16 % margin, however, carries into the forecasts and ignores fixed costs, so the profit forecasts (4,800 € with no change) are likely flattering.
- **C2 = 9.** It names the central issue: a shrinking town outweighs any marketing, and the target lies outside both forecast ranges. It also names the most dangerous event: signing a long lease in about June against a falling trend with only 4,000 € behind it. It ties that to the reserve and to the owner's €2/h. It does not check the lease notice deadline.
- **C3 = 8.** Steps are owner-doable and dated:
  - this week, an hourly takings log;
  - by 1 Dec, ask 20 regular customers and test one service for ≤500 €;
  - by the end of January, cut empty hours, negotiate a 12-month lease with an exit clause and lower rent, and read the distributor and lottery contracts.

  Two weaknesses: there is no "today" step, and starting lease talks only in January could miss an unknown notice period.
- **C4 = 8.** Latvian, plain words, 3 questions, and it answers the real question by reframing it. It sits right at the 600-word limit, and it is dense with numbers and a few abstract lines (""+30%" is a result, but marketing is only a method").
- **C5 = 9.** All the control elements are present and coherent:
  - an explicit priority order, proposed with an invitation to change it;
  - a red line (reserve ≥2,000 €) with a reason, tested against the forecast;
  - weekly and monthly metrics;
  - review dates (1 Dec, end of February);
  - a numeric stop rule;
  - a "Don't do now" list (no multi-year lease, no loan, no large stock).
- **C6 = 8.** No promises ("it cannot be promised, these are estimates"), two forecasts with ranges and conditions, and the key assumption labelled. It gives no explicit payback calculation for the 500 € test, and the constant-margin profit figures are optimistic.

**Response 2**
- **C1 = 8.** All the arithmetic is correct and consistent with its stated marginal-contribution assumption: 33k, −31 %, ≈2,900 h → ≈€2/h, 500 €/month, 4.5–5k this year, 8–11 months of reserve, 43k, a 2,000 € payback threshold ≈ 170 €/month, and +2…+11 %. Its fixed-cost modelling is sounder than Response 1's. There are two weaknesses: a period mismatch in the population comparison behind a causal claim, and this year's profit is given before the assumptions behind it are stated.
- **C2 = 8.** It sees the core contradiction ("luck, not a plan"; the decline is driven by population) and names the lease as the biggest risk. It is precise about the nearest irreversible step, the notice deadline. But it frames the lease decision only as how to renew and never puts exit or closure on the table.
- **C3 = 9.** Very practical, ordered steps:
  - today (15 min), find the end date, notice deadline and rent;
  - this week, a revenue breakdown by category and a landlord conversation;
  - this month, two 6-week tests for ≤500 € with a clear comparison method.

  The test options are concrete, and the choice is left to the owner.
- **C4 = 9.** Latvian, plain and readable, about 420 words, 2 well-targeted questions. It respects the request by explaining clearly why +30 % is not a plan.
- **C5 = 5.** Metrics (monthly vs same month last year) and a review point (end of January, before the notice deadline) are present. Missing: a priority order, a proposed threshold or red line (it asks the owner to set one), a check of any red line against the forecast, and an explicit list of what not to do now.
- **C6 = 8.** Two forecasts with labelled assumptions, "There are no guarantees", and a payback for the test that includes its start-up cost. The plan-works range (+2…+11 % growth in a shrinking town) is on the optimistic side, but it is labelled.
