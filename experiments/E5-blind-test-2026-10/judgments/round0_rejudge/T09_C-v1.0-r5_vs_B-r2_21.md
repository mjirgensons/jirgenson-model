# Judgment — T09 (Newspaper kiosk in a small town), Response 1 vs Response 2

## Step 1. Recomputed key numbers

- Turnover: 48,000 → 42,000 = −12.5 %; 42,000 → 37,000 = −11.9 %. 2026 run-rate: 25,000 / 9 × 12 = 33,333 ≈ 33,300, which is −9.9 % against 37,000. From 2023 to the 2026 run-rate: −31 % ("almost a third").
- Population: 8,000 → 6,000 = −25 % in five years. The factory closed in 2023.
- +30 % on 33,300 = 43,300 (+10,000 a year ≈ €27/day). +30 % on 37,000 = 48,100, which is the 2023 level.
- Owner's hours: 55 × 52 = 2,860 h, so €6,000 / 2,860 = €2.10/h. The gold figure of 2,640 h gives €2.27/h. Either way it is about €2/h.
- Current net margin: 6,000 / 37,000 = 16.2 %. Extra profit from +10,000 at 16 % is ≈ €1,600, or ≈ €0.56/h.
- Reserve: €4,000 is about 8 months of current profit (€500/month).
- The lease ends 8 months after mid-October 2026, so around June 2027. That is the decision point. The cost of a wrong exit is low: €4,000 reserve and no stated long-term liabilities.
- A plausible "nothing changes" 2027 figure is 33,300 × 0.88–0.90 ≈ 29,000–30,000.

## Step 2. Mandatory elements

**Response 1**
- M1 = 1. Plain everyday Latvian. None of the banned jargon.
- M2 = 1. Says turnover "falling by about 10% a year" and estimates 2026 at 33,000–35,000 (−5 to −10 %), with the caveat that year-end may be stronger. The −5 % lower bound is optimistic but it is flagged as uncertain.
- M3 = 0. The shrinking town appears only in a subordinate clause ("while the town keeps shrinking"). The response never links the decline to the population loss or the factory closure. It never names the environment as the dominant force, stronger than anything the owner can do. "Marketing does not remove this risk" refers to the lease risk, not to reversing the town's decline. This is borderline, but under a strict reading it is a gesture.
- M4 = 1. Does not give a +30 % marketing plan as the answer. It shows that +30 % means 43,000–45,000, above the 2024 level, while the town keeps shrinking, and says "Nobody can promise that."
- M5 = 0. Names lease renewal as the biggest risk, which is a sharp framing: renewing ties you to a declining business. It values the owner's time (~€2/h, and only €2.5–3/h even if +30 % works) and proposes cheap tests and a short 6–12-month extension. However, it does not give the lease end date. It does not say that the cost of a wrong exit is low. It does not present an orderly exit with the €4,000 intact as an option; "Whether to continue… you decide" only implies it.
- M6 = 1. Gives three numeric 2027 forecasts: nothing changes (30–33k, profit 3.5–5k), plan works (+30 %: 43–45k, profit 7–8.3k after marketing costs), and partial (34–37k, labelled "more realistic"). Using +30 % as the "plan works" case partly validates the unrealistic target, but the forecasts are numeric and conditional.
- M7 = 1. Gives a measurable condition: compare each month with the same month last year; if the decline slows from ~10 % to ≤5 %, the steps work. Checks are at the end of January and in March, before the lease decision. Asks one question (rent and year-end seasonality). It does not ask about what has been tried or what is growing in the town.
- M8 = 1. About 320 words.

**Response 2**
- M1 = 1. Everyday Latvian. "red line" is common usage, not banned jargon.
- M2 = 1. Gives "~12 %" for two consecutive years, "about 10% lower" this year, and 25,000/9 × 12 ≈ 33,000, with a labelled assumption about the last quarter.
- M3 = 1. "The town has a quarter fewer people… That is your strongest headwind, and advertising does not turn it around." This explicitly names the environment as the strongest force and says advertising will not reverse it.
- M4 = 1. Shows that +30 % means 43,000 (+10,000, ≈ €27/day), or 48,000 (the 2023 level) if counted from last year. It adds that even success would give only ≈ €0.6/h extra. It explicitly says not to base decisions on +30 %.
- M5 = 0. Names the lease end (~June 2027) as the biggest threat, with talks in January and a decision by March. It asks for a shorter term or a 3-month termination right, values the owner's time (~€2/h, "what these hours give you"), and proposes cheap tests capped at €500. However, it does not say that the cost of a wrong exit is low; "you may lose the place" frames losing the site as a threat. It does not list an orderly exit as an explicit option. The proposed priority order (savings first, kiosk third) only implies it.
- M6 = 1. Nothing changes: 29–30k, profit 4.5–5k, under €2/h. Plan works: 33–37k if new services offset the decline (~€100/week), labelled "This is not a promise". This matches the gold shape.
- M7 = 1. Weekly log of receipts, turnover and reserve. Review on 30 November. Stop any new activity that brings under €50/week. Stop experiments if the reserve falls below €2,500. No new long lease until the hourly data exist. Asks two questions (rent and the landlord's stance; product mix and margin). Instead of asking what the owner would give up last, it proposes an order and asks to be corrected. It does not ask what has been tried or what is growing in the town.
- M8 = 1. About 515 words. Even counting every whitespace token, it stays under 600.

## Step 3. Forbidden items and invented facts

**Response 1**
- Forbidden: none counted. F1 is close: among the "this month" tests it lists a regular-customer discount card and a post in a local group and on the shop window, which are the classic generic items. A discount card on thin press and lottery margins is questionable. These are examples inside a capped €300–500 test, not the main answer, so F1 is not triggered. F2, F3 and F4 are absent; the choice is explicitly left to the owner ("Which to choose is up to you", "you decide").
- Invented facts: "the second in March, when you have to decide about the lease" presents March as the lease decision month. The notice period is not in the task, and the response itself tells the owner to check the notice date. This is minor. The 25–35 cents of profit per euro of turnover is explicitly labelled as an assumption, so it is not counted.

**Response 2**
- Forbidden: none. It explicitly advises against paid advertising. It proposes an order of priorities and asks the owner to correct it rather than imposing it. It does not declare the kiosk dead.
- Invented facts: "the decision must be made by March" presents a March deadline without knowing the notice period. Read as planning advice it is minor; I count it for consistency with Response 1. "I assume…" and "if it follows turnover" are labelled assumptions. "the rent does not go up" is a reasonable implicit assumption that rent is a fixed cost. The opening "marketing can stop the decline" is an unsupported optimistic claim, not a figure, so it is noted under C6.

## Step 4. Criteria

**Response 1**
- C1 = 8. The arithmetic is correct and consistent with its labelled margin assumption: 2,800 h → ~€2/h; +30 % → 43–45k; plan-works profit 7–8.3k equals €2.5–3/h. The lower bound of −5 % for 2026 is optimistic, the 4,700 floor drifts slightly (the computation gives 4,600), and the March deadline is unlabelled.
- C2 = 6. It correctly sees that +30 % contradicts the data and that renewing the lease is the real exposure. It does not name the shrinking town as the cause, gives no lease end date, and never notes the low cost of exiting.
- C3 = 7. The today / this week / this month order is good, and "today: open the lease and note the notice deadline" is the best first step in either response. The tests, however, are a loose menu that includes generic marketing, and the "this month" items have no dates.
- C4 = 8. Latvian, plain words, well within budget, one question. It redirects the literal request sensibly, but still hands over a partial menu of marketing tactics.
- C5 = 6. It has a monthly measurement, a ≤5 % threshold, review points in January and March, a test budget of 10 % of savings, and leaves the choice to the owner. It has no priority order, no red line on the reserve tested against the forecast, and no "do not do now" list.
- C6 = 7. It uses ranges with conditions and says "nobody can promise". However, it gives +30 % as the "plan works" forecast with a profit figure, which lends the target more credibility than the data support, though it labels the partial case as more realistic.

**Response 2**
- C1 = 9. Every derived number checks out: 33,000 run-rate, −31 % since 2023, €27/day, the 48,000 = 2023 level, 2,860 h → ~€2/h, 16 % margin → ≥€1,600 → €0.6/h, €500/month → 8 months of reserve, 2,500 = 5 months, and 29–30k → 4.5–5k profit. Assumptions are labelled. The only blemishes are the March deadline and the unsupported line in the opening.
- C2 = 8. It names the shrinking town as the strongest headwind and reframes the question around what the owner's hours earn. It dates the lease end (~June 2027) and says what each mistake would hit (losing the site, or being tied to a falling business). It misses that a wrong exit is cheap.
- C3 = 8. Steps are dated (from Mon 19 Oct to 2 Nov, by 15 Nov, December, January), owner-doable, cheap, and run one at a time, with a €500 cap and specific asks for the landlord. It lacks a "check the lease notice clause today" step.
- C4 = 8. Latvian, plain words, under 600 words, two questions. A short empathetic opener and a clear redirection of the request. Longer than Response 1, but it carries proportionally more substance.
- C5 = 9. It proposes a priority order and invites correction. The €2,500 red line has a reason and is tested against the forecast ("The forecast does not cross this line…"). It sets weekly measurement, a dated review (30 Nov), a €50/week stop rule, a reserve-based stop rule, and an explicit "do not do now" list.
- C6 = 8. Two conditional forecasts with ranges; "This is not a promise"; it refuses to plan on +30 %. One soft optimistic claim ("marketing can stop the decline") is not supported by the data.

## Step 6. Preference

Response 2 would serve this owner better this week. It says plainly that the shrinking town is the strongest force and that advertising will not reverse it. It turns +30 % into daily euros and into the value of the owner's hours, and it ties a dated, cheap plan to a savings red line and stop rules before the lease decision. Response 1 is sound and concise, and its "open the lease today" step is excellent. However, it treats the environment only in passing, mixes generic marketing items into its tests, and offers +30 % as a "plan works" forecast. Neither response names the cheap orderly exit explicitly.
