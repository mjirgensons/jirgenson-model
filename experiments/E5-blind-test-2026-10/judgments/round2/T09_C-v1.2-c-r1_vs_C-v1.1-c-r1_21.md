# Judgment — T09 (Response 1 vs Response 2)

## Step 1. Recomputed key numbers

- Turnover decline: 48,000 → 42,000 = −6,000 / 48,000 = **−12.5 %**; 42,000 → 37,000 = −5,000 / 42,000 = **−11.9 %**.
- 2026 run-rate: 25,000 / 9 × 12 = **33,333** (≈ −9.9 % vs 2025). From 2023 to 2026 turnover falls by about 31 %, which is roughly a third.
- +30 % on 33,333 = **43,333**. That is above 2024 (42,000), a year when the factory was already closed. If Q4 is stronger, as in a 35,000–36,000 year, +30 % is about 45,500–46,800.
- Population: 8,000 → 6,000 = **−25 %** in five years.
- Owner's pay: 6,000 / year = 500 / month. Hours are 55 × 48 = 2,640 h, which gives **€2.27/h**. With 52 weeks it is 2,860 h, or €2.10/h. So about €2/h.
- 2025 costs = 37,000 − 6,000 = 31,000, about 2,583 / month.
- Nothing-changes forecast for 2027: −10 to −12 % gives about **29,300–30,000**. If costs scale with turnover, profit is 6,000 × 30/37 ≈ 4,700–4,900. If costs are fixed, profit is 29,500 − 31,000 ≈ **−1,500**. So the realistic profit range runs from near zero or negative up to about 4,800.
- Lease ends 8 months from mid-October 2026, so about **mid-June 2027**.
- The €4,000 reserve covers about 8 months of the owner's €500/month income, or about 1.5 months of gross costs.
- Cost of a wrong exit: low. There are no stated long-term liabilities, and the reserve stays intact. The owner's time currently earns only about €2/h.
