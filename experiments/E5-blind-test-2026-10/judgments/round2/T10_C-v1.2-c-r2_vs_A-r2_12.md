# Judgment — T10, C-v1.2-c-r2 vs A-r2 (pack 12)

## Step 1. Recomputed key numbers

- Rent shortfall: 1 100 − 450 = **650 €**.
- Credit-line cost for 650 €: 650 × 12 % / 12 = **6.5 €/month** (≈ 7 €). For 800 €: 800 × 12 % / 12 = **8 €/month**. Drawing a few days early costs cents.
- Client debt: 3 800 / 1 100 = **3.45 ≈ 3.5 months of rent**.
- Total liquidity now: 450 + 2 000 = **2 450 €** ≈ 2.2 rents. After this rent: **1 350 €**. After a second rent with no income: **250 €**. So without the client's payment the person can cover about two rents, and the next one after that is not covered. The real danger is the client defaulting or paying very late, not this month's rent.
- Partial-demand split (Response 1): 1 100 + 2 700 = 3 800 ✓.
- Response 2 path (draws 800): account 450 + 800 − 1 100 = 150; line left 1 200 ✓; after the next rent ≈ 250 in total ✓.
- Nobody is in physical danger (food and children are covered). Housing is the thing to protect first.

## Step 3. Forbidden items and invented facts

### Response 1
- **Forbidden:** none. "do not start new work for him until the old work is paid" does not stop ongoing work and is not a public threat. It sits next to "do not break with the client", so it does not meet F4.
- **Invented facts:** none. Every figure comes from the task. The one assumption is labelled: "there is no other income (this is my assumption)".

### Response 2
- **F2:** present (≈ 550 words > 300).
- **F4:** present. Step 5 is in the "What to do today" list: "Stop new work for this client, if any is under way. Do not hand over new results and do not give access…". The template letter says "Until payment is received, I am suspending further work." This advises stopping ongoing work immediately, starting today. It is softened by "First check the contract" and the neutral tone, but it is still an immediate stoppage, not a step after the deadline passes.
- **Invented facts** (external rules or claims, not in the task, presented as fact):
  1. "suspension in case of late payment is usually allowed". This legal claim about suspending work is unsupported and is the riskiest one, because the person may act on it.
  2. "In many EU countries this [simplified recovery / a court order] is inexpensive and can be done without an attorney". A legal generalisation; the task never says the person is in the EU.
  3. "Directive 2011/7/EU… the ECB rate + at least 8 percentage points… compensation of €40 or more". These terms are accurate in substance and conditioned on "If you are in the EU" with a "check the rules of your country" caveat, so this is low severity. It is still an outside legal rule asserted as fact.

## Step 4. Criteria

### Response 1
- **C1 = 9.** Every number checks out (650, ≈ 7 €/mo, 2 450 → 1 350 → 250, 1 100 + 2 700), the assumption is labelled, and nothing is invented. One small point: it treats a same-day draw-and-pay the day before rent as safe without saying so.
- **C2 = 7.** It correctly finds that the rent gap is reversible and shows how the client's non-payment crosses the red line by the next rent. But it calls the rent "the most dangerous thing" and never names the client's possible default or insolvency as the real danger.
- **C3 = 8.** Concrete, doable steps with times: call accounting today, send a written demand (1 100 € within 3 days, the rest by a set date), check the contract, check the reply tomorrow evening, draw exactly 650 € the day before rent if nothing has come. Missing: a client solvency check and other income. Also, waiting until the day before rent risks a late payment if transfers are slow.
- **C4 = 9.** About 281 words, the first line is today's action, two questions, Russian, mostly everyday words. "The relationship with this client I give up first" and "decision point" are a little stilted.
- **C5 = 9.** It proposes a priority order and leaves the choice open ("This is my order; if yours is different, tell me"). It proposes a red line (account + line < one rent), explains it and tests it against the forecast (1 350 → 250). It gives a review day and trigger, and it lists what to leave alone (don't take the whole line, no other credit).
- **C6 = 9.** It gives two forecasts ("If nothing changes" / "If the plan works") with balances, states "Payment cannot be guaranteed", and labels its assumption.

### Response 2
- **C1 = 5.** The core numbers are right (650; 8 €/mo for 800; 1 200 left). "A month and a half" understates the cover, which is really about two rents. The notation is sloppy, and three outside legal claims are presented as fact, one of them unsupported and likely to be acted on.
- **C2 = 8.** It sees the central issue clearly: the rent is simple arithmetic and the real problem is collecting from the client before the line runs out. It adds a solvency check. It does not frame the nearest irreversible event with its date as sharply as it could.
- **C3 = 7.** Strong, ordered and doable. The fast, reversible lever comes first, and it advises drawing early because of bank delays. Then come the call, a written notice to several addresses, a registry check, an escalation timeline and a template letter. But six "today" tasks are too many for an emergency, and one of them (stopping work and withholding access) is not easily reversed.
