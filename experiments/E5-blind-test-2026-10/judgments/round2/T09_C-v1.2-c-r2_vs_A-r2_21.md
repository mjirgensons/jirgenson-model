# Judgment — T09 (Newspaper kiosk in a small town), pair 21

## Step 1. Recomputed key numbers

- Turnover decline: 48,000 → 42,000 = −12.5 %; 42,000 → 37,000 = −11.9 %; 2026 run-rate 25,000 / 9 × 12 = 33,333 (≈ −9.9 % vs 2025). That is three periods of decline at about 10–12 % a year. Cumulative 2023 → 2026 ≈ −31 %.
- Population 8,000 → 6,000 = −25 % in five years (≈ −5.6 % a year). The kiosk's turnover is falling roughly twice as fast a year as the population.
- +30 % on 33,333 ≈ 43,300 (above the 2024 level). +30 % on 2025 (37,000) ≈ 48,100, the 2023 level.
- Nothing-changes 2027: 33,333 × 0.9 ≈ 30,000. Gap to 43,300 ≈ 13,300 a year ≈ 255 € a week.
- Margin 2025: 6,000 / 37,000 ≈ 16.2 %. Profit per hour: 6,000 / (55 × 52 = 2,860 h) ≈ 2.10 €/h (≈ 2.27 €/h at 48 weeks). That is 500 € a month.
- 2025 costs = 37,000 − 6,000 = 31,000 ≈ 2,580 € a month. If costs did not fall at all, 30,000 turnover would give −1,000 €. If all costs scaled with turnover, it would give ≈ 4,800 €. Reality lies between, since rent is fixed.
- The lease ends in 8 months, about June 2027, so the renewal decision is due around February–March 2027. Reserve 4,000 €, no stated debts, so a wrong exit costs little (≈ 2 €/h of foregone income).
- A 5 % discount at 16 % margin removes 5/16 ≈ 31 % of profit, about one third.

## Step 2. Mandatory elements

- **M2 = 1.** "falling by roughly 10–12% a year for the third year now" and a 2026 estimate of "about €33–35 thousand". The trend and the estimate are both present and correct. The 35k upper bound rests on an unsupported Q4 assumption, but the range contains 33.3k.
- **M3 = 1.** It names the shrinking town as the cause: "what is missing is not advertising but buyers" and "in a town that is losing residents". It says marketing is unlikely to reverse this: "+30% with marketing … very unlikely" and "Advertising raises … only a little".
- **M4 = 1.** No generic plan is the main answer. It shows what +30 % needs: 44k, "higher than in 2024", and 13–14k of extra turnover in a shrinking town. It also adds a strong extra point: even if the target were reached, the gain is about +1 €/h.
- **M5 = 1.** The plan is organised "until the end of the lease". It gives concrete lease levers: shorter term, early exit, turnover-linked rent, and no investment before renewal. It sets "Decision by March". It frames exit as legitimate with the reserve as "a safety cushion for the exit" and "a kiosk earning about €2 an hour does not have to be saved at any cost", which conveys that a wrong exit costs little. It values the owner's time at ≈ 2 €/h. The options are all there: exit, cheap service test before renewal, and stay only if thresholds are met.
- **M6 = 0.** There is a nothing-changes revenue figure ("2027 will be about €30–31 thousand") but no profit forecast for that case. The "plan works" case is only a target point ("hold on to about €34 thousand"), not a labelled forecast range. These are not two clear forecasts.
- **M7 = 0.** The continue/stop condition is a template with blanks ("at least €X and … at least €Y"), so it is not measurable as given. It also asks for four enumerated items plus one direct question. In substance that is about five questions, against a budget of 3.
- **M8 = 1.** About 535 words.

## Step 3. Forbidden items and invented facts

**Response 1**
- Forbidden: none. Facebook group posts appear as one small, cheap retention tactic, not as the answer, so F1 does not apply. Closure is offered as an option alongside niche ideas, not as a verdict, so F3 does not apply. The owner sets Y and is asked whether they want to keep the kiosk, so F4 does not apply.
- Invented or unsupported facts:
  1. "Advertising raises demand for newspapers and lotteries only a little: people already know the kiosk exists" is a causal claim stated as fact.
  2. "€300–500 is enough for these tests" is an unsupported cost figure stated as fact.
- Minor inaccuracy (not invented): the revenue decline is said to be "on the same scale as the fall in the number of residents". In fact it is about twice the annual population decline.

**Response 2**
- Forbidden: none. The priority order is "suggested" with "if your order is different, tell me", so it is not imposed.
- Invented facts: none found. The assumptions are labelled ("I assume the €6,000 is what you live on", "if expenses do not fall at all", "if expenses fall proportionally").
- Minor imprecisions:
  - The worst-case profit of −1,000 € matches 30k turnover, not the 29k lower end of its own range (which would give −2,000 €).
  - The 2,600 € red line is based on monthly *business* costs, which include goods. As a personal reserve threshold this basis is conceptually loose, though the reasoning is shown and tested.

## Step 4. Criteria

### Response 1
- **C1 = 7.** The core arithmetic is right: 44k, 13–14k, ≈ 2 €/h, +2–3k at 16–25 % ≈ +1 €/h, and a 5 % discount eating a third of profit. There are two unsupported factual claims and one loose comparison with the population decline, but none of them changes the advice.
- **C2 = 8.** It finds the contradiction between the +30 % request and a shrinking town. It adds a sharp insight: even success leaves the owner far below minimum wage, and fixed costs are already eroding 2026 profit. The lease is treated as the decision frame, but it is not singled out as the nearest risky event with what it would hit.
- **C3 = 8.** The steps are ordered and dated: two weeks, Nov–Dec, Jan–Feb, decision by March. The content is concrete: category and hour analysis, cutting hours from 55 to 45, specific lease terms to request, no capital spending before renewal, and a per-transaction payback check before adding a service. A few steps lean on partners (school, library), but they are offers the owner can make.
- **C4 = 7.** It is in Latvian, plain, within the word budget, and answers the real question by redirecting it with reasons. It breaches the ≤3 question budget in substance: a four-item "write" list plus one direct question.
- **C5 = 5.** Measurement is set (categories, hours) and there is a decision deadline (March). The reserve is protected and capital spending is frozen, and the owner sets Y. But the thresholds are blank (X/Y), there is no measurement frequency or review date, and nothing is tested against a forecast.
- **C6 = 7.** There are no promises. Ranges come with conditions, assumptions are labelled ("an optimistic assumption"), and it asks the owner to check the real profit figure. It lacks two explicit forecasts and gives no profit range for the nothing-changes case.

### Response 2
- **C1 = 8.** All the key numbers check out: 33k ±10 %, 43k, 48k, 13k = 250 €/week, 2.10 €/h, 31k costs, 1,400 € headroom, and 4,800 € at 16 %. Assumptions are labelled. Minor imprecisions: "~12 %" for all three years, and a profit lower bound that does not match the 29k end of the revenue range.
- **C2 = 9.** It names the shrinking town as the force marketing cannot reverse. It puts the lease forward as the "biggest threat" with its date and what it would hit (rent paid from ≈ 500 €/month with a 4,000 € cushion), and it weighs the opposite error of leaving too early.
- **C3 = 7.** The steps are well timed: this week (data, lease, hourly log), this month (two 4-week tests capped at 400 €), lease decision by end of February, and weekly checks. The test content is deferred ("what exactly, we will decide after the group numbers"), and it does not say what to ask the landlord for.
- **C4 = 8.** It is in Latvian, uses everyday words, stays within both budgets (2 questions) and answers the question asked. Some paragraphs are dense with figures for a quick read.
- **C5 = 8.** The priority order is proposed and the choice left to the owner. The 2,600 € red line has a reason and is tested against the worst-case forecast ("it does not cross the line"). It sets a weekly and monthly measurement rhythm, a review date (16 November), explicit switch conditions and a "What not to do" list. The basis of the red line is somewhat loose.
- **C6 = 9.** There are two labelled forecasts with ranges and conditions. "I cannot promise +30%" and "this is not a promise" avoid any promise, and it names what would narrow the range (rent, product mix, monthly data). The only wording that could be softer is the flat "marketing does not turn that around".

## Step 6. Preference

Response 2 is better. It frames the lease renewal as the real decision. It sets a red line tested against a worst-case forecast, a review date, explicit continue/stop triggers and two labelled forecasts, and it stays within the question budget. Response 1 is a strong, concrete answer with richer practical ideas and a memorable per-hour reframing. But its thresholds are left as X/Y, it has no proper two-scenario forecast, and it asks about five things against a budget of three. That leaves this owner less ready to decide before the lease runs out.
