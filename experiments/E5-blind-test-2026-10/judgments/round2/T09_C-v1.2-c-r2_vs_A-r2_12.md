# Judgment — T09 (Newspaper kiosk in a small town), Response 1 vs Response 2

## Step 1. Recomputed key numbers

- Turnover decline: 48,000 → 42,000 = −12.5 %; 42,000 → 37,000 = −11.9 %.
- 2026 run-rate: 25,000 / 9 × 12 = 33,333 € (≈ −9.9 % vs 2025). Three consecutive years of roughly −10…−12 %.
- Population: 8,000 → 6,000 = −25 % over five years (≈ −5.6 % a year on average). The largest factory closed in 2023.
- +30 % on 33,333 = 43,333 €. If the base is 2025 instead: 37,000 × 1.3 = 48,100 €, which is the 2023 level.
- 2027 if nothing changes (−10…−12 % from 33,333): ≈ 29,300–30,000 €. Gap to the +30 % target ≈ 13,000–14,000 € (≈ €250/week).
- Owner's pay: 6,000 € / (55 h × 48–52 weeks = 2,640–2,860 h) ≈ €2.1–2.3 per hour, ≈ €500 a month.
- 2025 margin before the owner's pay: 6,000 / 37,000 = 16.2 %. 2025 outflows: 37,000 − 6,000 = 31,000 € (≈ €2,580 a month).
- The lease ends in 8 months, which from October 2026 is about June 2027. That is the natural decision point. A wrong exit is cheap: the reserve is €4,000, no long-term liabilities are stated, and the job pays about €2/h.
- Unknowns that call for questions: any niche where the numbers could work (parcels, payments, coffee, other services), the rent, what has already been tried, and the owner's priorities.

## Step 2. Mandatory elements

**Response 1**
- M1 = 1. The reply is in Latvian and uses everyday words ("red line", "cushion", "lease"). It avoids "vector", "parameter" and "zone". One phrase is a little awkward ("with a combined ceiling of €400"), but meaning is not affected.
- M2 = 1. It says the decline is "~12% a year" for the third year running and that "2026 is heading for about €33,000". It also derives the figure (25,000 for nine months) and gives a ±10 % band.
- M3 = 1. "A town that has lost a quarter of its residents in five years pulls the other way, and marketing does not turn that around."
- M4 = 1. It gives no generic plan. It shows +30 % ≈ 43,000 € (48,000 € from the 2025 base, the 2023 level) against ≈ 30,000 € with no change, a gap of 13,000 € or ≈ €250/week. It concludes: "with these numbers it is not a realistic goal."
- M5 = 1. The section "The biggest threat: the lease" dates the lease end at about June 2027. It weighs both errors. A long renewal means paying rent out of ≈ €500/month with €4,000 as the only cushion. Leaving too early means losing "this €500 a month". That puts a size on the cost of a wrong exit and shows it is small. The options are tests capped at €400, a short extension, a closure plan plus other income, and stopping if the conditions fail. The owner's time is valued at €2.10/h.
- M6 = 1. Two forecasts are given. If nothing changes: 29,000–31,000 € in 2027, profit from −1,000 € to ≈ 4,800 €, each under stated assumptions. If the plan works: 33,000–35,000 € ("the decline stops"), ≈ €60–100/week extra, "this is not a promise".
- M7 = 1. There is a measurable stop condition: if January–February turnover does not reach the 2026 level, or savings fall below 2,600 €, switch to a short lease or a closure plan. There are two questions (the monthly rent, and how much of the 6,000 € the owner takes out to live on). It does not ask about niches or what has been tried, but it proposes testing a parcel or payment service. The priority order is proposed, not asked.
- M8 = 1. About 545 whitespace tokens, under 600.

**Response 2**
- M1 = 1. The Latvian is natural and everyday. "Margin" and "fixed costs" are ordinary words for a shop owner.
- M2 = 1. It gives "10–12% a year for the third year now" and puts 2026 at "about €33–35 thousand". The upper end rests on an unexplained quarterly assumption, but the substance is correct.
- M3 = 1. "So, most likely, what is missing is not advertising but buyers", and "+30% with marketing in this situation is very unlikely."
- M4 = 1. The main answer is not a marketing plan. +30 % of 34k ≈ 44k, which is "higher than in 2024", and the owner would need 13–14k of extra turnover in a shrinking town. The retention tactics in step 2 are a small test budget, not the answer.
- M5 = 1. It names the lease as ending in 8 months. It gives negotiation options (lower rent, 6–12 month term, early exit, turnover-linked rent) and says to make no investment before renewal. The decision is due by March. "The €4,000 savings are a safety cushion for the exit", and "a kiosk earning about €2 an hour does not have to be saved at any cost" covers the low cost of exit and values the owner's time. The options (exit, test a service, continue if thresholds are met) are present. It is weaker than Response 1 on framing the lease as the dated main threat, but substantively present.
- M6 = 0. The nothing-changes turnover is given (30–31k in 2027), but there is no profit figure for that case. The plan-works case is only a target ("If in 2027 you hold on to about €34 thousand"), not a labelled forecast range for a new format or niche. The +2–3k profit figure belongs to the +30 % scenario the response has just called unrealistic. The two-forecast structure is not clearly present.
- M7 = 0. The continue/stop rule is a template with blanks: "at least €X and profit per hour at least €Y". No measurable threshold is proposed. The ending asks for four numbered pieces of information plus one direct question. In substance that is five requests, above the ≤3 limit.
- M8 = 1. About 535 whitespace tokens, under 600.

## Step 3. Forbidden items and invented facts

**Response 1.** No forbidden items. F1: no generic plan, and advertising is capped at €400. F3: it does not declare the kiosk dead; it tests a service and keeps an exit check. F4: the priority order is explicitly "In the order I suggest … if your order is different, tell me." No invented facts. The assumptions are labelled ("I assume the €6,000 is what you live on"; "if expenses do not fall at all"; "if expenses fall proportionally"). The services are given as examples ("for example parcels or payments").

**Response 2.** No forbidden items. F1: the Facebook-group posts and school/library contacts are minor test tactics under a 300–500 € cap, not the answer. F3: closure is framed as a legitimate option, but only after tests and thresholds. F4: it proposes a replacement goal but asks whether the owner wants to keep the kiosk and says "You set Y yourself". One invented fact: the comparison to the minimum wage ("well below the minimum wage") brings in an outside benchmark not given in the task. It is accurate for Latvia, so it is minor. Two minor imprecisions rather than invented facts: "on the same scale as the fall in the number of residents" (population fell ≈ 5–6 %/yr against turnover's 10–12 %/yr), and the upper end of 33–35k depending on quarterly patterns the task does not give.

## Step 4. Criteria

**Response 1**
- C1 = 8. Every figure checks out: 33,333; 43,000 and 48,100; the 13,000 € gap at €250/week; €2.10/h and €500/month; 31,000 € outflows; 4,800 € at 16 %; €60–100/week. Assumptions are labelled. There are small looseness points that do not change the advice. "~12 %" slightly overstates the 2026 drop. "1.5 months of expenses" measures the reserve against total outflows, which include stock purchases paid from turnover. "compare with the same month in 2026" does not fit the November–December months.
- C2 = 9. It finds the real problem: a shrinking town against a growth target, and work paid at ≈ €2/h. It names the lease as the most dangerous event, with its date (about June 2027) and what it would hit (rent paid from ≈ €500/month with €4,000 as the only cushion). It weighs both errors.
- C3 = 8. The steps are ordered and doable by the owner. This week: monthly figures by product group, read the lease and its notice date, log sales by hour for two weeks. This month: two 4-week tests capped at €400. Landlord talks start by February, and the lease decision comes by end of February. The first test is left undefined ("we will decide after the group numbers").
- C4 = 9. It answers the actual request by showing why +30 % is not realistic and what to do instead. It is in Latvian, within budget, asks 2 questions and uses everyday words. It is dense with numbers, with one or two slightly awkward phrasings.
- C5 = 9. The priority order is proposed and the choice is left to the owner. The red line (2,600 €, one month of outflows) is tested against the worst-case forecast ("it does not cross the line, but everything you take for living above the profit eats into that €1,400"). Measures are weekly turnover and savings plus a monthly comparison. The next review date is 16 November. There is a clear stop/continue condition and an explicit "What not to do" list. The only flaw is the comparison year for the monthly check.
- C6 = 9. No promises ("I cannot promise +30%", "this is not a promise"). Both forecasts are ranges with conditions. Test spending is capped. It names what would narrow the range most: rent, sales mix and monthly data.

**Response 2**
- C1 = 7. The core arithmetic is correct: 44k; the 13–14k gap; 16 % margin; +2–3k profit ≈ +€1/h; a 5 % discount eats about a third of the margin; 34k ≈ 10 % above trend. Three things hold it back: an unexplained 35k upper bound, a loose "same scale as population decline" claim, and one outside benchmark (the minimum wage) presented as fact.
- C2 = 8. The diagnosis is strong. "What is missing is not advertising but buyers", and even a successful +30 % would add only ≈ €1/h, which is a sharp reframing. It also notes that fixed costs probably push 2026 profit below 6,000 €. The lease appears as plan step 4 with good negotiation levers, but it is not named as the dated main threat, and the response does not say what a bad renewal would hit.
- C3 = 8. The phases are concrete: two weeks of measurement, November–December retention, a January–February service, lease negotiation, a decision by March. The ideas are practical: cut 55 to 45 hours, home delivery subscriptions, per-transaction economics before adding a service, turnover-linked rent. Some depend on others (the school, the landlord), and the retention bundle is partly generic.
- C4 = 7. In Latvian, within the word budget, with a clear opening summary and natural language. However, the closing block of four numbered information requests plus one direct question breaches the ≤3-question limit in substance.
- C5 = 5. Some control is present: savings are reserved for exit, no investment before renewal, the owner sets the acceptable income. The continue/stop thresholds are blank (X, Y). There is no measurement frequency, no review date, and no red line tested against a forecast.
- C6 = 7. Honest and hedged ("very unlikely", "most likely", ranges for 2026 and 2027), with no promises. The two-forecast structure is missing: no profit under no change and no labelled plan-works range. The 34k figure is a target, not a forecast.

## Step 6. Preference

**Response 1 is better, confidence 0.7.** Both responses refuse the +30 % plan for the right reasons. Response 2 makes a sharper point: even success would add only about €1/h. Response 1, though, gives this owner what they need before the lease decision: a dated main threat, a reserve red line checked against a worst-case forecast, a measurable stop condition with a review date, and two labelled forecasts.
