# Judgment — T10 (Freelancer: client not paying, rent in 6 days; EMERGENCY, RU)

## Step 1. Recomputed key numbers

- Rent shortfall: 1,100 − 450 = **€650**.
- Credit line cost: 12 % p.a. ≈ 1 % per month. On €650: ≈ **€6.5/month**. On €800: €8/month (2 months ≈ €16). On €1,000: €10/month ≈ €0.33/day.
- Client debt: 3,800 / 1,100 ≈ **3.45 months of rent**. The bigger danger is a default or a long delay, not this month's rent.
- Runway if nothing comes in (both draw options): 450 + 2,000 − 1,100 = **€1,350** left after this rent (cash plus unused line). That covers one more rent (leaves €250), not two. So the runway is ≈ 1.2 rents after the current one, not 1.5.
  - With a €1,000 draw: €350 cash + €1,000 line = €1,350.
  - With an €800 draw: €150 cash + €1,200 line = €1,350.
- No immediate danger to people (food and children are covered by the wife's income). Housing is the thing to protect first.

### Response 1

That is a minor unlabelled assumption derived from "in 6 days", not an external fact. The forecast assumptions are labelled ("I am assuming there are no other expenses or incoming payments"), and so is the red line ("proposed by me").

### Response 2

- **Hedged generalisations:** "Many contracts allow you to suspend work…" and "many countries have one". These are presented with qualifiers.

## Step 4. Criteria

### Response 1
- **C1 = 9.** Every number is correct and comes from the task (650; 1,000 draw at €10/month and €0.3/day; 350 + 1,000 = 1,350; covers the second rent but not the third). The assumptions are labelled. The only gap is the implicit "today = 16 Oct" date assumption.
- **C2 = 7.** It names the nearest irreversible damage precisely (rent on 22 Oct hits the home) and its forecast shows the reserve failing by the second rent. But it calls the rent the "most dangerous" item and never names a client default (≈ 3.5 months of rent) as the bigger danger.
- **C3 = 8.** Steps are concrete and timed: call and email today, use the accounts department if there is no answer, cite the penalty, draw €1,000 by 19 Oct if there is no written date, ask the bank about transfer time. There is no escalation or new-client step for later in the month.
- **C4 = 9.** The first line is one action, it stays within 300 words, asks two questions, is in Russian, and uses everyday words. It fits the emergency mode well.
- **C5 = 9.** The priority order is proposed and left open ("The order is mine; correct me"). The red line is proposed with a reason and tested against the forecast (crossed at the second rent, about 22 Nov). It sets a review date with a trigger and says what to leave alone (no lawsuit, no cutting ties, no worse credit).
- **C6 = 9.** No guarantees ("This cannot be guaranteed"). There are two scenarios (nothing changes: reserve runs short; client pays: the line can be repaid), and the assumptions are named.

### Response 2
- **C1 = 6.** Core figures are correct (650; €8/month on 800; about €16 over two months). There is a minor runway error (1.5 instead of ≈ 1.2 months), an external EU legal rule and €40 figure stated as fact for a person whose country is unknown, and a speculative cause for the client's silence.
- **C2 = 8.** It correctly reframes the problem: the rent is the easy, cheap part and recovering €3,800 is the real task. It ties the time available for collection to the credit runway. It does not name the rent date or what a miss would hit.
- **C3 = 8.** The steps are very practical: draw €800 today or tomorrow, call accounts payable with specific questions, send a written confirmation template, file a formal claim after 3–5 days with a 7-day deadline, then a lawyer, and invoice other clients in parallel. Some dates are placeholders.
- **C4 = 3.** Several breaches of the emergency mode: the first line is not an action, three actions are given instead of one, it is about 398 words (≈ 33 % over budget), and it ends with three information requests. Some terms are in English or legal ("accounts payable", the directive number). It is in Russian and does answer "what to do today".
- **C5 = 5.** Some control is present: an escalation trigger at +3–5 days, a 7-day claim deadline, a runway limit, and one "leave untouched" item (don't draw the full €2,000). But there is no priority order offered for the person to choose, no red line tested against a forecast, and no single review date.
- **C6 = 6.** No promises, and the cost is given as a conditional range. But "The rent here can be solved quickly" is stated confidently, there is no two-scenario forecast, and several "often / many" generalisations are presented as near-facts.
