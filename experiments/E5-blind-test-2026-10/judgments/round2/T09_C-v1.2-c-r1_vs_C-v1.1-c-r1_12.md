# Judgment — T09 — C-v1.2-c-r1 vs C-v1.1-c-r1 (pack 12)

## Step 1. Recomputed key numbers

- Turnover decline: 48,000 → 42,000 = −12.5 %; 42,000 → 37,000 = −11.9 %.
- 2026 run-rate: 25,000 / 9 × 12 = 33,333 (≈ −9.9 % vs 2025). If Q4 is seasonally stronger, 35–36k is a plausible upper end.
- 2023 → 2026 run-rate: 48,000 → 33,333 = −31 % (about a third). Population 8,000 → 6,000 = −25 %.
- +30 % target: 33,333 × 1.3 ≈ 43,300 (35,000 × 1.3 = 45,500; 36,000 × 1.3 = 46,800). That is above the 2024 level of 42,000, so it means undoing about three years of decline.
- Owner's pay: 6,000 / (55 × 48 = 2,640 h) ≈ €2.27/h; at 52 weeks (2,860 h) ≈ €2.10/h. That is ≈ €500/month.
- Costs implied for 2025: 37,000 − 6,000 = 31,000/yr ≈ €2,583/month (this includes cost of goods).
- 2027 if nothing changes: 33,333 × 0.88–0.90 ≈ 29,300–30,000. Profit then ranges from ≈ +4,800 (all costs fall in proportion to turnover, 16.2 % margin) down to ≈ −1,500 (all costs fixed at 31,000). Realistically it ends up near zero to low thousands.
- The lease ends in 8 months, about mid-June 2027. That is the decision point. The cost of a wrong exit is low: the €4,000 reserve stays intact, no long-term liabilities are stated, and the hours earn only ≈ €2/h.
- 4,000 / 500 = 8 months of the owner's current income.
