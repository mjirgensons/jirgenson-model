# Judgment — T09 (Newspaper kiosk in a small town), pack T09_C-v1.2-c-r1_vs_A-r1_12

## Step 1. Recomputed key numbers

- Year-on-year decline: 48,000 → 42,000 = −12.5 %; 42,000 → 37,000 = −11.9 %. That is about −12 % a year.
- 2026 run-rate: 25,000 / 9 × 12 = 33,333 (≈ −9.9 % vs 2025). If December is seasonally stronger, the full year could plausibly reach 34–35k, but only as a labelled assumption.
- +30 % on 33,333 = 43,333 (43–45k if the base is 33–35k). That is above the 2024 level (42k) and close to 2023 (48k), with population down 25 % (8,000 → 6,000) and the main employer gone.
- Nothing-changes path: about −10 % again gives ≈ 30,000 in 2027. Profit falls towards zero, or below if a large share of costs is fixed.
- Owner's pay: €6,000 / (55 × 48 = 2,640 h) ≈ €2.3/h, or / (55 × 52 = 2,860 h) ≈ €2.1/h, so about €2/h. €6,000 / 12 = €500 a month.
- 2025 outflows (turnover minus profit) = 31,000/yr ≈ €2,580/month, so a €4,000 reserve ≈ 1.5 months of all outflows, cost of goods included.
- Lease ends 8 months from about October 2026, so around June 2027. This is the natural decision point. A wrong exit costs little: the income given up is only about €2/h, the reserve is €4,000 and no long-term liabilities are stated.

- **M7 = 1.** Measurable stop rule: "If the savings drop below €2,600 or the tests by 31 January do not improve the comparison with last year, we plan without an extension". There are 2 questions (rent; monthly drawings). The topics differ from the suggested ones (what was tried, what is growing, what to give up last), but the budget is kept and the conditions are measurable.
- **M8 = 1.** About 507 words, counted wc-style with headings.

## Step 3. Forbidden items and invented facts

### Response 1
- **Forbidden items:** none.
  - F1: no. Marketing is a capped €300 test after diagnostics.
  - F2: no.
  - F3: no. The kiosk is not declared dead; niches (parcels, payments) are to be checked.
  - F4: no. The priority order is offered explicitly for correction ("I am putting this in order for you, correct me if it is different").
- **Invented facts:** none. The 52 weeks, the December uplift and expenses = turnover − profit are all labelled. "Notice may be earlier than the end" and "are the press/lottery contracts tied to the premises" are framed as things to check, not as facts.

### Response 2
- **Forbidden items:** none.
  - F1: no. Facebook, sign and leaflets are one test among four.
  - F3: no.
  - F4: not flagged. "So I would change the goal" reframes the goal as minimum-wage-level pay without asking the owner's priorities. It is phrased as the adviser's proposal, and the A/B/C choice is left to the owner, so it is penalised under C5 instead.
- **Invented facts:**
  1. "the minimum wage is about €4.5 an hour gross";
  2. "Full-time work at the minimum wage gives about €780 a month gross";
  3. "the main product, newspapers, is shrinking across the whole country".

  None of these is in the task, and each is stated as fact. They are plausibly accurate external facts, but the comparison behind option C rests on them.
- **Minor issues:**
  - The +30 % profit calculation adds €2,000–2,500 to the 2025 profit of €6,000, although profit on 33–35k turnover would already be lower. This does not change the advice.
  - "almost the 2023 level" for 44k vs 48k is loose.
  - "the landlord doesn't have many alternative tenants" is an unlabelled inference.

## Step 4. Criteria

### Response 1
- **C1 = 9.** All arithmetic matches the recomputation, every assumption is labelled, and there are no invented facts. The "1.5 months of expenses" figure includes cost of goods and is crude, but it is labelled.
- **C2 = 8.** It names the shrinking town as the stronger force and the lease end (June 2027) as the biggest threat, with what it hits: a thin reserve and falling income. It sharply flags the notice date and whether the press/lottery contracts are tied to the premises. It does not fully see that €2/h makes exit cheap.
- **C3 = 8.** Steps are dated and owner-assigned: 23 Oct split turnover; two weeks counting customers; 16 Nov ask 10 regulars and check parcel/payment commissions; €300 cap to 31 Jan. Cheap, reversible steps come first. The two marketing tests are vague.
- **C4 = 9.** Latvian, everyday words, about 507 words, 2 questions. It answers the actual request by showing why +30 % is not supported and what to do instead.
- **C5 = 9.**
  - A priority order is proposed and left open to correction.
  - A €2,600 reserve red line comes with a reason and is tested against the worst-case 2027 forecast ("it would be crossed").
  - Weekly metrics, a next review on 16 Nov, a stop rule by 31 Jan, and a "what not to do now" list.
- **C6 = 9.** No promises. Ranges come with conditions, the two forecasts are given, 43–45k is explicitly not promised, and it names what would narrow the forecast.

### Response 2
- **C1 = 5.** The core arithmetic is right (12 %, 33–35k, 44k, €2/h, €500/month) and the margin is labelled. But minimum-wage figures and a national press trend are stated as fact, and the +30 % profit calculation mixes bases.
- **C2 = 9.** This is the deepest diagnosis of the two:
  - the decline follows the town;
  - even a successful +30 % leaves about €3/h, below minimum wage;
  - the lease end in June 2027 is the decision point;
  - the shrinking town also gives the owner leverage in rent talks.
- **C3 = 7.** The steps are concrete and the tests are cheap. They are ordered by month (Oct–Nov, Dec–Feb, Feb decision, March negotiation) rather than by specific dates, and each test's threshold is left for the owner to set.
- **C4 = 5.** It answers the request in Latvian, but it is about 650 words against a 600 cap and asks 4 questions against a cap of 3. That is two budget breaches, plus mildly technical words.
- **C5 = 5.** It has a decision date (February), a continue criterion (minimum-wage-level pay) and an experiment cap (€300–500). It lacks:
  - priorities made explicit and left to the owner, since the goal is reframed for them;
  - a reserve red line tested against a forecast;
  - a measurement cadence.
- **C6 = 7.** Honest ("unlikely to work") and no promises, with ranges for 2026. But the nothing-changes forecast is a single point with no profit, and its own plan has no "plan works" range.
