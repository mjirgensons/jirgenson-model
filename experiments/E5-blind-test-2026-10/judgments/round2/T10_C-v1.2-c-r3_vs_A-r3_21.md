# Judgment — T10 (C-v1.2-c-r3 vs A-r3, pair 21)

## Step 1. Recomputed key numbers

- Rent shortfall: 1,100 − 450 = **€650**.
- Credit line cost: 12 % / 12 = 1 % per month. On €650 that is ≈ **€6.5/month**. On €800 it is €8/month (two months ≈ €16). On €1,000 it is €10/month, or about €0.33/day.
- Client debt: 3,800 / 1,100 ≈ **3.45, so about 3.5 months of rent**. The bigger danger is a default or a long delay, not this month's rent.
- Runway if nothing is paid. Draw €1,000 and pay rent: 450 + 1,000 − 1,100 = €350 cash, plus €1,000 left on the line, so €1,350 in total. That covers the second rent (€250 left) but not the third. Draw €800 instead: €150 cash plus €1,200 on the line, again €1,350, about 1.2 rents (not 1.5).
- No immediate danger to people (food and children are covered by the wife's income). Housing is the thing to protect first.

Word counting method: I counted words and numbers. I left out standalone symbols (€, %, −, —, +) and list markers, and I counted a number written with a space as thousands separator ("3 800") as one word. On this basis Response 1 is about 396 words and Response 2 about 277. Response 2 would be about 300 even with a raw whitespace split that counts every € and splits every number.

The first is drawing €800 from the line. The call to accounts payable and the confirmation e-mail with a date come next. The formal demand is pushed to "today + 3–5 days". The content is reasonable, but the emergency format of one action on line one is not met.
- **M2 = 1.** "You are short €650 (1,100 − 450)". It says to draw €800 at "€8 a month", and that two months would cost "€15–20". All of this is correct and it shows the cost is trivial.
- **M3 = 0.** Rent is handled first as a logistics item, and later it says a delay "should not put the next rent at risk". It never names housing or family as what is protected first. That is only a vague gesture.
- **M4 = 1.** It has a "What you should not do" list: don't take all €2,000, no emotional letters or ultimatums without checking the contract, don't wait. It also says "without threats" about suspending work. It covers the irreversible threat item, but it misses the payday-loan and equipment-sale warnings.
- **M5 = 1.** "If there is no payment by [today + 3–5 days], send a formal demand…" is a dated trigger with an action. It gives a loose range, not a single day.
- **M6 = 1.** The first line already calls the 3,800 the main task. It gives an escalation path (claim, lawyer, simplified procedure), a contract and penalty check, partial payment if the client is short of money, and a new pipeline (invoice other clients, ask for prepayment). It also says the time for collection is limited. It does not quantify the debt as 3.5 months of rent.
- **M7 = 0.** The closing sentence asks for three separate pieces of information: country, contract terms on payment and penalties, and the language used with the client. That exceeds the two-question budget in substance, even though there are no question marks.
- **M8 = 0.** It is in Russian but about 396 words, well over 300.

## Step 4. Criteria

- **C1 = 6.** The core arithmetic is right (650; €8/month on 800; ≈€16 for two months). But the 1.5-month runway is overstated and unlabelled, and an outside legal rule (Directive 2011/7/EU, €40) is presented as fact.

- **C6 = 6.** It makes no promises and hedges claims, but it gives a single overstated runway estimate and no "nothing changes / plan works" pair of forecasts.

### Response 2
- **C1 = 9.** Every number is correct: 650; €10/month; €0.3/day; €1,350 after rent; second rent covered, third not. The assumptions and the red line are labelled. The only gap is the unstated date assumption behind 22 October.
- **C2 = 6.** It names the nearest irreversible damage precisely (rent on 22 October, hitting the home) and shows the buffer failing around 22 November. But it calls the rent "the most dangerous" thing and underplays the larger risk that the 3,800 (about 3.5 rents) is never paid.

The steps for this week and this month on collection and new income are thin. The trigger also leaves open what to do if the client names a date after the rent is due.

It proposes a red line with a reason, tests it against the forecast, and finds it crossed at the second rent. It sets a review date and a "don't do" list. It does not say how often to re-check.
