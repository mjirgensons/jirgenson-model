# Judgment — T10 (C-v1.5-c-r2 vs A-r2, pair 12)

## Step 1. Recomputed gold facts

- Rent shortfall: 1 100 − 450 = **650 €**.
- Credit-line cost for 650 €: 650 × 12 % / 12 = **6.5 € per month**. For other draw sizes: 800 € → 8 €/month; 1 000 € → 10 €/month. The line is cheap, fast and reversible either way.
- Client debt: 3 800 / 1 100 = 3.45, so about **3.5 months of rent**. The bigger risk is the client defaulting or paying very late, not this month's rent.
- Runway if the client never pays: cash 450 + line 2 000 = 2 450, which covers this rent and the next one (2 200) with 250 € left. The third rent, about 2 months from now, is not covered.
- If the client pays in full before rent: 450 + 3 800 − 1 100 = 3 150 € left after rent.
- Food and children are covered by the wife's income, so nobody is in immediate danger. Housing is what needs protecting.
- Deadline geometry: rent is due on day 6, so the fallback trigger has to come early enough (around day 3–4) for the transfer to clear.

## Step 2. Mandatory elements

### Response 1
- **M1 = 1.** The written demand is in substance the first action: "Today: send the client a written demand for €3,800 (both invoices, payment within 3 days) and duplicate it with a call or in a messenger, preferably also to the accounts department". It has a payment date and second channels. The fallback with its trigger day is in line 5: "in 3 days in the evening, if there is no payment: draw €1,000 from the line and pay the rent". The demand is not literally the first line. It follows a 7-word reassurance ("Food and the children are covered…"), and that positional breach is docked under C4.
- **M2 = 1.** "€650 short; the credit line covers this". The cost is given for its 1 000 € draw as "about €10 a month", which is correct (1 000 × 1 %). The route differs from the gold but is correct.
- **M3 = 1.** "We protect (the order is mine, correct me): the housing and the children, then taxes, last — the relationship with this client."
- **M4 = 1.** It has an explicit "Not today" list of irreversible moves: don't cut the client's access, don't go to court, don't draw the whole line. Suspending work comes only on day 3, and only together with an official notice. It does not mention payday loans or selling equipment, but the irreversible-escalation category is clearly covered.
- **M5 = 1.** "Check: in 3 days in the evening", linked to the trigger "if there is no payment: draw … from the line … pay the rent".
- **M6 = 1.** "Something else is more dangerous: 30 days of silence may mean that the client will not pay at all, and €3,800 is 3.5 rents. Check the company (register, news, other contractors)." The danger is quantified and comes with a follow-up action. It does not mention contract penalties or a new pipeline.
- **M7 = 1.** One question: other mandatory payments this month.
- **M8 = 1.** About 262 words, in Russian.

### Response 2
- **M1 = 0.** The first line is framing ("The rent here is not a catastrophe but simple arithmetic…"), not one action. The actions come after a "The numbers" section, as a six-item "today" list: credit line, call, formal reminder, check the client, stop work, other clients. The demand is not inside the first five lines, and there is no single first action.
- **M2 = 1.** "1,100 − 450 = €650", draw about 800 €, "800 × 12% ≈ €8 a month. That is nothing…". Correct, and the cost is called trivial.
- **M3 = 0.** Housing is protected only implicitly: the line is drawn first and "Pay the rent after the due date" is on the don't-list. The response never names housing or family as the thing to protect first.
- **M4 = 1.** "What you should not do": no angry letters or public accusations, no high-interest quick loans, no paying rent late.
- **M5 = 1.** It sets an escalation ladder with days and a trigger: "If the client keeps silent — after 5–7 days: a letter from a lawyer…; after 2 weeks: simplified recovery". The demand also has a 5-working-day deadline. This is a range rather than a single check day, but the substance is present.
- **M6 = 1.** "The real task is to get the money out of the client… the credit line only covers the next month and a half", "€1,200 will remain… one next rent payment if the client never pays", plus a bankruptcy check, contract/penalty clause, collection route and other clients. It does not compute the 3.5-months ratio, but the larger danger is clearly flagged and the follow-up is strong.

- **M8 = 0.** About 590 words, nearly double the 300-word budget.

## Step 3. Forbidden items and invented facts

### Response 1
- Forbidden: none. Suspending new work is deferred to day 3 and paired with an official notice, so F4 does not apply.
- Invented facts: none. Assumptions are labelled ("I assume rent once a month", "no other income", "The red line (I propose)", "the order is mine"). "Taxes" appears inside a proposed priority order and is not stated as a fact.

### Response 2
- Forbidden:
  - **F2**: about 590 words, more than 300.
  - **F4**: item 5 of the "today" list says "Stop new work for this client… do not give access until the overdue amount is paid", and the template letter announces "I am suspending further work". That is an immediate stop of work for the main client on day one, before the call or the deadline has had any chance to work. It is mitigated by "First check the contract" and by the written notice, but it is still an immediate stop.
- Invented facts (not in the task, presented as fact, partly hedged):
  1. EU Directive 2011/7/EU terms: statutory interest at "the ECB rate + at least 8 percentage points" and a fixed compensation "of €40 or more".
  2. "In many EU countries [simplified recovery] is inexpensive and can be done without an attorney", plus the "After 2 weeks" timing for a court order.
  3. "suspension in case of late payment is usually allowed".
  
  These are broadly true in the real world and conditioned on "If you are in the EU" / "check the rules of your country", which keeps them from being severe, but none comes from the task.
- Minor arithmetic looseness: "the credit line only covers the next month and a half". In fact cash plus the line covers two rents, about 2 months. This does not change the advice.

## Step 4. Criteria

### Response 1
- **C1 = 9.** Every number checks out (650; 1 000 draw → 350 left, 10 €/month; 1 000 left on the line; 250 after the next rent; 3.5 rents; 3 150 if the client pays), and the assumptions are labelled. The forecast ignores the small interest cost, which it covers with "about".
- **C2 = 8.** It separates the near event (rent on day 6, a 650 € gap) from the larger danger (a 30-day silence pointing to default, 3.8k ≈ 3.5 rents), and it names the deadline and what is hit. It does not look at contract terms or penalties.
- **C3 = 8.** "Today" and "day 3 evening" are clear, the owner is "You", the conditional fallbacks are concrete, and the fast reversible lever is held as a backup. It does not check the line's transfer time or fees, and drawing on day 3 evening leaves a thin margin. It says nothing about other clients or the pipeline.
- **C4 = 8.** It is within budget (~262 words), has one question, is in Russian and uses plain words. One breach: the first line is a reassurance rather than the action.
- **C5 = 9.** The priority order is proposed and left to the person ("correct me"). The red line is proposed with a reason and tested against the current state ("You are already below it… the alarm is already ringing"). It has a review date with triggers and an explicit "leave untouched now" list.
- **C6 = 9.** It gives two forecasts with their conditions (nothing changes → two rents covered, third not; client pays in 3 days → 3 150 €), makes no promises, and states its assumption.

### Response 2
- **C1 = 6.** The core arithmetic is correct (650, 800 → 8 €/month, 1 200 left). It brings in several legal facts and figures from outside the task (Directive rates, €40, cheap no-lawyer procedure, suspension "usually allowed"), only partly hedged, and the "month and a half" runway is imprecise.
- **C2 = 8.** The reframing is sharp: rent is arithmetic and the real problem is extracting the money. It lists plausible causes for non-payment, checks for bankruptcy, and notes the line buys only about one more rent.
- **C3 = 7.** The steps are concrete and doable, with times attached (10 minutes, today or tomorrow, same day, 5–7 days, 2 weeks), and the letter template is very usable. But six "today" actions overload an emergency, and stopping work immediately is a premature, hard-to-reverse escalation with the main client.
- **C4 = 3.** At about 590 words it is roughly double the emergency budget. The first line is not an action, and the heavy sectioning goes against the "one action today" mode. It is in Russian, uses plain words and keeps the asks to ≤2.
- **C5 = 5.** It has a don't-list and an escalation timeline. It has no explicit priority order offered for the person to choose, no threshold or red line tested against a forecast, and no balance-check review date.
- **C6 = 6.** It makes no guarantees and hedges the legal details, but there is only one forecast ("if the client never pays") and no plan-works scenario. "Usually allowed" is a soft assurance.
