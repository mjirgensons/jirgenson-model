# Judgment — T08 (Print shop: "profitable" but cash falling) — pair 12

## Step 1. Recomputed key numbers

- Cash: €22,000 → €13,000 = −€9,000 over 6 months = **−€1,500/month**.
- Receivables: €8,000 → €21,000 = **+€13,000** (≈ +€2,167/month).
- Paper profit: €3,000 × 6 = **€18,000**. Without the receivables build-up, cash should have risen ≈ €18,000; with it, ≈ +€5,000 (18,000 − 13,000). Actual −€9,000, so the gap is €27,000 against expectation, of which €13,000 is receivables and **≈ €14,000 is unexplained** (drawings, loan principal, tax/VAT, equipment, stock, or a wrong profit figure). This should be raised as an open question, not given a cause.
- Reserve: 13,000 / 26,000 = **0.5 months** of costs, below a proposed 1-month red line.
- Months to zero if nothing changes: 13,000 / 1,500 ≈ **8.7 months**.
- Implied sales ≈ 26,000 + 3,000 = €29,000/month if the stated figures are taken at face value. The 40% customer is then ≈ €11,600/month of sales. Their share of current receivables, at 40%, is ≈ €8,400. Note that the data do not fully agree with each other: a customer worth €11,600/month paying at 90 days would on its own hold ≈ €35,000 of receivables, more than the €21,000 total. So any figure built on implied sales is an assumption and should be labelled as one.
- Default scenario check: if the 40% customer pays nothing and deliveries continue, inflows ≈ 0.6 × 29,000 = €17,400 against €26,000 costs, so −€8,600/month. 13,000 / 8,600 ≈ 1.5 months ≈ 6 weeks.
- Plan-works check: collecting €6,500–13,000 of the receivables rise gives €19,500–26,000 = 0.75–1.0 month of costs.

It then makes the bank balance in months of costs the alarm that drives the week ("You are already under it: the alarm is ringing now"), so cash governs this week's decisions, with the reason given.
- **M2 = 1.** "€13,000 is half a month of your €26,000 costs. I propose a red line at one month… You are already under it."
- **M3 = 1.** The −€1,500/month burn appears in the forecast. The +€13,000 receivables growth is stated. The unexplained €14,000 is named and flagged as an unknown to resolve: "ask your accountant what left the account… (drawings, loan instalments, VAT, equipment)". Day 2 repeats it as a step. It is not put to the user as a question mark, but it is clearly raised as an open question and no cause is asserted.
- **M4 = 1.** The event is "Your biggest customer not paying at all". The amount uses a labelled assumption (≈ €11,600/month), and the effect on runway is spelled out: "minus €8,600 a month, and the €13,000 lasts about six weeks". It does not size the customer's share of the current €21,000 but asks for it (Q1).
- **M5 = 1.** Levers: a written demand with a 7-day deadline or a written part-payment schedule, a phone call, demands to all other overdue customers, checking late-payment interest, half up front on that customer's new work if they have not paid by Day 7, 30-day terms and deposits on large jobs. All are collection and terms levers, not sales. Factoring is not mentioned.
- **M6 = 1.** "Nothing changes: … zero in about 9 months; worst case six weeks." "Plan works: collecting half to all of the €13,000 rise gives €19,500 to €26,000, 0.75 to 1 month of costs."
- **M7 = 1.** "Every Monday: bank in months of costs (now 0.5), total unpaid (now €21,000), the big customer's overdue amount. Next check: day 7." It also sets an emergency threshold (€6,500). Days-to-payment and a full ageing by customer are not explicit, but the substance is there: weekly cash and receivables tracking, a customer-level figure, and a review date.
- **M8 = 1.** 2 questions, ≈ 519 words.

- **M8 = 1.** 1 question, ≈ 472 words.

## Step 3. Forbidden items and invented facts

### Response 1
- **Forbidden:** none. It does not accept "profitable" as a reason to grow (F1). It gives candidate causes for the €14,000 and assigns none (F2). It asks 2 questions (F3). It says "No promise" explicitly (F4).
- **Invented facts:** none. The €29,000 sales figure is labelled ("I am assuming sales of about €29,000 a month (costs plus profit)"), and everything derived from it (€11,600, €17,400, −€8,600, six weeks) follows from that labelled assumption. "€6,500 (about one week of costs)" is a quarter-month approximation, not a new fact. One caveat: the labelled assumption conflicts somewhat with the receivables data (see Step 1), and the response does not notice this. It does hedge with "a 10% error in my assumptions does not change the actions" and asks for the customer's share of the €21,000.

### Response 2
- **Forbidden:** none. It lists sources for the €14k without asserting one. It asks 1 question. "Cash could get tighter before it improves" leans slightly towards reassurance but is not a promise of recovery.
- **Invented facts:**
  1. "mostly your biggest customer's" (the receivables). This is an unlabelled inference; the task does not say how the €21,000 is split.
  2. "In the EU, late B2B payments legally carry interest and a fixed fee". This is an external legal rule presented as fact. It is accurate, and hedged with "Check the details for your country", but it is not in the task and the task does not state the country.
  3. "Mentioning that in reminders often speeds things up": an unsupported effectiveness claim (minor).

## Step 4. Criteria

### Response 1
- **C1 = 8.** Every figure checks out (27k gap, 14k unexplained, 0.5 months, −1,500/month, ≈9 months, −8,600/month, ≈6 weeks, 19,500–26,000) and the sales assumption is labelled. It loses points because the default scenario rests on a sales assumption that does not fully agree with the receivables data, and the response does not flag this.
- **C2 = 9.** It finds the real problem (cash, not profit), separates the €13k of receivables from the €14k leak, names the nearest damaging event (the big customer's default) with its timing (≈6 weeks of cash), and checks whether the slowdown is market-wide.
- **C3 = 9.** The steps are dated (Today / Day 1 / Day 2 / Day 7 / This month), owned ("you"), and run from fast and reversible to firmer. The Day 7 step is conditional and spells out the cost of being too soft and too hard. "Today" versus "Day 1" is slightly ambiguous.
- **C4 = 9.** It answers both "what's going on" and "what do I do this week" in ≈519 words with 2 questions, in plain English. It is dense but within budget.
- **C5 = 9.** Priorities are proposed with the choice left to the owner ("My proposal; tell me if yours differs"). The red line has a reason and is tested against the forecast ("back to the red line, not safe"). There is an emergency threshold, weekly measures, a Day 7 review, and a "not now" list. Days-to-payment is not tracked.
- **C6 = 9.** It says "No promise" and gives two forecasts with ranges and conditions plus a worst case. It is candid that even success only reaches the red line, and only if the €14k leak stops.

### Response 2
- **C1 = 6.** The arithmetic it shows is correct (18k, +5k expected, −9k actual, 14k, two weeks), but there is one unlabelled assumption ("mostly your biggest customer's") and an external legal rule stated as fact. That rule is accurate and hedged, so the error is minor.
- **C2 = 6.** It explains the contradiction and the €14k gap well, and the question about what "90 days late" means is a useful insight. But it gives no burn rate or runway, no size for the big-customer risk, and it downplays the 0.5-month reserve with "It's not a crisis today."
- **C3 = 7.** The six actions are sensible and doable (phone rather than email, deposit or credit-hold policy, aging, finding the €14k, a cash forecast, an overdraft line). Nothing is ordered beyond "this week", there are no day assignments, and a 13-week forecast is a heavy task for a quick request.
- **C4 = 8.** It answers the question in ≈472 words with 1 question, in clear everyday English. The 13-week forecast and the closing offer to do more analysis go slightly beyond a quick answer.
- **C5 = 3.** There is no red line or threshold, no weekly measures, no review date, and no explicit priorities or "leave untouched" list. The only control device is a credit limit the owner has to set.
- **C6 = 5.** It is honest about the unknown €14k and makes no promises. But there are no forecasts at all, the urgency is understated ("not a crisis today"), and "before it improves" is mildly reassuring.
