Two red lines have reasons and are tested against the forecast (turnover 31,000 = break-even, already crossed next year; reserve 2,600 = one month of costs). It sets daily measurement, Monday reviews, a review after 4 weeks, and a decision rule tied to the lease. There is no explicit "leave alone now" list beyond not signing.

Minor problems: the stale 500 €/month as current income, "before taxes", and 365 days left unlabelled. None of these change the advice.

**Response 1:** H1 true (≈ 530 ≤ 600); H2 true (2 questions); H3 true (Latvian); H4 true (no internal labels); H5 true (not a youth task, and no management jargon); H6 true. The small rounding differences (12.5 vs 13 %, 29,100 vs 29,200) are not conflicting values for the same quantity.

## Step 6. Preference

Response 2 is the better answer for this owner this week. It says outright that the shrinking town is the main force against growth. It warns that the real lease deadline may be the earlier notice date. It gives concrete, dated, cheap steps toward realistic niches (parcel or ticket point, delivery, cutting dead hours) and an explicit list of what not to do. Response 1 has the sharper exit analysis (0.80 €/h at the current pace, wrong stay vs wrong exit, a lease decision rule), which Response 2 gets partly wrong by overstating what exiting costs. But Response 1's only test is a promotion, it never firmly names the town as the main force against growth, and its prose is harder to follow. So Response 2 is ahead overall, by a moderate margin.
