# Judgment — T10 (C-v1.5-c-r1 vs A-r1, pack 12)

## Step 1. Gold facts recomputed

- Rent shortfall: 1 100 − 450 = **650 €**.
- Credit-line cost: 12 % a year ≈ 1 % a month. 650 € costs ≈ **6.5 € a month**, 700 € ≈ 7 €, 1 000 € ≈ 10 € (≈ 0.33 € a day).
- Total liquidity with no income: 450 + 2 000 = **2 450 €**. After rent 1: 1 350 €. After rent 2: 250 €. Rent 3 is short by **850 €**.
- Client debt 3 800 / 1 100 ≈ **3.45 months of rent**. A default or a long delay is the bigger danger, not this month's rent.
- Food and children are covered by the wife's income. Housing comes first.
- If 1 000 € is drawn on day 5: 450 + 1 000 = 1 450 €, and 350 € is left after rent. If 700 € is drawn, 1 300 € of the limit stays unused.

But it makes this conditional on checking the contract first, limits it to new work, and explicitly forbids disconnecting anything. That is not an immediate full stop or a public threat.

## Step 4. Criteria

### Response 1
- **C1 = 9.** Every number checks out (650, 2 450, 250, −850, 1 450, 350, 10 €/month, 0.33 €/day), and every assumption is labelled.
- **C2 = 9.** It finds the real problem: the client may never pay, and rent is only the first hit. It walks the cash path to the exact point where the buffer breaks the red line (rent 2) and where money runs out (rent 3). It does not explore why the client went silent.
- **C3 = 8.** It gives a clear dated sequence: demand today plus a second channel, check today that the line pays out at once, day 3 check, day 4 claim and pause, day 5 draw. Everything is doable by the person. It does not cover a new pipeline or invoicing other clients.
- **C4 = 9.** The first line is one action, it is under 300 words, it asks one question, and it is in Russian. A few compressed phrases ("Drawing early costs about €0.33 a day; not drawing means late rent of €650") read a little cryptically.
- **C5 = 9.** The priority order is offered for correction, the red line (one month's rent) is justified and tested against the forecast, the review day and triggers are set, and it lists what not to do today. Only the "plan works" path is missing.
- **C6 = 8.** No promises ("hard, but solvable"), the key assumption is named, and the no-income forecast is given. There is no explicit second forecast for the case where the client pays.

### Response 2
- **C1 = 6.** The arithmetic is correct (650, 7 €, 1 300 left). It brings in several outside legal and cost facts as fact (the EU directive's 40 €, cheap simplified recovery). They are accurate and hedged, but they are not from the task. The wife's salary is counted as a cushion without being labelled as an assumption.
- **C2 = 6.** It usefully lists possible reasons for non-payment and checks for client insolvency. But it frames the situation as "not critical", never names the most dangerous event or its date, and gives only a loose runway ("for at least another month").
- **C3 = 6.** Many concrete, doable steps: call the work owner and accounting separately, write to several recipients, check the registry, invoice other clients, list upcoming payments. Most have no time attached, the rent draw has no trigger day, and there are too many steps for an emergency.
- **C4 = 3.** Two major breaches: the first line is not an action, and the response is about 43 % over the word limit. It is long and sectioned, with jargon such as "B2B" and "Lever". It does answer in Russian and does answer what to do today.
- **C5 = 4.** It sets no explicit priority order for the person to choose, no red line or threshold, and only one review point (5 working days). Its only "leave untouched" is the client's systems.
- **C6 = 6.** It hedges the legal points ("the details depend on the country and the contract"). But "not critical" and "cushion for at least another month" are soft reassurances, and there are no conditional forecasts.
