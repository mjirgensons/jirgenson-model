## Step 1. Recomputed key numbers

- Turnover decline: 48,000 → 42,000 = −12.5 %; 42,000 → 37,000 = −11.9 %.
- 2026 run-rate: 25,000 / 9 × 12 = 33,333 (≈ 33,300), which is −9.9 % (≈ −10 %) vs 2025. Total slide 2023→2025 = 11,000.
- +30 % on 33,333 = 43,333 (≈ 43,300), i.e. +10,000, which is close to undoing the whole 2023–2025 slide while the population fell 8,000 → 6,000 (−25 %).
- Hourly return: 6,000 / (55 × 48 = 2,640 h) = €2.27/h; with 52 weeks (2,860 h) = €2.10/h. So about €2/h.
- 2025 costs: 37,000 − 6,000 = 31,000 (≈ 2,583/month). Profit margin 2025 = 16.2 %.
- 2026 profit bounds: costs fixed → 33,333 − 31,000 = 2,333; 16 % margin → 5,405.
- No-change 2027 ≈ 30,000 (another −10 %). Profit: costs fixed → −1,000; 16 % → 4,860. That is near zero at the pessimistic end.
- Weekly turnover now: 33,333 / 52 = 641. The +30 % target needs 43,333 / 52 = 833 per week. 700/week × 52 = 36,400.
- The lease ends in 8 months, which is the decision point. The reserve is €4,000 and no long-term liabilities are stated, so a wrong exit costs little and a long renewal on falling turnover is the costlier error.
