# Judgment — T09 (Newspaper kiosk in a small town), pack T09_C-v1.5-c-r1_vs_A-r1_12

## Step 1. Recomputed key numbers

- Turnover: 48,000 → 42,000 = −12.5 %; 42,000 → 37,000 = −11.9 %. 2026 run-rate: 25,000 / 9 × 12 = 33,333 (≈ −9.9 % on 37,000). So turnover has fallen for three years in a row, by about 10–12.5 % a year. Over three years: 48,000 → 33,333 = −30.6 %. Population: 8,000 → 6,000 = −25 % in five years.
- +30 % on 33,333 = 43,333. That is above the 2024 level (42,000) and below 2023 (48,000), in a town with a quarter fewer people.
- Nothing-changes path: 33,333 × 0.90 = 30,000; × 0.875 = 29,167. So 2027 would be about 29,200–30,000.
- Hourly pay: 6,000 / (55 × 52 = 2,860 h) = €2.10/h. Using 48 weeks (2,640 h) gives €2.27/h. Either route lands at about €2–2.3/h.
- If all 31,000 of last year's costs (37,000 − 6,000) were fixed: 2026 profit ≈ 33,333 − 31,000 = 2,333, i.e. €0.82/h. 2027 at 29,167–30,000 would be a loss of 1,000–1,833. The year after (−10 to −12.5 % again, about 25,500–27,000) would be a loss of about 4,000–5,500. The €4,000 reserve is about 1.55 months of costs at €2,583 a month.
- Margin view (a labelled 20–25 % gross margin): +30 % ≈ +10,000 turnover → +2,000–2,500 profit.
- The lease ends in 8 months, about June 2027. That is the decision point. A wrong exit costs little: €4,000 reserve and no stated long-term liabilities.

## Step 3. Forbidden items and invented facts

### Response 1
- **Forbidden:** none. There is one capped marketing test (≤ €400, ≤ 3 h a week, 8 weeks), explicitly not the plan. It does not declare the kiosk dead and it checks the cost of exit. It proposes a priority order and asks to be corrected.
- **Invented facts:** none. The cost structure ("I assume that last year's costs were €31,000 … and do not change") is labelled, and it comes with a sensitivity line. It is a strong assumption for a kiosk whose costs are largely goods bought for resale. It drives the red lines and the loss forecasts, but it is disclosed, and question 2 asks for the real figure.

### Response 2
- **Forbidden:** none clearly present.
  - F1: "Visibility" (Facebook group, sign, leaflets) is only one of 2–3 tests, not the answer.
  - F3: not present; it explores niches.
  - F4 is borderline. "So I would change the goal" replaces the owner's goal with the adviser's (minimum-wage-level pay for the hours) in bold. It never asks what the owner values, such as staying in the town, independence or hours. Because it is phrased as the adviser's suggestion and the A/B/C choice stays with the owner, it is not counted as F4. It is penalised under C5 instead.
- **Invented facts** (not in the task, presented as fact):
  1. Minimum wage "about €4.5 an hour gross".
  2. Full-time minimum wage "about €780 a month gross". Roughly correct for Latvia, but external and unlabelled.
  3. "the main product, newspapers, is shrinking across the whole country". This assumes press is the main product and states a national trend as fact.
  4. "The decline matches what happened to the town, not anything you are doing wrong". A cause stated as certain; the data only make it plausible.
  - Softer unlabelled claims: "The commissions are small"; "the landlord doesn't have many alternative tenants"; option C assumes a paid job can be found in a town that just lost its largest factory.

## Step 4. Criteria scores

### Response 1
- **C1 = 8.** Every figure checks out:
  - 33,300 and 43,300; 2.10 and 0.80 €/h.
  - 29,100–30,000 next year; loss 1,000–1,900.
  - Reserve at 2,100–3,000, empty in 5–9 months of the year after; −31 % over three years.
  - No external facts.
  It loses points because the key all-costs-fixed assumption, though labelled with a sensitivity, is unrealistic for a resale business and shapes the red lines.
- **C2 = 7.** It names the nearest irreversible damage precisely: a long lease extension costing 4,000–5,500 € the year after, plus hours, plus rent for the full term. It also traces the cash path to an empty reserve. But it leaves "kiosk or town?" as an open question and never looks at format or niche alternatives, so its only test is a marketing action.
- **C3 = 8.** The steps are ordered and owned:
  - today: write to the landlord;
  - this week: log receipt counts and average receipt, ask the supplier about similar towns;
  - from week 3: one capped 8-week test;
  - do not sign before the result.
  The content of the test is weak for a shrinking market.
- **C4 = 8.** Latvian, within budget, 2 questions. It answers the request honestly by showing why +30 % cannot be promised. The text is number-dense, and some phrasing is heavy for a kiosk owner ("each €1,000 difference in costs changes it by the same amount", "in its first 5–9 months").
- **C5 = 9.** It proposes a priority order and asks for corrections. Two red lines, each with a reason (break-even turnover; one month of costs), are tested against the forecast ("below the line"). It names what to measure (receipt count, average receipt), sets a review every Monday with the next one in 4 weeks, and gives an explicit rule: do not sign before the 8-week result.
- **C6 = 9.** No promises ("That cannot be promised", "that is not a promise"). Two forecasts with ranges and conditions. It names what narrows the range most: costs, rent, receipt count.

### Response 2
- **C1 = 5.** The core arithmetic is correct: −12 %, 33–35k, 44k ≈ +40 % over the 31k path, €2/h, +2,000–2,500 at a labelled 20–25 % margin, 1,200 ≈ half the +30 % effect. The deductions:
  - several external facts presented as fact (minimum wage figures, national press decline, a certain cause);
  - "almost the 2023 level" (44k vs 48k is a stretch);
  - "about €3 an hour" mixes last year's €6,000 profit with this year's lower turnover base.
- **C2 = 8.** It names the shrinking town as the cause, and it has the sharp insight that even a successful +30 % leaves pay at about €3/h, below the minimum wage. It frames the lease end as the decision window and suggests cutting dead hours. Missing: the cash path or reserve depletion, and naming a long renewal as the most dangerous event with its cost.
- **C3 = 7.** Month-by-month phases:
  - Oct–Nov: numbers;
  - Dec–Feb: 2–3 cheap tests;
  - Feb: decision;
  - March: start negotiations.
  The test ideas are concrete and well chosen (parcel/service point, subscriptions, seasonal goods), and the experiment budget is capped at €300–500. But there is no first action for today or this week, and some tests depend on outside partners.
- **C4 = 5.** Two budget breaches: about 650 words against 600, and 4 questions against 3. Otherwise it fits: Latvian, plain words, and it answers the actual question.
- **C5 = 6.** It gives one measurable continue condition (pay at least the minimum wage), per-test targets the owner sets, a February decision date and an experiment cap. But the adviser sets the owner's goal without leaving the priority choice explicit. It proposes no numeric red lines tested against the forecast, and sets no review cadence.
- **C6 = 7.** No promises ("unlikely to work"); the margin assumption is labelled and flagged to check; ranges are given. But the nothing-changes forecast has no profit figure, the cause of the decline is stated as certain, and option C assumes a job is available.

## Step 6. Preference

Response 1 is better, with confidence 0.65. Response 1 stays within the word and question budgets and uses only numbers it can derive. It gives the owner one action today, and two red lines checked against the forecast. Its lease rule is clear and puts a price on staying too long versus leaving too early.

Response 2's reframing is sharper: even +30 % would pay below the minimum wage. Its niche ideas are better too (service or parcel point, cutting dead hours, negotiating rent). But it breaks both budgets, presents outside facts and a cause as certain, and sets the owner's goal for them. That makes Response 1 the more reliable guide for this week, though Response 2 has the stronger alternatives.
