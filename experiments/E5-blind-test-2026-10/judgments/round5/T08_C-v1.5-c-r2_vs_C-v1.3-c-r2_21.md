## Step 1. Recomputed key numbers

- Cash: €22,000 down to €13,000 = −€9,000 over 6 months = **−€1,500/month**.
- Receivables: €8,000 up to €21,000 = **+€13,000** (about +€2,167/month).
- Paper profit: 6 × €3,000 = €18,000. If €13,000 of it is sitting in receivables, about €5,000 should have reached the bank. Cash fell €9,000 instead, so about **€14,000 (about €2,333/month) is unexplained**. Equivalently, an €18,000 expected rise against a €9,000 fall is a €27,000 gap, and €27,000 − €13,000 = €14,000.
- Reserve: €13,000 / €26,000 = **0.5 months**. A one-month red line means the shop is already in the red.
- Months to zero if nothing changes: €13,000 / €1,500 ≈ **8.7 months**.
- Implied sales (labelled assumption): €26,000 + €3,000 = €29,000/month. The 40% customer is about €11,600/month. Going from 30 to 90 days ties up 2 × €11,600 ≈ €23,200. If the whole €13,000 rise is that customer's, up to about €10,000 more could still build.
- Worse case: €10,000 extra build over about 3 months, with profit and the leak unchanged. The underlying flow is €3,000 − €2,333 ≈ +€667/month, so 13,000 + 2,000 − 10,000 ≈ **€5,000 in 3 months**. The baseline −€1,500/month trend already includes about €2,167/month of receivable build. Stacking the full €10,000 on top of the baseline double-counts it: 8,500 − 10,000 ≈ 0 is too pessimistic, and the correct figure is about €5,000.
- Plan check: collecting €10–12k gives €23–25k. Collecting €15k gives about €28k before other flows.
