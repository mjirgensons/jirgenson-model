## Step 1. Recomputed key numbers

- Cash: €22,000 → €13,000 = −€9,000 over 6 months = **−€1,500/month**.
- Receivables: €8,000 → €21,000 = **+€13,000** (≈ +€2,167/month).
- Paper profit: 6 × €3,000 = €18,000 (if the profit was €3,000 every month). Minus €13,000 held in receivables leaves ≈ €5,000 that should have come in as cash. Cash fell €9,000 instead, so ≈ **€14,000 is unexplained** (≈ €2,333/month). Possible causes: tax/VAT, loan principal, equipment, owner's drawings, stock, or a wrong profit figure. This should be asked about, not asserted.
- Reserve: €13,000 / €26,000 = **0.5 months** (about two weeks). That is red against a proposed line of 1 month (€26,000).
- Months to zero at −€1,500/month: 13,000 / 1,500 ≈ **8.7 months**.
- The 40% customer holds about 40% × €21,000 ≈ **€8,400** of receivables. If sales are about €29,000/month (expenses + profit, an assumption), their sales are ≈ €11,600/month. Losing them at unchanged costs turns +€3,000 into −€8,600/month, so €13,000 lasts ≈ 1.5 months. Months-to-zero would come forward sharply.
- Check on the identity: 3,000 − 2,167 − 2,333 = −1,500/month. This matches.

### Response 2

The specific items are framed as "usual suspects", so I do not count F2. It does quietly rule out the chance that the profit figure itself is wrong.   3. (Minor) "It takes an hour or two" for the 13-week forecast is an unsupported estimate. Low stakes.
