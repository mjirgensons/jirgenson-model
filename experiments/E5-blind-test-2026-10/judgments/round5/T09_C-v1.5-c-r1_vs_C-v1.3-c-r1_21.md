# Judgment — T09_C-v1.5-c-r1_vs_C-v1.3-c-r1_21

## Step 1. Recomputed key numbers

- Decline: 48,000 → 42,000 = −12.5 %; 42,000 → 37,000 = −11.9 %; 2026 run-rate 25,000 / 9 × 12 = 33,333 ≈ 33,300 (−9.9 % vs 37,000). Three-year change 48,000 → 33,300 = −30.6 %. Population 8,000 → 6,000 = −25 % in five years.
- +30 % on 33,333 = 43,333 ≈ 43,300, which is above the 2024 figure of 42,000. Gap ≈ +10,000/year ≈ €27/day.
- Hours: 55 × 52 = 2,860 h (gold uses ≈ 2,640 h with holidays). 6,000 / 2,860 = €2.10/h; 6,000 / 2,640 = €2.27/h. Either is acceptable.
- 2025 costs implied = 37,000 − 6,000 = 31,000. If costs are fixed: 2026 profit ≈ 2,333 (≈ €0.80/h). If costs scale with sales (16.2 % margin): 2026 profit ≈ 5,400.
- 2027 "nothing changes": 33,333 × 0.875–0.90 = 29,167–30,000. Profit −1,833…−1,000 with fixed costs; ≈ 4,700–4,900 with proportional costs.
- Savings 4,000 = 8 months of last year's profit (500/month), or ≈ 1.55 months of costs (2,583/month).
- If costs stay fixed, savings path: end-2027 = 2,100–3,000. 2028 loss = 4,000–5,500, so savings are empty 5–9 months into 2028.
- Lease ends in 8 months, ≈ June 2027. This is the decision point. A wrong exit costs little: at most a few thousand euros of profit a year at ≈ €1–2/h, with the €4,000 kept.

## Step 4. Criteria

### Response 1
- **C1 = 8.** Every figure checks out: −12.5 / −12 / −10 %, 43,000, €27/day, €2.10/h, 8 months of savings, 30,000, −1,000 / 4,900, 15 % of savings = €600. Assumptions are labelled. A minor soft spot: it never computes the current-year profit, and its €500/month exit cost uses last year's profit.
- **C2 = 8.** It names the shrinking town as the dominant force and the lease renewal as the nearest irreversible step, with an earlier notice deadline and what it would hit (rent owed regardless of sales; working for free). It does not see that this year's profit has likely already collapsed toward about €2,300.
- **C3 = 9.** Concrete, owner-doable, dated steps (this week / 15 Nov / 15 Dec): read the lease, ask the landlord in writing for a 6–12-month option, check the distributor and lottery contracts, pull monthly sales by product line, close dead hours. Reversible tests are capped at €600 and aimed at real niches (parcel point, ticket point, delivery).
- **C4 = 9.** Latvian, plain words, about 524 words, two questions. It answers the actual request by showing why +30 % runs against the trend and what would be needed instead.
- **C5 = 8.** Strengths: the priority order is proposed and open, a 2027 target and a savings red line are given with reasons and tested against the worst-case forecast, there are weekly and monthly measures, a 15 Dec review, and an explicit "do not do now" list. Weakness: the triggers lead only to "The plan changes", and the savings line cannot fire before the lease decision.
- **C6 = 9.** Two forecasts with conditional ranges that cover both cost behaviours. The plan-works range is labelled an estimate, not a promise. No guarantees.

The 8-week test is capped by money and hours, and the lease step is conditional. However, the only growth test is a generic promotion for regulars, and the timing is in relative weeks.
- **C4 = 8.** Latvian, about 532 words, two questions. It answers the request by showing why +30 % cannot be promised. Several sentences are dense chains of numbers (for example the savings-path sentence), which makes it heavier to read for a kiosk owner.

  - The priority order is proposed, includes time and savings as last to give up, and is open to change.
  - Two red lines are given with reasons (break-even revenue €31,000; one month of costs €2,600) and tested against the forecast.

That makes the loss look more certain than it is.

## Step 5. Hard checks

Both responses pass every hard check:

- **H1:** about 524 and about 532 words, both ≤ 600.
- **H2:** two question marks each.
- **H3:** Latvian.
- **H4:** no internal labels or codes.
- **H5:** not a family task, and no management jargon.
- **H6:** numbers are consistent within each response. In Response 2, "10–13 %" is the historical range and "10–12,5 %" the forecast range, so they describe different things.

## Step 6. Preference

Response 1, with confidence 0.55.

Response 1 serves this owner slightly better. It states plainly that the shrinking town outweighs any marketing. It points to the formats that could actually change the economics (parcel or ticket point, delivery) and gives dated, low-cost steps, including checking the renewal-notice deadline. Response 2 is sharper on the lease crux: it names the orderly exit as an option, costs both kinds of mistake, and gives a clear stop/continue rule. But its only growth move is a generic promotion, and it leaves the town-vs-kiosk question open, so the difference is modest.
