# Judgment — T09 (Newspaper kiosk in a small town), pair C-v1.5-c-r2 vs A-r2, #12

## Step 1. Recomputed key numbers

- Turnover decline: 48,000 → 42,000 = −12.5 %; 42,000 → 37,000 = −11.9 %.
- 2026 run-rate: 25,000 / 9 × 12 = 33,333 (≈ 33,300), i.e. −9.9 % vs 2025.
- Cumulative: 2023 → 2025 −11,000 (−23 %); 2023 → 2026 run-rate −31 %. Population 8,000 → 6,000 = −25 % over five years (≈ −5.6 %/yr).
- +30 % on 33,333 = 43,333 (+10,000). +30 % on 34,000 = 44,200.
- Trend 2027: 33,333 × 0.9 ≈ 30,000.
- 2025 margin: 6,000 / 37,000 = 16.2 %.
- Hourly pay: 55 h × 52 wk = 2,860 h → 6,000 / 2,860 = €2.10/h (with 48 weeks, 2,640 h → €2.27/h). Monthly ≈ €500.
- 2026 profit bounds: all costs fixed (31,000) → 33,333 − 31,000 = 2,333; constant margin → 33,333 × 0.162 = 5,405.
- 2027 profit bounds at 30,000: −1,000 (all costs fixed) to 4,865 (constant margin).
- Weekly turnover: 33,333 / 52 = 641; 43,333 / 52 = 833; 700 × 52 = 36,400.
- Incremental profit on +13,000 at 16–25 % = 2,080–3,250 → ≈ +€0.7–1.1/h.
- 5 % discount at 16 % margin consumes 5/16 = 31 % of profit (≈ one third).
- Lease expires in 8 months; reserve €4,000; no other liabilities stated → cost of a wrong exit is low.

It never states that the shrinking town is the strongest force or that marketing alone is unlikely to reverse it. This is a hedge, not a statement.
- **M4 = 1.** No generic marketing plan. It shows what +30 % requires: "+€10,000, almost as much as annual turnover slid in 2023–2025 (€11,000), while the town is a quarter smaller", and "The €43,300 goal needs about €830 a week; the numbers do not support that at present."

It compares the two possible mistakes explicitly: a long extension while decline continues versus leaving too early ("you lose €2,300–5,400 a year, but get back 55 hours a week"). It concludes that the long extension is the costlier mistake. Options: short extension, orderly exit, a 12-week test. Owner's time valued at ≈ €2/h.
- **M6 = 1.** Nothing changes: 2027 ≈ 30,000 €, result −1,000 to 4,900 € (both assumptions labelled). Test works: 700 €/week → ≈ 36,400 €/yr. Both have numbers and are labelled as scenarios.
- **M7 = 1.** Measurable conditions: 700 €/week for four weeks in a row means continue; an average below 640 € after 12 weeks means planning an orderly exit. Test cap is 300 € and 3 h/week, with a 4-week review. Two questions (rent and offered term; monthly income needed). The priority question is handled as a proposed order the owner can change. It does not ask what has been tried or what is growing in the town, but the questions it does ask are decision-relevant and within budget.

## Step 3. Forbidden items and invented facts

### Response 1
- Forbidden: none. There is no marketing plan (F1). It is in Latvian (F2). It does the exit-cost check and asks the distributor and lottery operator about extra services (F3). The priority order is proposed and left to the owner ("My suggestion, your choice … If the order is different, say so") (F4).
- Invented facts: none. The decision point at 5 months is tied to the contract's notice period ("earlier if the notice period … is longer"). The cost assumptions, 52 weeks and fixed rent are all labelled. One minor unlabelled simplification is that the 640 €/week baseline ignores seasonality; this is not a fact claim.

### Response 2
- Forbidden: none. The retention tactics are a sub-step, not the answer (F1). It does not declare the kiosk dead; it asks what else is sold and proposes a service test (F3). It asks whether the owner wants to keep the kiosk and lets the owner set Y (F4).
- Invented facts (minor):
  1. "people already know the kiosk exists" is given as fact for why advertising won't work. It is a plausible but unsupported cause.
  2. The comparison to "the minimum wage" uses an external benchmark not in the task. It is true and has no number attached, so it is minor.
- Imprecise claim: "on the same scale as the fall in the number of residents". Turnover is falling about 10–12 %/yr against roughly 5–6 %/yr for population; the totals are only loosely comparable (−31 % vs −25 %). The response skips the possibility that part of the decline is specific to the kiosk.

## Step 4. Criteria

### Response 1
- **C1 = 9.** All my recomputations match: 33,300, −10/−12 %, €2/h, 2,300–5,400, −1,000 to 4,900, 36,400, 830, 10/20 months. Assumptions are labelled. The only gap is the seasonality simplification in the weekly threshold.
- **C2 = 8.** It names the nearest irreversible damage precisely: a long lease extension on falling turnover, with savings as the only cushion and a dated decision point. It shows +30 % means reversing three years of decline in a town a quarter smaller. It stops short of naming the shrinking town as the dominant force and leaves that as a question.
- **C3 = 9.** Day 1 / day 3 / day 5 actions the owner can take alone (hourly sales log, emails to landlord and distributor), then a 12-week test capped at 300 € and 3 h/week with Monday logging. Cheap and reversible actions come first.
- **C4 = 8.** In Latvian, within the word and question budgets, and it answers the real question by explaining why the +30 % framing fails. The prose is dense, with many short headings and some telegraphic sentences ("My suggestion, your choice: the last thing to give up is the savings…"), which slightly hurts readability for a kiosk owner.
- **C5 = 9.** It has a proposed priority order with the choice left to the owner, a red line of 2,000 € tested against the forecast (200 €/month burn reaches it in 10 months), continue and exit thresholds (700 / 640 €/week), weekly measurement, a 4-week review and a "don't do now" list. The reason given for the red line is thin.
- **C6 = 9.** There are no promises. It uses ranges with stated conditions, gives two forecasts, and says plainly that the numbers don't support 43,300.

### Response 2
- **C1 = 7.** The arithmetic is correct (44k, 13–14k, €2/h, €500/month, +2–3k, 5/16 ≈ one third, +10 % vs trend). The 2026 range leans optimistic, the population comparison is imprecise, and one causal claim is unsupported.
- **C2 = 8.** Strong diagnosis: the town is short of customers, not short of advertising, and even full success leaves the owner at about €3/h, well below minimum wage. It also notes that fixed costs are eating profit. The lease is treated as a negotiation item rather than flagged as the most dangerous near-term event.
- **C3 = 7.** The steps are sensible and the owner can do them (category and hour analysis, cutting 55 → 45 h, one repeat-visit service, lease negotiation). Timing is only by month, the lease step has no date, and the retention step drifts into marketing tactics.
- **C4 = 6.** Language and length fit, and the tone is plain and warm. The question budget is breached (five requests against a limit of three), and it partly carries out the marketing request it argues against.
- **C5 = 5.** Placeholder thresholds (X, Y), no proposed priority order, no review cadence. It does have a decision date (March), a "don't invest before renewal" rule and "savings are an exit cushion, not ad money".
- **C6 = 7.** Honest and hedged ("most likely", "may"), with no promises. The scenario structure is incomplete (no profit forecast if nothing changes, no outcome range for its own plan), and the 300–500 € tests have no payback logic.
