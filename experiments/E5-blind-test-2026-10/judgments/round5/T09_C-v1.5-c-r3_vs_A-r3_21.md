# Judgment — T09 (C-v1.5-c-r3 vs A-r3, pair 21)

## Step 1. Recomputed key numbers

- Revenue: 48,000 → 42,000 = −12.5 %; 42,000 → 37,000 = −11.9 %; 2026 run-rate 25,000 / 9 × 12 = 33,333 (≈ 33,300) = −9.9 % vs 2025. Three-year change 2023 → 2026 ≈ −30.6 %. Population 8,000 → 6,000 = −25 % over five years (≈ −5.6 %/yr), so revenue is falling about twice as fast per year as population.
- +30 % on 33,333 = 43,333, which is about the 2024 level (42,000): it means reversing roughly three years of decline in a town that is still shrinking.
- Hourly pay: 6,000 / (55 × 48 = 2,640 h) = €2.27/h; with 52 weeks (2,860 h) = €2.10/h.
- If costs other than the owner's pay are treated as fixed: 37,000 − 6,000 = 31,000/yr ≈ 2,583/month. Then 2026 profit ≈ 33,333 − 31,000 = 2,333 (≈ €0.82/h over 2,860 h). 2027 nothing-changes ≈ 30,000, so profit ≈ −1,000 ("near zero", as the key says). 2028 ≈ 27,000, so ≈ −4,000.
- 2026 average monthly revenue 25,000 / 9 = 2,778, which is ≈ 7.5 % above the 2,583 monthly cost level. Savings 4,000 ≈ 1.55 months of costs.
- The lease ends in 8 months, which is the decision point. A wrong exit costs little: at most about 2,300–6,000/yr of profit before pay, the €4,000 reserve and no stated long-term liabilities.

## Step 2. Mandatory elements

**Response 1**
- M1 = 1. Fluent, natural everyday Latvian (informal "you" form). It explains "margin" in plain words and uses none of the banned terms.
- M2 = 1. It gives "by ~10-12% each year" and "about €33-35 thousand for the whole of 2026". The upper bound of 35k is not derived (the run-rate is 33.3k), but the range contains the correct value.
- M3 = 1. It says "This is not a problem of too little advertising but a shrinking customer base, and advertising does not bring it back", so the shrinking town is named as the force that marketing cannot reverse.
- M4 = 1. No generic plan is given as the answer. It shows what +30 % requires: "back to the 2024 turnover, while the town has a quarter fewer people. You would have to turn −10% into +30%."
- M5 = 0. Several parts are present: the lease is named as "your real clock" with a decision by month 5, the options are given (cheap tests capped at 300–500 € with the 4,000 € kept as a cushion, no renewal or only a shorter and cheaper one, exit "without losses"), and time is valued at ~2 €/h. What is missing is the cost of a wrong exit: the response never weighs it or says it is low. This is the reason the element exists.
- M6 = 1 (weak). The nothing-changes forecast is "2027 will be about €30 thousand". The plan-works range is "stop the decline ... +5-10%". Neither scenario says what happens to profit (near zero in 2027), and the +5–10 % is framed as a target rather than a forecast.
- M7 = 0. The lease criterion has no number ("do not reach a specific level"). The 6–8-week rule for dropping tests has no threshold. The closing paragraph asks for four separate items, which in substance exceeds the ≤3-question budget.
- M8 = 1. About 519 words.

**Response 2**
- M1 = 1. Latvian, polite "you" form, none of the banned words. The style is dense and telegraphic in places ("Within 3 days, you: from the till, the number of receipts…"), and phrases like "the share of fixed costs" and "red line" are still understandable.
- M2 = 1. It gives "falling by about 10% a year" and "this year about €33,300 (… I assume the other months are average)".
- M3 = 0. The population figure 8,000 → 6,000 appears only as an open check ("Whether the decline is the whole town's … or the kiosk's own needs to be checked"). The response never names the shrinking town as the strongest force. It does say marketing is unlikely to help ("there is nothing … showing that marketing will achieve it"), but that does not cover the environment part of the element.
- M4 = 1. It says "+30% of €33,300 is €43,300 … €43,300 is a hope, not a plan" and gives no marketing plan. It shows the required level against a 10 %/yr decline, but less vividly than Response 1, since it does not connect the figure to a return to 2024 levels.
- M5 = 1. The lease is named as the decision point in the first line. The cost of a wrong exit is weighed explicitly: "Staying too long: … about €1,000 of loss a year and your 2,860 hours. - M7 = 1. Monthly revenue is compared with 2,600 € and with the same month last year, the next review is in 3 months, and the stop rule is revenue or savings below 2,600 € (the revenue clause is elliptically worded). It asks 2 questions. It does not ask the key's question about what the owner would give up last, but it proposes a priority order and invites correction.
- M8 = 1. About 532 words.

## Step 4. Criteria

**Response 1**
- C1 = 6. The core arithmetic is correct and the 25 % margin is labelled as an assumption. The 35k upper bound is not derived, the claim that revenue falls "at the same pace" as population is wrong, and the minimum-wage comparison and the customer-loyalty claim are unlabelled.
- C2 = 7. It finds the real problem: a shrinking customer base, a +30 % target that contradicts the data, about €2/h pay, and the lease as the clock. It does not project the profit or cash path, so it never says when the kiosk stops paying anything.
- C3 = 7. The steps are ordered and timed (2–3 weeks of data, 3–4 months of tests within 300–500 €, drop after 6–8 weeks, decide by month 5) and the owner can do them alone. It skips the urgent contract check (the notice deadline), and the lease criterion is left for the owner to define.
- C4 = 7. Natural, warm Latvian within the word budget, and it answers the request honestly by reframing it. The closing list asks for four items, one more than the question budget allows in substance.
- C5 = 5. Keeping the reserve, the budget cap, the per-test monthly tracking and the month-5 deadline are good. The priorities are not made explicit, there is no numeric red line, and nothing is tested against the forecast.
- C6 = 7. It makes no promises, says plainly that +30 % is unrealistic, and uses ranges. The forecasts cover revenue only, and the plan-works case is a target rather than a conditional forecast.

**Response 2**
- C1 = 9. Every figure checks out: 33,300; 31,000 and 2,600 a month; 2,300 and 0.80 €/h; 2,780 and +7 %; 1.5 months of reserve; +11 %; −1,000 and −4,000; 3,700 and 1,100. The assumptions are labelled. The all-costs-fixed assumption is strong but is flagged as a range driver.
- C2 = 8. It names the nearest danger precisely: monthly revenue will hit the break-even level of about 2,600 € in about 8 months or sooner, which is exactly when the lease ends, and the reserve covers only 1.5 months. It sees the contradiction in the +30 % target. It leaves the shrinking town as an open check rather than naming it as the main force.
- C3 = 8. Each step has a time and an owner (3 days, 7 days, months 1–3, "you"). Fast, reversible actions come first: till data, finding the contract's notice date, asking the landlord in writing for terms, and asking the supplier how other outlets are doing. The 1–3-month new-format work is thin: ask 20 regulars and check whether services are available.
- C4 = 7. Latvian, within both budgets, with 2 questions, and it answers the actual request. The prose is compressed and occasionally awkward ("the savings fall below their red line … and run out"), and a kiosk owner would need more effort to read it.
- C5 = 9. It proposes a priority order and leaves the choice to the owner ("correct it"), sets red lines with reasons, tests them against the forecast (when the line is reached, the reserve at lease end), sets monthly measures and a 3-month review, and lists what not to do. Using the same 2,600 € for two different red lines is slightly confusing.
- C6 = 9. It gives no guarantee ("a hope, not a plan"), offers two numeric forecasts with conditions, and names the factor that would narrow the range.
