# Judgment — T10 (C-v1.5-c-r1 vs A-r1, pair 21)

## Step 1. Recomputed key numbers

- Rent shortfall: 1 100 − 450 = **650 €**.
- Credit line cost: 12 % a year ≈ 1 % a month. 650 € → ≈ 6.5 €/month; 700 € → 7 €/month; 1 000 € → 10 €/month (≈ 0.33 €/day). Cheap, fast and reversible.
- Client debt: 3 800 / 1 100 ≈ **3.45, about 3.5 months of rent**. The bigger danger is the client defaulting or paying very late, not this month's rent.
- Runway if no money comes in: 450 + 2 000 = 2 450 €. After rent 1 (day 6): 1 350. After rent 2 (about day 36): 250. Rent 3 (about day 66): short by 850 €.
- If 1 000 € is drawn on day 5: balance 1 450, and 350 left after rent.
- People are not in immediate danger, because the wife covers food and children. Housing is what to protect first.

The credit line and the call/email do appear later, but the EMERGENCY first-line rule is broken, and the written demand has no backup with a trigger day in the first lines.
- **M2 = 1.** "You are short €650 (1,100 − 450). Taking €700 from the line for a month costs about €7". Correct, and it says the cost is trivial.
- **M3 = 0.** Rent comes first in the order, but the response never says that housing or family is protected first. This is only an implicit gesture.
- **M4 = 0.** The only "don't" is "Don't switch off or delete anything in their systems". There is no warning about payday or expensive loans or selling equipment. The response also tells the person to pause new work and the handover of results today, which is the opposite of the required "don't stop work without written notice".
- **M5 = 1.** "If the silence continues — after 5 working days a formal demand…". It gives a day and a trigger, though the timing is loose, and it is not tied to when the rent is drawn and paid.
- **M6 = 1.** Section 3 raises possible insolvency (registry, bankruptcy, liquidation, complaints from other contractors, lawyer, creditor queue), and section 5 and the closing line add a new-pipeline and concentration follow-up. It does not quantify the debt (≈ 3.5 months of rent) or call it the larger danger outright, but the substance is there.
- **M7 = 1.** It asks no questions (no question marks). The closing line is a request for information.
- **M8 = 0.** It is in Russian, but about 407 words, well over 300.

## Step 3. Forbidden items and invented facts

### Response 1
- **F2: present.** Over 300 words.
- **F4: present (mitigated).** Under "The order for today" it says "Put new tasks and the handover of results on pause until payment or a firm date". That means stopping new work and withholding deliverables today, before any written deadline has run out. "Check the contract first" and "only new work" soften it, but it is still an immediate work stoppage.
- F1 and F3: absent.
- **Invented facts:**
  1. EU Directive 2011/7/EU entitles the person to late-payment interest and at least 40 € compensation per invoice. This is a real rule, hedged with "If you are in the EU" and "the details depend on the country and the contract", but it is not in the task and is presented as fact.
  2. "simplified recovery of undisputed debts (in many EU countries this is inexpensive)". A general claim about cost, not from the task.
  3. "positions in the queue of creditors are lost quickly". A general legal claim presented as fact.

  There is also one unlabelled assumption: "The remaining limit … together with your wife's salary is your cushion for at least another month" assumes there are no other outgoing payments and that the wife's salary can absorb more than food and children.

### Response 2
- No forbidden items. The "pause in new work" comes only on day 4, after an unanswered written demand, and comes with a contract check, so it is not F4.
- No invented facts. The day-3 deadline is labelled as the responder's own ("the deadline is mine, name your own"). The rent being monthly and there being no other payments is labelled ("I assume that…").

## Step 4. Criteria

### Response 1
- **C1 = 6.** All of its arithmetic is correct (650, about 7 € on 700, 1 300 left). However, it adds several non-task legal and cost claims as facts (accurate and hedged, but not labelled as assumptions) and one unlabelled cushion assumption.
- **C2 = 6.** It sees that the rent is solvable and that the client is the real problem, and it lists plausible causes including insolvency. It never names the most dangerous event with a date (the runway running out by rent 2–3) and never quantifies the 3 800 € exposure.
- **C3 = 6.** The steps are concrete and doable (call the requester and accounts separately, email several people, check the registry, invoice other clients). But there are too many for an emergency "today", the timing is loose (draw "closer to the payment date"; "3–5 working days"), and the work pause is not the fast, reversible move.
- **C4 = 3.** It breaks the EMERGENCY mode twice: the first line is not an action, and it is about 407 words against a 300-word budget. It is in Russian and asks no unwanted questions.
- **C5 = 4.** The order is imposed rather than offered. There is no red line tested against a forecast, and only a loose next check ("after 5 working days"). "What to leave untouched" is limited to not deleting things in the client's systems.
- **C6 = 6.** It makes no guarantees and hedges the legal points. "not critical" and "cushion for at least another month" are mild reassurances with no conditions, and it gives no forecast.

### Response 2
- **C1 = 9.** Every number checks out (650; 2 450; 1 350 → 250 → −850; 1 000 → 1 450 → 350; 10 €/month; 0.33 €/day), and the assumptions are labelled. A couple of phrases are slightly opaque ("this is the lowest balance, day 6").

It explores the causes of non-payment less (only "whether the client is paying other contractors").
- **C3 = 8.** The sequence is clear and dated: today, send the written demand, use a second channel and check the line pays out at once; day 3 is the check; day 4 is the formal claim plus pausing new work; day 5 is the 1 000 € draw. The reversible lever is placed correctly. It is missing actions on other clients or new work.
