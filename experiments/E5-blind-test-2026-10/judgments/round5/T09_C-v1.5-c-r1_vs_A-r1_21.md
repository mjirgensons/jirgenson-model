# Judgment — T09 (Newspaper kiosk in a small town), pair C-v1.5-c-r1 vs A-r1, #21

## Step 1. Recomputed key numbers

- 2023 → 2024: 48,000 → 42,000 = −12.5 %. 2024 → 2025: 42,000 → 37,000 = −11.9 %.
- 2026 run-rate: 25,000 / 9 × 12 = 33,333 € (≈ −9.9 % vs 2025). 2023 → 2026: −30.6 %.
- Population 8,000 → 6,000 = −25 % in five years.
- +30 % on 33,333 = 43,333 €. That is above the 2024 level (42,000) and about 4,700 € below 2023 (48,000).
- No change in 2027: 33,333 × 0.90 = 30,000; × 0.875 = 29,167. So about 29,200–30,000 €.
- Owner's hourly return in 2025: 6,000 € over 55 × 48 = 2,640 h (≈ 2.27 €/h) or 55 × 52 = 2,860 h (≈ 2.10 €/h). About 500 €/month.
- If costs stay at 37,000 − 6,000 = 31,000 €, the 2026 profit is ≈ 2,300 € (≈ 0.80 €/h). The 2027 no-change result is then a loss of ≈ 1,000–1,900 €, which matches the gold's "profit near zero".
- Reserve 4,000 € ≈ 1.5 months of 31,000 €/yr costs (2,583 €/month).
- The lease ends in 8 months (≈ June 2027). That is the decision point. The cost of a wrong exit is low, because the reserve is intact and no other liabilities are stated.

## Step 4. Criteria

### Response 1
- **C1 = 6.** The core arithmetic is right: −12 %, 33–35k, 44k, ≈ 40 % above trajectory, ≈ 2 €/h, ≈ 500 €/month. There are minor slips. "≈ 3 €/h" after +30 % adds the extra profit to last year's 6,000 € rather than this year's lower base, giving about 2.5–2.8 €/h. "Almost the 2023 level" is loose (44k vs 48k). Several unlabelled external facts are stated (minimum wage, national press decline, landlord's weak position). None of this changes the advice.
- **C2 = 7.** It finds the real problem well: a shrinking town, plus an hourly return so low that even +30 % would not fix it. It treats the lease end as an opportunity rather than naming a long renewal as the most dangerous event. It also misses that this year's profit is already falling much faster than revenue.
- **C3 = 8.** Month-by-month blocks: Oct–Nov get the numbers straight at no cost, Dec–Feb run 2–3 cheap tests with preset targets, February decides, March starts lease talks. The tests are concrete and doable by the owner, including the service point and press delivery for older customers. There is no "today" action.
- **C4 = 5.** It answers the real question in Latvian with everyday words. It breaks two budgets: about 650 words against ≤600, and 4 questions against ≤3.
- **C5 = 6.** It has a decision date, an experiment budget cap, and the reserve kept as a cushion. Kill criteria exist, but the owner has to set them. There are no numeric red lines tested against the forecast and no review cadence. The goal is re-set to minimum-wage-level pay without asking what the owner values (staying in the town, independence).
- **C6 = 7.** No promises: "Even if the target worked, it would not be enough" and the margin assumption is labelled. Ranges are given. The no-change forecast has no profit consequence. The riskiest assumption (cost structure) is asked about but not named as such, and the cause of the decline is asserted rather than tested.

### Response 2
- **C1 = 8.** Every number checks out against the stated assumptions: 43,300; 2,300 €; 2.10 → 0.80 €/h; 29,100–30,000; loss 1,000–1,900; reserve 2,100–3,000; empty after 5–9 months of 2028; −31 %. All assumptions are labelled. The only issue is that the all-costs-fixed assumption is the extreme case, and the early-exit cost ignores alternative earnings. These are modelling choices, not errors.
- **C2 = 8.** It names the nearest irreversible damage precisely: a lease renewal in 8 months while revenue is below costs. It shows what that would hit (losses, 2,860 hours, the reserve path). It spots operating leverage: profit is falling far faster than revenue. It frames town-vs-kiosk as the key open question but does not commit to the environment as the dominant force.
- **C3 = 8.** "Today: ask the landlord in writing…" is a fast, reversible, informative first step. This week: log the daily receipt count and average receipt, and ask the supplier. From week 3: one test for 8 weeks, ≤400 € and ≤3 h a week. "Do not sign it until there is the 8-week result". The owner and timing are clear throughout. Weakness: the only test is an advertising measure, with no niche or service-format idea.
- **C4 = 8.** Within the word and question budgets, Latvian, polite register. Some sentences are dense and numeric to the point of being hard to read for a kiosk owner (the priority line, the "the year after next" construction).
- **C5 = 9.** Priorities are proposed and the choice is left to the owner. Red lines (31,000 € revenue, 2,600 € reserve) come with reasons and are tested against the forecast. It says what to measure (receipt count, average receipt), how often (daily logging, every Monday), when the next review is (in 4 weeks), and what limits apply (400 €, 3 h a week, no signing).
- **C6 = 8.** "That cannot be promised", "that is not a promise", "a test, not a plan". Two forecasts with conditions. It names what narrows the range (costs, rent, receipt count). It docks slightly for presenting the early-exit cost in gross terms, which makes leaving look dearer than it is.

## Step 6. Preference

**Response 2**, confidence 0.65.

Response 2 stays within the word and question budgets. It makes the lease renewal the explicit danger, with numeric red lines tested against a two-scenario forecast, a "do not sign before the 8-week result" rule, and a today/this-week/weekly-review rhythm.
