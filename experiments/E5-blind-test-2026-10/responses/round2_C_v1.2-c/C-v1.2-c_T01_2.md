## Where you stand

Over a full year the shop earns about €4,600 (€118,000 revenue, €35,400 parts, €78,000 fixed costs with your pay inside). But it all comes in summer: about +€3,300 a month in season, about -€3,700 a month in winter. Your €9,000 is 1.4 months of fixed costs.

## The biggest danger

Winter, before the e-bikes. I am assuming October is an average month. Then, if nothing changes, the account is about €12,300 on 31 October, €8,600 end of November, €4,900 end of December, €1,200 end of January. It runs out in early to mid February, and the gap reaches about €6,200 by end of March. The first things you could not pay are the mechanic and the rent.

I propose a red line of one month of fixed costs (€6,500). The forecast crosses it in December, so it is an alarm already ringing. The €12,000 for the e-bikes is more than you hold, and about 2.6 years of the shop's current profit.

This holds if I am wrong by 10%: winter revenue 10% off moves March by €1,400; an October of €10,000 instead of €14,000 makes it €2,800 worse.

## What to do

This week:
- Do not sign or pay a deposit for the e-bikes.
- Decide your own pay for November to March. Pausing it saves €7,500, which covers the €6,200 gap with only €1,300 to spare.
- Check whether the mechanic's contract allows fewer hours in winter, and ask your landlord about lower winter rent.
- Sell winter work: prepaid spring tune-ups and winter servicing. Each extra €1,000 of winter revenue leaves €700 after parts. Sales alone would need about €1,770 more a month (+44%), so they help but will not carry it.

By 31 October: ask the supplier about staged delivery or leasing, and check whether your insurance covers rental e-bikes and what it adds to the €1,000.

## What we protect first

My proposed order (yours to change), last to give up first: the shop open with the mechanic and rent paid; then your own pay; then the e-bikes, first to go. If you would rather protect your pay and cut the mechanic's hours, the savings come from him, and you risk losing him before the season.

## What not to do now

No e-bike purchase while cash is under the line. Do not borrow for them before you know what one bike earns. At 10 bikes each must net €1,200 just to repay the price, before financing or servicing.

## What to watch

Every Monday: the account balance. Next review 1 November, with October's actual result. Trigger: if the account is below €9,300 on 31 December, cut deeper.

## Two forecasts

If nothing changes: about -€6,200 at end of March (-€4,800 to -€7,600 for winter revenue ±10%).

If the plan works (pay paused plus €1,000 a month more winter revenue, which is my target, not a promise): about €4,800 (€3,400 to €6,200). With only half the extra revenue, about €3,050. It dips just under €6,500 in February, so the mechanic and rent checks are your extra cushion. Growth waits until the balance is back above the line. If April and May bring an average +€3,300 each, a first batch of 4 bikes (€4,800) leaves about €6,600. That is 4 bikes, not 10.

The range narrows most with: what one rental e-bike earns per day and how many days a season you could rent it, whether the mechanic's hours can flex, and how October is actually going.
