The biggest weak spot: goals 3 and 4 cannot both happen. A 20% price cut turns your €2,500 monthly profit into a €1,100 monthly loss. Here is everything else, most important first.

## 1. The tax arrears are not a goal

€9,000 "sometime this year" has no date. It is a debt to someone else, so it stays outside the trade-offs and the rest is planned around it. Spread over 12 months it is €750 a month, 30% of today's profit. Check with the tax office which date and terms apply, and whether a payment plan is possible. That sets the real deadline. Paying too late risks penalties; paying too fast drains cash you need elsewhere. Do this first this week.

## 2. The price cut destroys the margin it is meant to protect (goals 3 and 4)

Each member brings €150 and costs about €30 to serve (€3,600 / 120). The other €120 goes towards fixed costs of about €11,900. At €120 a member, only €90 is left.

- With 120 members after the cut: revenue €14,400, costs €15,500, a loss of €1,100 a month.
- If the cut applies to existing members too, it costs you €3,600 a month.
- To get back to today's €2,500 profit you need 160 members, 40 more.
- For a 25% margin: about 145 members at €150 (+25), or about 199 at €120 (+79, roughly two thirds more).

Nothing in the plan shows that members leave over price. Check how many left in the last six months and what they said.

## 3. Spending items work against the 25% margin (goals 5, 6, 7)

Every extra €1,000 a month in fixed costs needs about 8 more members at €150, or 11 at €120, before margin improves. You gave no cost for the two teachers, the second room or the app. I also do not know whether your current room is full. If it is not, extra room and teachers come before extra members, not with them. The app is a method: it needs a goal it serves.

## 4. Too many goals, and several are wishes

Eleven goals split your attention; three to five are usually enough for a year. Goals 1 and 2 are one goal (more members mean more revenue), and neither has a number or a date. The same holds for 10 and 11. Items 3, 5, 6 and 7 are methods, not goals. Tie each to what it serves:

- the price cut to members;
- teachers and the room to capacity and to working less yourself;
- the app to whichever of those it helps.

Goal 8 is a good goal; decide who counts complaints and from what.

## 5. Smaller points

- I do not know whether your own pay is inside the €15,500. If it is not, the real margin is lower and the 145 members rises.
- "More revenue" reached through a price cut is the opposite of "margin 25%".
- Working less yourself and opening a second room pull in opposite directions in year one.

## Where you must choose

The price cut and the 25% margin cannot both be kept. Keeping the cut means a loss for now and about 160 to 199 members before it pays. Keeping the margin means holding the price and finding the other answer to the new studio. Which would you give up first?

## What is missing, and what to check first

The missing number is your cash in the account. Without it I cannot judge the spending items or whether the arrears can wait. In order:

1. Ask the tax office for the exact terms on the €9,000.
2. Count the members who left in the last six months and why.
3. Check how full your room is, and get a monthly cost for each teacher and for the second room.
