[English translation. The original answer was written in Latvian.]

Working 55 hours a week and seeing a decline for three years is hard. In short: your own numbers do not support +30% next year with marketing alone. What will help you more is stopping the decline and making a decision with numbers in hand by the end of the lease.

**Where you are**
Turnover is falling by about 12% a year (48 → 42 → 37 thousand €). €25,000 in nine months gives about €33,000 a year (I assume the months are similar; if December is stronger, up to €35,000). +30% of that is €43–45 thousand, so more than in 2024 (€42,000), when the factory had already closed. Last year's €6,000 for 55 hours a week is about €2 an hour (assuming 52 weeks). The €4,000 savings are about 1.5 months of expenses (I assume expenses are turnover minus profit, about €2,600 a month).

**The biggest threat**
The end of the lease around June 2027, when the savings are thin and income is falling. The town has lost a quarter of its residents and its largest employer; you cannot change that, and it is stronger than any advertising. A mistake in both directions: by extending for a long time, you tie yourself to fixed rent while profit may drop to zero or below; by leaving too early, you lose income (€6,000 last year) before you know whether the decline can be stopped. Check in the lease by what date you must give notice of not continuing (it may be earlier than the end) and whether the press and lottery contracts are tied to these premises.

**What to do**
- By 23 October (you): break down the 2025 and 2026 turnover by month and by press, lottery and other; you will see what is falling.
- For two weeks, count customers at the till by the hour; the empty hours can be closed and restored if needed.
- By 16 November: ask 10 regular customers what they now buy elsewhere; check whether parcel handout or payment services would give a commission here.
- Marketing only after you know what is falling, and no more than €300 by 31 January (my suggestion; the savings stay above €3,700). Two small tests: an offer in the local residents' group and to regular customers.

**What to protect first**
I am putting this in order for you, correct me if it is different. I would give up last the savings and having no debts; then the income from the kiosk; then your time; I would drop +30% first. For the savings I suggest a red line of €2,600 (about 1 month of expenses): you are above it now, but close. In the worst 2027 case it would be crossed.

**What not to do now**
Do not sign a long lease and do not buy equipment before you have numbers on the products. Do not extend the opening hours.

**What to measure**
Every Monday: the week's turnover against the same week last year, the number of customers per day, savings. Next check on 16 November. If the savings drop below €2,600 or the tests by 31 January do not improve the comparison with last year, we plan without an extension.

**Two forecasts**
- If nothing changes: in 2027 €29–30 thousand (the decline continues), profit from about −€1,500 to €4,800 depending on how much of the costs are fixed.
- If the plan works: the decline stops, €32–35 thousand. I cannot promise €43–45 thousand, and without a new source of income it is not in sight.

The forecast narrows the most with the breakdown of turnover by product, the rent and terms, and how much of the costs are fixed.

Two questions: how much is the rent per month? How much money do you take for yourself from the kiosk each month?
