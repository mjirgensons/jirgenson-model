The biggest problems: a tax bill written as a goal, a price cut that turns your profit into a loss, and a margin target with no route to it. Today you make €2,500 a month, a margin of about 14%. Each member brings you €120 after their own cost of about €30, and your fixed costs are about €11,900.

**1. The €9,000 tax arrears are a bill, not a goal.**
"Sometime this year" gives it no date. Spread evenly it is €750 a month, 30% of today's profit, so it must be planned first and spending comes after it. Check with the tax office this week whether interest or penalties are running, whether the notice carries a deadline, and whether an instalment plan is possible. A deadline in the notice is the one date here you cannot move; I cannot price a late payment without it.

**2. The 20% price cut works against four of your other goals.**
At €120 and 120 members, revenue is €14,400 against costs of €15,500: a loss of €1,100 a month, €13,200 over the year, with nothing left for the arrears. Each member then brings €90 instead of €120. To break even you need 133 members; to keep today's €2,500, 160; to reach a 25% margin, 199. Revenue only returns to €18,000 at 150 members. So the cut contradicts "higher revenue", the margin goal and the arrears.

It pays only if, without it, you would lose more than 30 of your 120 members. Do you have any evidence of that, such as members who have left or said they will?

**3. The 25% margin by December has no route.**
At today's prices and fixed costs it takes 145 members, 25 more than now. This holds if the €30 per member is off by 10% either way (150 members at the high end). Every extra €1,000 a month for a teacher, room or app needs 8 to 9 more members on top. None of your spending items has a cost in the plan, so the target cannot be checked against them.

**4. Five goals are wishes.**
"More members", "higher revenue", "work less" and "better reviews" have no number or date. "Fewer than 3 complaints" has a number but no starting point. "More members" and "higher revenue" are also one goal, since revenue is members times price.

**5. Four items are methods or spending, not goals.**
The price cut, the app, the two teachers and the second room are ways to reach something. Each needs a goal it serves and a monthly cost. Eleven goals split your attention; three to five are enough for a year. The room, the app and the hires also take your own time at the start, which pulls against "work less". I am assuming that, since you have not said otherwise.

Keeping the price cut means 160 members just to stand still on profit. Keeping the 25% margin means 145 members at today's price. Which would you give up first, the price cut or the margin target?
