## What the numbers show

No honest analysis can give you an exact monthly profit or a guaranteed payback before the salon has opened. Here is what your own figures support.

The second salon costs at least €5,000 a month (rent €1,800 plus two technicians at €1,600). I am assuming supplies, utilities and insurance add about €1,000; this is a placeholder, not checked for you, and every €1,000 of difference moves the monthly result by €1,000. That puts break-even at about €6,000 of revenue, just over half of your first salon's €11,000. Check whether €1,600 is the full cost to you or comes before employer charges.

| Second salon's revenue | Per month |

Payback has to cover the €28,000 fit-out plus the opening losses. I am assuming six months at half of salon one's revenue (-€3,000 in total) and no loan interest. Then payback is about 20 months from opening at three quarters, about 12 months at equal revenue, and never at half. So the range is 12 to 20 months if it works, and the worst end is no payback.

## The greatest danger

The fit-out is €13,000 more than your whole reserve of €15,000, which is 1.7 months of the first salon's expenses. I propose a red line at one month of expenses, €8,800. Paying more than €6,200 of the fit-out from savings crosses that line on the day the invoices are paid. If the plan is to pay from savings, the alarm is already ringing, and nothing would be left to carry the opening months.

## What to do

This week: get the fit-out quote in writing with a price ceiling and a payment schedule, and check the lease's length and exit terms. Before signing anything: have the full €28,000 secured without taking the reserve below €8,800. Two months of expenses (€17,600) is safer. Hire the second technician only when bookings fill the first one's time.

Do not sign the lease or pay a deposit until the financing is settled.

What I would protect, last to first (my proposal; tell me if yours differs): the first salon running and your €2,000 pay, then the reserve above €8,800, and the second salon is the first thing to delay.

Check weekly the second salon's revenue against €6,000, and monthly the reserve. If revenue is still under €5,500 after month six, stop adding hours or staff and rethink.

If nothing changes (savings pay for the fit-out, revenue stays at half): about -€500 a month, no payback, reserve near zero. If it works: 12 to 20 months.

The range narrows most with the fit-out's financing and cost, the second salon's other monthly costs, and how full the first salon's appointment book is today.
