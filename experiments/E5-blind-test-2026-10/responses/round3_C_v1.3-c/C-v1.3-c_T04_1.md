## Where you stand

I am sorry about the second client; it is a hard message to get. Your income averages €6,500 a month (€19,500 over three months). Your biggest client brings about €3,575 of it, the departing one about €975. Without the departing client you are at about €5,525 a month, and the biggest client's share rises from 55% to about 65%. To reach €10,000 you need about €4,475 more a month, 81% above that level.

I am assuming these figures are takings before your employee's pay and other costs, and that your 55% and 15% refer to the same figures. If not, the gap changes, not the picture.

## The greatest danger, and one safe step

The danger is your biggest client, not the €975. If they also cut back or move work to a machine, income would fall to about €1,950 a month (the remaining 30%). The second client's move is a signal to test: check which of the biggest client's texts are routine enough for a machine. I cannot date this danger yet; questions 2 and 4 will.

"Stop depending on one client" has no number yet, so I propose one, as my suggestion: no client above 40% of income. If the biggest client stays at €3,575, reaching €10,000 puts them at about 36%, so your two aims fit together as long as the growth comes from new clients.

Safe step now: write to the departing client and ask, in writing, for the date of their last order and whether they would want someone to check the machine's output. Hold off on any decision about your employee until I have your answers.

## Five questions

1. If you had to give things up one after another, what would go first and what last: your own pay, keeping your employee, the €10,000 target, or less dependence on the big client?
2. On what date does the second client's work stop?
3. How many months of all your fixed costs, your employee included, would the money in your account cover?
4. How much notice must your biggest client give you before stopping work?
5. Which languages and types of text bring in most of your income?
