**What your numbers show**

Your income averages €6,500 a month (range €6,100 to €6,900). By your shares, the biggest client brings about €3,575, the second about €975, and all others together about €1,950. I am assuming "income" means money invoiced before costs, and that the shares fit the three-month average. If income is after costs, the picture is tighter.

When the second client leaves, you drop to about €5,500 a month, and the biggest client becomes about 65% of it. The €10,000 target then means about €4,500 more a month than you will have.

"Stop depending on one client" needs a number. I propose: no client above 30% of income. Note that at €10,000, today's biggest client alone would still be 36%, so both goals fit together only if the other clients grow while the biggest one does not.

**The danger and the first step**

The second client is already lost. The real danger is the biggest client cutting or ending its work. Without its €3,575 you would be at about €1,950 a month, and that cannot carry an employee. The shift that took your second client may reach the biggest one too. This is a hypothesis to check, not a fact. I cannot yet say how long your money would last, so I will set a red line for your reserve once I have your answer to question 1.

This week, ask the second client in writing for the date of their last order and any finished work still to be invoiced. In your next talk with the biggest client, ask how much translation work they expect to send you over the next 12 months. Their answer shows whether the danger is real. Do not change your employee's terms or take on new fixed costs until you know more.
