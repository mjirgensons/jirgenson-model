**What your numbers show**

No honest analysis can give an exact profit or a guaranteed payback before the salon has clients; here are ranges.

The second salon costs €5,000 a month before it earns anything (rent €1,800, technicians €3,200). I am assuming materials and other running costs of 12% of revenue (an illustration), so it breaks even at about €5,700 revenue a month, or about €6,250 if those costs are 20%. Your first salon leaves €2,200 a month after your €2,000 pay; the reserve is €15,000.

The danger is the €28,000 fit-out: nearly twice your reserve, and irreversible. Paid from the reserve, it leaves the first salon, which pays you, with no cushion. My proposed red line is one month of costs for both salons, about €13,800; you are €1,200 above it, so taking more than that from the reserve crosses it at once.

**What to do**

- Do not sign for the fit-out until the €28,000 is found outside the reserve.
- Check whether you can open with one technician: break-even drops to about €3,900.
- Check whether €1,600 is your full cost with contributions; each extra €500 adds about €570 to break-even.

Order I used, my proposal: last to give up, the first salon and your €2,000 pay; first to give up, the second salon.
