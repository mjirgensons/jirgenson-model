**The tax arrears come first**

The €9,000 is not a goal to trade against the others; it is a debt, and "sometime this year" is not a date. Assuming 12 months, it is €750 a month, about 30% of your current profit of €2,500. This week, ask the tax office for the exact amount including interest or penalties, and whether they will agree a written instalment plan. Until then, I would not sign for a second room or new teachers.

**What is wrong, most important first**

1. **The price cut works against goals 2 and 4.** After variable costs (€3,600 ÷ 120 = €30) each member leaves you €120; after the cut, €90. If the cut covers everyone, revenue falls from €18,000 to €14,400 and you lose €1,100 a month from day one (fixed costs about €11,900). Break-even moves from about 100 members to 133; keeping today's €2,500 profit needs 160, which is 33% more. A 25% margin needs about 145 members at today's price, but about 199 at the cut price. Which would you give up first, margin or member count? That decides whether the cut can stay.

2. **Nothing guards against the real danger: members leaving for the new studio.** Your profit is about 21 members (17%) from zero. I propose a red line at 105 members (profit about €700 a month); a studio that takes 15 members crosses it. The plan has no cancellation figure, so you would learn of it late, and I cannot test the line against a forecast without one.

3. **Goals 5, 6 and 7 have no cost, and each eats margin.** Your margin today is about 14%; 25% means €4,500 profit at 120 members, €2,000 a month more than now. Every extra €1,000 of monthly fixed cost needs about 8 more members just to stand still. An app, teachers and a room are ways to serve more members, not goals, and make sense only if your room is full at busy hours.

4. **Eleven goals split your attention.** Three to five are enough for a year. Four of yours are methods (price cut, app, teachers, room), and goals 1 and 2 are one goal: members times price.

5. **Several goals are wishes without numbers.** "More members": 145 is the bare minimum for the margin; 155 gives a cushion of about 10 members. "Work less" needs your hours a week now and a target; "better reviews" needs a rating now and a target; "fewer than 3 complaints" needs today's count, and a fixed count gets harder as members grow.

I assumed the €3,600 grows with members, fixed costs stay put, and your own pay is inside the €15,500. The conclusions hold with a 10% error in these figures; if your pay is outside the costs, the real margin is lower.
