**1. The tax arrears are a duty, not a goal**

"Sometime this year" has no amount or date, and unlike the others it cannot be dropped if money gets tight. €9,000 is 3.6 months of today's profit. This week, contact the tax office: ask whether interest or penalties are running and whether they accept instalments, and get the arrangement in writing. For scale, €750 a month clears €9,000 in 12 months (30% of today's profit, before any interest).

**2. The 20% price cut costs more than it looks**

At €120 a member you keep €90 after serving costs, not €120. With the same 120 members, revenue falls €3,600 a month and profit goes from +€2,500 to −€1,100 (−€13,200 a year), leaving nothing for the arrears. You would need 133 members to break even and 160 to get back to today's profit; the 25% margin would need 199, against 145 at today's price. Even if it kept every member, the cut beats doing nothing only if more than 30 of your 120 would otherwise leave. At today's price you can lose about 20 before profit reaches zero. Nothing you gave me shows how many have left or asked about the new studio: check that first.

You gave no costs for them, but each extra €1,000 a month of fixed cost needs about 8 more members at today's price (11 at the cut price) just to stand still. Which would you drop first: the price cut, the 25% margin, or the new teachers and room?

**4. Eleven goals split your attention**

Three to five are enough to steer by for a year; the rest become ways to reach those, or wait. Four of yours are ways to reach a goal, not goals: the price cut, the app, the two teachers and the second room. Draft targets: members from 120 to 145 by December; your weekly hours now and the target; one review site, its average rating now and the target; this month's complaint count.
