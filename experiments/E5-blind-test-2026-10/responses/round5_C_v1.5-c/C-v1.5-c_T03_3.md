Today you make €2,500 a month, 13.9% of €18,000. Fixed costs are €11,900; each member brings €150 and costs about €30, so leaves €120. On those numbers, goals 3, 4 and 9 cannot all work as written.

**1. The €9,000 tax arrears are a duty, not a goal**

"Sometime this year" has no monthly amount and no date, so nothing in the plan pays it. It is 3.6 months of today's profit, and goal 3 removes that profit (point 2). Keep it out of every trade-off. First step, you, within three days: ask the tax office in writing for an instalment plan. €750 a month clears it in 12 months, 30% of today's profit. Also check whether interest is running and what the office does after a missed payment.

**2. The 20% price cut turns your profit into a loss**

At €120, your 120 members bring €14,400 against costs of €15,500: a loss of €1,100 a month, before any teacher, room or app. Getting back to €2,500 needs 160 members (+40, +33%); breaking even needs 133 (+13). Both survive a ±10% error in the €3,600.

The cut is a method. The goal behind it is keeping members against the new studio. You gave no figure for members lost, so check: count cancellations and new enquiries since it opened, and ask leavers why. If the loss is real, you want to know that before you give up €3,600 a month on the members you already have.

**3. Goals 1, 2 and 3 pull against each other**

Revenue is members times price. After the cut, revenue gets back to €18,000 only at 150 members (+30, +25%). With a fixed price, goals 1 and 2 are the same goal under two names.

**4. A 25% margin by December needs many more members**

At €150 you need 145 members (profit €5,500 on €21,750), 25 more than now. At €120 you need 199, which is 79 more (+66%). Each extra €1,000 of monthly fixed cost adds 12 members at €150, or 17 at €120. I am assuming teachers, room and app add fixed costs; you gave no figures. Check how many members your room and teachers can hold: that tells you whether 145 or 199 is possible without the second room.
