Losing a client is hard; here is what your numbers already show.

**Where you stand**

Your income averages €6,500 a month. Your biggest client is about €3,575 of that, the leaving client about €975. Without the leaving client you are at about €5,525 a month, 15% less, and your biggest client becomes about 65% of what remains. Reaching €10,000 then needs about €4,475 a month of new work, 81% more than €5,525. I am assuming these are invoiced amounts, before your employee's pay and other costs; if they are after costs, you are better off.

**What is most dangerous**

The €975 is a loss; the larger danger is your biggest client. If it left too, income would fall to about €1,950 a month, which I cannot yet set against your costs. By a red line I propose (no client above 50% of income, because above it one departure takes more than half your income), you are already beyond it at 55%.

Two more points. Even at €10,000, a biggest client who stays at €3,575 would still be 36% of your income, so your two goals are not the same goal. And the leaving client may not be a one-off: your biggest client may be weighing the same move. I have no sign of it; it is a hypothesis to test: ask them this week how they plan to handle translation over the next 12 months.

**One step for now**

This week, you write to the leaving client and ask them to confirm in writing the date of their last job and to settle any open invoice. It fixes the date your income drops. Do not hire, dismiss or change prices yet.
