**In short.** By your own numbers, +30% next year (about €43,300) is not a well-founded goal, and I would not spend the savings on advertising. Instead: a cheap 12-week test and a timely decision on the lease.

## Where you are

This year will come to about €33,300 (€25,000 in nine months; I assume the last three months are average, ±10% does not change the conclusion). That is −10% against last year, before that about −12% a year. Last year's profit of €6,000 at 55 hours a week (I assume 52 weeks) is about €2 an hour. This year's result before your pay: from €2,300 (if expenses did not change) to €5,400 (if profit stayed at 16% of turnover).

## The biggest danger

The end of the lease in 8 months: a long extension for a turnover that is falling, and the €4,000 savings as the only cushion. If the decline continues, in 2027 turnover is about €30,000, the result from −€1,000 to €4,900 (the same two assumptions). +30% means +€10,000, almost as much as annual turnover slid in 2023–2025 (€11,000), while the town is a quarter smaller. The main question: is the demand of the whole town falling, or your kiosk in particular? If the whole town's, a smaller kiosk or an exit has to be considered, not only advertising.

## The lease decision

Decision point: in 5 months, earlier if the notice period in the lease is longer. The cost of mistakes:

- A long extension, the decline continues: rent for the whole term and 55 hours a week for a result that may be −€1,000.
- Leaving too early: you lose €2,300–5,400 a year, but get back 55 hours a week.

Assuming the rent is fixed, the long-extension mistake is the more expensive one. A short extension is the middle way; an organised exit (selling off stock, handing over contracts) is a real option.

## What to do

This week, you yourself:

- On day 1: for two weeks write down what is sold by the hour, to see which of the 55 hours earn money.
- On day 3: an email to the landlord: the terms after the term ends, whether a 12-month extension is possible; check the notice period in the lease.
- On day 5: an email to the press distributor and the lottery operator: how similar points in small towns are doing; what additional services (parcel handout, bill payments) may be offered and for what fee.

Then 12 weeks: choose one service for the test. Ceiling: €300 from the savings and 3 hours a week. Every Monday write down the week's turnover.

## What to protect

My suggestion, your choice: the last thing to give up is the savings (red line €2,000; below it the way back becomes hard to reverse), then your time, then the kiosk; the first thing to put off is +30%. If the order is different, say so.

## What not to do now

Do not spend the savings on advertising, do not take a loan, do not sign a long lease before the decision.

## Forecasts

If nothing changes: in 2027 about €30,000, the result −1,000 to €4,900. If a shortfall for living were covered from the savings, every €200 a month brings them to the red line in 10 months, to zero in 20 months.

If the test works: €700 a week (now about 640) four weeks in a row gives about €36,400 a year. The €43,300 goal needs about €830 a week; the numbers do not support that at present. If after 12 weeks the average is below €640, we plan an organised exit. Next check in 4 weeks.

## Questions

1. How much is the rent per month, and for how long will the landlord offer an extension?
2. How many euros a month do you need to get from the kiosk to live on?
