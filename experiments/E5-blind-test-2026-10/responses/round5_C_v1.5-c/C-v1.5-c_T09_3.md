**The main point:** turnover is falling by about 10% a year, and in 8 months you have to decide about the lease; start with that, not with +30%. I understand that this is hard.

**Where you are**
Turnover €48,000 (2023), €37,000 (2025), this year about €33,300 (€25,000 in nine months; I assume the other months are average). Costs without your pay last year: 37,000 − 6,000 = €31,000, about €2,600 a month. If they do not change (an assumption), this year's profit is about €2,300, or about €0.80 an hour (2,860 hours a year, 52 weeks). The €4,000 savings are 1.5 months of costs.

**The biggest danger**
The kiosk is approaching the level where it earns nothing (my red line): monthly turnover of €2,600, which only covers costs; this year on average €2,780, only 7% above. If the decline continues, you will reach the line in ~8 months or earlier, exactly when the lease ends. Whether the decline is the whole town's (residents 8,000 → 6,000) or the kiosk's own needs to be checked.

**What to do**
+30% of €33,300 is €43,300. An honest analysis cannot guarantee that, and there is nothing in your numbers showing that marketing will achieve it. My option: a goal of €37,000 (+11%, gives back last year's €6,000 profit); €43,300 is a hope, not a plan.
- Within 3 days, you: from the till, the number of receipts and the average receipt by month (2023, 2026); start recording sales by the hour; ask the supplier how sales have changed at other points.
- Within 7 days, you: find in the lease the last date for giving notice of not continuing; ask the landlord in writing for the rent and a 6–12 month extension that can be ended with notice of up to 3 months.
- In months 1–3, you, up to 3 h a week and €300: ask 20 regular customers what they buy elsewhere, and offer that; check whether services are available for the kiosk (parcels, payments, tickets).

Protect the longest: time and health, then the savings, then running the kiosk without losses; the first to drop is +30% (my order, correct it). Do not: a long lease, a loan, growth purchases, advertising above €300.

**The lease decision in 8 months**
Staying too long: fixed rent while turnover is below costs, about €1,000 of loss a year and your 2,860 hours. Leaving too early: about €2,300 of profit a year and the spot. The more expensive mistake is a long lease (the savings are 1.5 months), so I recommend a short extension with an exit or an orderly closure (check whether the contracts and the stock can be handed over or sold). At the end of the lease the savings are about €3,700 (after the €300 test, if you use the profit as your pay), €1,100 above the red line (one month of costs, €2,600).

**Forecasts and check**
If nothing changes (costs the same): next year about €30,000 and a loss of about €1,000 before your pay; a year later a loss of about €4,000, the savings fall below their red line at the start of that year and run out. If the tests work: €33,300 (the decline stops) to €37,000, profit €2,300–6,000. The range is narrowed by the rent, the share of fixed costs and the receipt data.
Every month: turnover against the same month last year and against €2,600, the savings. Next check in 3 months; if turnover for two months in a row or the savings are below €2,600, we prepare an orderly closure.

**Questions**
1. Is the kiosk your only income?
2. How much is the rent per month, and what term and exit does the landlord offer?
