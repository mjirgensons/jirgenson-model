## Where you are

The business earns money, but the cash is stuck with your customers. Six months of €3,000 profit should have added €18,000 to the bank; instead it fell €9,000, to €13,000. That is a €27,000 gap. Unpaid invoices grew €13,000 (to €21,000), about half of it. Your numbers do not explain the other €14,000: ask your accountant what left the account that is not in costs (your own drawings, loan instalments, VAT, equipment).

€13,000 is half a month of your €26,000 costs. I propose a red line at one month of costs (€26,000) and comfortable from two (€52,000). You are already under it: the alarm is ringing now.

## The greatest danger

Your biggest customer not paying at all. I am assuming sales of about €29,000 a month (costs plus profit), so they are about €11,600 a month. A customer sliding from 30 to 90 days late may not pay; ask them why. If they pay nothing from now and you keep delivering, the rest brings about €17,400 against €26,000 of costs: minus €8,600 a month, and the €13,000 lasts about six weeks. Also check whether the rise in unpaid invoices is spread over several customers: then your whole market is slowing and the plan changes.

## What to do

- **Today (you):** email the biggest customer a written demand: your overdue amount, payment within 7 days, or a written part-payment schedule with dates. Phone their accounts contact too.
- **Day 1 (you):** send every other overdue customer a demand with amount and date.
- **Day 2 (you, with your accountant):** list what left the account beyond costs. Check whether your contract allows late-payment interest.
- **Day 7 (you), only if no payment or schedule:** that customer's new work only with half paid in advance. Too soft: another month of their work, about €11,600 more unpaid against €13,000 in the bank. Too hard: you risk a customer worth about €11,600 a month.
- **This month (you):** ask for 30-day terms and a deposit on large new jobs.

My proposal; tell me if yours differs: wages, tax and VAT, and loan instalments are not negotiable, then supplies, then rent. You give up your own drawings and purchases first. Not now: new machines, hires, pay rises, discounts to the big customer.
