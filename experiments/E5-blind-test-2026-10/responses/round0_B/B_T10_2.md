**What your numbers show**
In the account €450, in 6 days rent of €1,100. You are short €650. The client has owed €3,800 (that is more than three rents) for 30 days already. The credit line of €2,000 at 12% costs roughly €10 a month for each €1,000 taken. Food and the children are covered by your wife's salary, that is your insurance.

**Main risk**
The issue is not the debt itself but the timing: the rent will come due earlier than the client pays. The second risk: almost all your income is tied to one client.

**Today**
1. Close the gap with the credit line. I would draw €1,000 (650 for the rent and about 350 as a buffer). That is ~€10 a month. First check the withdrawal fee and the repayment terms. How much to take is for you to decide.
2. Call the client, don't write. Preferably the accounts department or the project manager, not only the person who is silent. One question: "When will the invoices for €3,800 be paid?" Write down the date they give.
3. Right after the call, send a short email: the invoice numbers, the amount, a request to name a date within 3 working days. Put the accounts department or the manager in copy.
4. Find the contract: payment term, late-payment penalties, the right to suspend work.

**This week**
- No date by the third day: send an official demand (formal demand) with the amount and a deadline of 5–7 days. In most EU countries, for late payment between companies there are statutory interest and compensation of costs (at least €40 per invoice). Check the rules of your country.
- If you are still working for this client: do not hand over new stages without payment, if the contract allows it.
- Ask other clients whether you can get an advance or earlier payment.

**This month**
- No payment after the deadline in the demand: a lawyer, debt recovery or simplified court proceedings. Whether to do this against your main client is for you to decide, because the cooperation will most likely end.
- In new contracts: an advance of 30–50%, payment within 14 days, no single client more than half of income.

**Two forecasts**
Assumptions: your own expenses apart from rent ~€200 a month, no other income, your wife covers the family.
- *Nothing changes:* after the rent ~€350 in the account with a debt of €1,000 on the line. By the next rent (in ~5 weeks) you will have to draw another ~€1,000, and the line will be almost used up. Margin: 5–6 weeks. Interest €10–20 a month.
- *The plan worked (the client pays within 2–3 weeks):* about €4,000 in the account, after repaying the line and interest (~€10) ~€3,000 remains. That is roughly 2 months of rent and expenses. If they pay only half (€1,900), the next rent is still covered.

These are estimates, not guarantees. I cannot predict when the client will pay.

**What to track**
- In 3 days: whether the client has answered and named a date.
- Day 6: the rent is paid, how much is left in the account.
- Day 10–12: the reaction to the official demand.
- If more than €1,500 has been taken on the line and there is no money from the client, look for urgent temporary income.
