**What your numbers show**
- In the account €450, rent of €1,100 in 6 days: you are short €650.
- The client owes €3,800 — that is 8 times more than your balance. This is a timing gap, not a lack of income.
- Food and the children are covered by your wife's salary, so there is only one urgent payment — the rent.
- The credit line closes the gap: €1,000 at 12% for a month costs about €10.

**Main risk**
Not the debt itself, but the client's silence: the money may come in 2 weeks, in 2 months or in parts. Plan as if it will not come tomorrow.

**Today**
1. Take €1,000 from the credit line (at least €650, the rest is a buffer). The transfer may take 1–2 working days, so don't put it off. The amount is your decision.
2. Call the client, don't only write. In parallel, send an email to the accounts department or the manager: "Invoices No. … for €3,800 are 30 days overdue. Please confirm the payment date by [in 3 days]. Otherwise I will send a formal demand with a late-payment penalty charged."
3. Find in the contract the payment term, the size of the late-payment penalty and the right to suspend work.

**This week**
- No answer within 3 days — a written formal demand by post and by email with a specific payment date.
- Whether to continue working for this client and on what terms (for example, prepayment) is for you to decide.
- If you have other clients, invoice them for finished work and ask for an advance for new work.

**This month**
- No payment after 14 days — a final demand and a choice of recovery method (lawyer, simplified proceedings for undisputed debts, agency). If the client is from the EU, directive 2011/7/EU gives the right to a late-payment penalty and compensation from €40 — check how this works in your country.
- In new contracts: an advance of 30–50%, a payment term of 14 days, the late-payment penalty written in.

**Two forecasts**
Assumptions: the 30 days are the delay after the payment due date; the business has almost no other expenses; there are no fees on the line; your wife still covers the family.
- If nothing changes (the client stays silent): after the rent you have about €350 left, in a month another rent is due, and you will take roughly another €750 from the line. The balance of €450 plus the line of €2,000 cover exactly two rents (2,450 versus €2,200), there is no money for the third. Interest over this time — roughly €10–30.
- If the plan works (payment within 1–3 weeks): €3,800 comes in, you repay €1,000 and pay interest of about €2–10, and roughly €3,100 remains in the account until the next rent. If only part comes in, the line can be repaid partly.

**What to track**
- In 2 days: the money from the line is in the account.
- In 3 days: whether the client has answered.
- In 6 days: the rent is paid.
- In 14 days: whether the payment has come; if not, move to recovery.
- Alarm: more than €1,500 has been taken from the line and there is no payment.
After that, check the balance and the line once a week.

One question the text of the formal demand depends on: which country is the client in, and what does the contract say about the payment term and the late-payment penalty?
