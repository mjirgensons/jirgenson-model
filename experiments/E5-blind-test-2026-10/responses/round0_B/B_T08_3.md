**What your numbers show**

You are profitable on paper but short of cash, because part of the profit is sitting in unpaid invoices.

- Six months of profit is about €18,000, yet your bank fell €9,000. That is a €27,000 gap.
- Unpaid invoices grew €13,000. That explains about half the gap, most likely your big customer paying late. In effect you are lending them money for free.
- The other €14,000 (about €2,300 a month) left the bank without counting as a cost. Typical causes are owner withdrawals, loan repayments, equipment, VAT or tax payments, and paper stock. I can't tell which from your numbers.
- €13,000 is only half a month of your €26,000 costs.

**Biggest risk**

Cash runs out before the big customer pays. If they pay even later, or stop paying, the gap gets worse fast.

**What to do**

*Today:* List what the big customer owes, oldest invoice first. Phone their accounts payable (don't email). Ask for a payment date for each invoice and a part payment this week.

*This week:*
- Chase every other overdue invoice.
- Ask your bank what overdraft or credit line you could use. Ask while your balance still looks healthy.
- Ask your accountant to list everything paid from the bank that isn't a cost, to find the €14,000.
- Decide whether to pause new work for the big customer until the oldest invoices are paid. That protects cash but risks the relationship, so it is your call.

*This month:* Put new terms for that customer in writing: shorter payment time (for example 30 days), a deposit on big jobs, and late-payment interest. In most EU countries you have a legal right to interest and a fixed fee on late business invoices. Check the rules in your country.
