**What your numbers show**

- Turnover has been falling for the third year in a row, by 10–12% a year (€48 → 42 → 37 thousand). In 2026, €25,000 for nine months is about €2,800 a month versus €3,100 last year. At the end of the year it will come to about €33,000 (32–35 thousand, depending on December).
- Last year's profit of €6,000 for about 2,900 working hours (55 × 52) is about €2 an hour. That is well below the minimum wage.
- 30% growth on €33,000 is about €43,000, so more than in 2024, when the town was bigger. You would need about €10,000 extra, roughly €30 every working day.

**The biggest risk**

The lease in eight months. Signing a new lease with the same rent for a business that gives about €2 an hour and keeps shrinking is the biggest money decision, more important than any advertising. The marketing results need to be known before this decision, not after it.

**What to do**

*Today (1–2 hours)*
- From the till data, write out the monthly turnover for the last 24 months, separately: newspapers, lottery, other goods. Without this you cannot know where to aim.
- Open the lease and write down the rent amount and the deadline by which you must give notice of non-renewal.

*This week*
- Ask the landlord on what terms the lease can be renewed (length, rent). Without promising anything yet.
- Choose 2–3 cheap trials. Which to choose is up to you; ideas:
  - a list of regular customers on WhatsApp or SMS: a message when a new magazine or holiday goods arrive;
  - a newspaper subscription with home delivery in the nearest streets, especially for older people;
  - services that bring people to the window (parcels, bill payments, tickets), if a commission is paid for them;
  - posts in the local Facebook group: opening hours, news, what's on offer.
- Lottery advertising has restrictions. Before you promise or put up anything, check with the lottery operator what is allowed.

*This month*
- Launch 1–2 trials, in total up to €300–500 (my assumption, about a tenth of the savings). Keep the remaining €3,500 in reserve.
- Every week, write down the turnover and compare it with the same week in 2025.

**Two forecasts for 2027**

Assumptions: of each extra euro of turnover you keep about 25–30 cents, rent and other fixed costs do not change, working hours stay at about 2,900.

- If nothing changes: turnover €29–31 thousand, profit before your pay €3,500–4,500, about €1.2–1.6 an hour. If the rent goes up, even less. (2026 estimate: about €5,000, about €1.7 an hour.)
- If the plan works: turnover €34–37 thousand, profit €5,000–6,000 after marketing costs, about €1.7–2.1 an hour.
- If the full +30% is achieved: turnover about €43,000, profit about €7,000–7,500, about €2.5 an hour. That is unlikely, and even then the profit per hour stays below €3.

There are no guarantees; these are ranges from your numbers and my assumptions.

**What to watch and when to check**

- Weekly turnover versus the same week last year. Now the decline is about 10%; if it shrinks to 5% or less, something is working.
- On 15 December: the first check, in the middle of the holiday season.
- At the end of February: if January and February are not better than the same months last year, you will not reach 30% with marketing alone. Then it is worth seriously comparing "renew at a lower rent" and "do not renew". The decision is yours.
- At the latest two weeks before the deadline by which you must notify the landlord.

Two questions that could change the recommendation: how much is the rent per month and how far in advance must you give notice? And what percentage of turnover is newspapers, lottery and other goods?
