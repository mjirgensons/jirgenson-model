**What your numbers show**
- Turnover is falling for the third year: €48,000 → 42,000 → 37,000. €25,000 for nine months means about €33,000–35,000 this year (−5 to −10%; the end of the year may be stronger, I don't know that).
- Last year's profit of €6,000 over ~2,800 working hours is about €2 an hour before taxes.
- This year's profit is most likely about €4,700–5,500.

Assumptions: each euro of turnover changes profit by 25–35 cents; rent and other costs do not change.

**The biggest risk**
Renewing the lease. A new lease for several years on the current terms ties you to a business that is falling by about 10% a year. Marketing does not remove this risk. +30% means €43,000–45,000, so more than in 2024, while the town keeps shrinking. Nobody can promise that. And even if it worked, it would give only about €2.5–3 an hour.

**What to do**
*Today:* open the lease and write down by what date you must give notice of extension or termination, and what the terms are.

*This week:* from the till, take out the 2025 and 2026 turnover by month and by group (newspapers, lottery, the rest), and if possible also by hour. You will see what is falling and which hours are empty.

*This month:*
- Try 1–2 cheap steps, in total €300–500 (about 10% of the savings). Examples: a service that brings people in (parcel pick-up, bill payment; find out the commission from the service provider), a discount card for regular customers, home delivery of newspapers for older people, a post in the local group and on the shop window. Which to choose is up to you.
- Start a conversation with the landlord about lower rent or a short extension (6–12 months), based on the fall in turnover.

**Forecast for 2027**
- If nothing changes: turnover €30,000–33,000, profit about €3,500–5,000 (less than €2 an hour).
- If the plan works (+30%): turnover €43,000–45,000, profit about €7,000–8,300 after marketing costs.
- Partly worked out (a more realistic outcome): turnover €34,000–37,000, profit about €4,500–5,700.

**What to watch and when to check**
Every month, compare the turnover with the same month last year. If the decline shrinks from ~10% to 5% or less, the steps are working. The first check at the end of January, the second in March, when you have to decide about the lease. Whether to continue and for what pay, you decide.

One question that can change the calculation: how much does the rent cost per month, and is the end of the year usually stronger?
