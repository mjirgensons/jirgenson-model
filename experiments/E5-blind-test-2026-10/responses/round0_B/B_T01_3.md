**Where you stand**

- In season you keep about **€3,300 a month**: €14,000 − €4,200 parts − €6,500 fixed costs (your pay included).
- In winter you lose about **€3,700 a month**: €4,000 − €1,200 − €6,500.
- Over a year that is 7 × 3,300 − 5 × 3,700 = **+€4,600**. It is positive but thin, and the profit comes in summer while the losses come in winter.

**Biggest risk**

Your €9,000 does not last the winter, even without the e-bikes. If October is an average month, you start November with about €12,300 and the account hits zero around February. By end of March you are about €6,200 short. If October is weak (say €10,000 revenue), you are about €9,000 short. The €12,000 for the bikes would push the shortfall to roughly €18,000–21,000 unless it is financed.

**Two forecasts (to next October)**

| | Nothing changes | The e-bikes work |
|---|---|---|
| Lowest point (end March) | about −€6,200 | about −€18,200 if paid in cash |
| Cash, end of next October | about €16,900 | about €12,250–19,600 |

Both assume you can bridge the winter gap. For "the e-bikes work" I assumed they add €1,500–3,000 revenue a month for the 7 season months, nothing in winter, 70% kept after parts, and no extra fixed costs. That pays the bikes back in 0.8 to 1.6 seasons. To pay back in one season you need about €2,450 extra a month (+17%).

**What to do**

*Today:*
- Do not pay or sign for the bikes yet. The account cannot carry it.
- Check the €9,000 is really free: any VAT, tax or supplier bills due this winter?

*This week:*
- Ask your bank for an overdraft or credit line now, while the account is positive and you have no loans. Ask what it costs.
- Ask the e-bike seller about leasing, instalments or paying in March, and about delivery time.

*This month:*
- Choose how to cover winter. Each €1,000 of extra winter revenue adds €700. Closing a €6,200 gap takes about €1,770 more revenue a month (+44%), or about €1,240 a month less in fixed costs, or a mix.
- Decide how many bikes. Each costs €1,200 and needs about €1,700 of extra season revenue to pay back in one season. Whether that means 10, fewer, or waiting is your call.

**What to watch**

Compare your balance on the 1st of each month with this path: 1 Nov €12,300, 1 Dec €8,600, 1 Jan €4,900, 1 Feb €1,200. If you are more than €1,500 below it, winter is going worse than this plan. Check again on 1 November (the real October result) and 1 January (winter sales, the bank's answer, the financing terms). Set the final order date from the delivery time you are given.

**Assumptions**

October is average, winter is flat, there is no VAT or tax timing effect, and I added no financing interest.

**One question that would change the decision:** what day rate do you expect for an e-bike, and how many days a season could each be out? At €40–60 a day, each bike needs roughly 29–43 paid days to pay back in one season.
