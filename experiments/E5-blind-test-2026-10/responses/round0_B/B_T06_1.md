**What your numbers show**
- Salon 1 leaves €2,200 a month after your €2,000 pay.
- Salon 2 has known costs of €5,000 a month (rent €1,800 plus two technicians at €3,200). Supplies, utilities, card fees and insurance come on top. I assumed total costs of €6,200-7,400 a month. The low end is €1,200 extra. The high end is what salon 1 costs, with the higher rent. Both are before tax and loan interest.
- So salon 2 breaks even at about €6,200-7,400 revenue a month, which is 56-67% of what salon 1 earns. Every €1,000 above that is roughly €1,000 profit.
- I assumed €1,600 is the full cost per technician. If employer contributions come on top, add about €500-1,000 a month.

**Biggest risk: cash, not demand**
The fit-out is €28,000 and your reserve is €15,000, so you are €13,000 short before the first customer. A new salon rarely fills on day one. I assumed revenue climbs from about €3,000 to €7,000 over six months, which means a loss of €7,000-14,000. You would need €35,000-42,000. You have €15,000 plus about €13,000 of salon 1 surplus over six months, roughly €28,000. The shortfall is €7,000-14,000, and it leaves your reserve at zero, with salon 1 as the safety net.
