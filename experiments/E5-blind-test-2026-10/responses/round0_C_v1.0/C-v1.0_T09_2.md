With marketing alone, +30% next year is not realistic: the town is shrinking, and advertising will not bring back those who have left. More important is what you get out of the turnover, and the end of the lease in June. The first step: read the lease and write down the date by which you must give notice of extension. Your workload is heavy, and the shrinking of the town is not your fault.

**Where you are**

Turnover has been decreasing for three years already, by roughly 12% a year. 2026 is heading for about €33,000 (25,000 ÷ 9 × 12; 10% less than last year), if the end of the year is not stronger. The town has lost a quarter of its residents. +30% on this level is about €43,000, or €10,000 more a year. Your profit last year was €6,000 for roughly 2,860 hours: about €2 an hour or €500 a month. Savings €4,000, the lease ends around mid-June.

**The biggest threat**

The end of the lease in June. If you sign a long lease at the same price while turnover falls by about 10% a year, the only cushion is the savings. If the lease renews automatically or requires notice a couple of months in advance, the deadline may come as early as spring. The second threat is spending the savings on advertising. So the order: the lease, working hours and the offer, only then marketing.

**What to do**

By 22 October:
- Read the lease: notice period, automatic renewal, price.
- Start writing down receipts by hour: how many customers, how many euros (two weeks).

By 15 November:
- Based on the records, close the hours when there are no customers. No cost, reversible.
- Ask two operators about the commission: parcel pick-up, bill payment, tickets. Take the one that brings people in at least once a week, and announce it in the local Facebook group and at the door.
- For marketing and the new services together: no more than €500 and 3 hours a week.

By 15 January:
- Lease talks: a lease for 1 year, a lower price or one that depends on turnover, an option to end it earlier.

**What to protect first**

This order is suggested by me; if the kiosk matters more to you than the income, tell me, and we will be able to give more ground in the lease talks. First to protect are the savings and a life without debts (red line €3,000; now €4,000, with the €500 cap €3,500 remains). Then your income and health: at least €6,000 a year with fewer than 55 hours a week until the end of 2027; the forecast reaches this only with lower rent. Then the kiosk itself. Turnover growth drops out first.

**What not to do now**

Do not sign a lease for longer than a year before the talks, do not take a loan, do not spend over €500 and do not increase your hours.

**What to measure**

Every Monday: account balance, number of receipts and total for the week. Every month: turnover versus the same month last year. The next check on 2 November. If for three months in a row turnover is more than 15% below last year's or the savings fall below €3,000, we change the plan: smaller premises or ending it.

If nothing changes: in 2027 turnover about €30,000 (28,000–32,000), profit before your pay €3,900–4,600, or €330–380 a month; at the worst end about €3,300.
