Before marketing: +30% means about €43,000 next year, more than in 2024, when the factory was still working. I cannot promise that, and the savings should not be spent on it. A more realistic target: stop the decline and by the end of the lease know clearly whether the kiosk is worth it for you. It is not your fault: the shrinking of the town is the strongest factor, and advertising will not stop it.

## Where you are

Turnover is falling by 10–12% a year: €48,000 → 42,000 → 37,000, this year about €33,000 (€25,000 in nine months, recalculated for the year; a ±10% error does not change the conclusions). Last year the profit was €6,000 for 55 hours a week (costs about €31,000): about €2.10 an hour or €500 a month, considerably below the minimum wage (I assume before taxes). This year the profit is most likely lower: from €2,000, if costs have not shrunk, to €5,400, if they have shrunk along with sales.

## The biggest threat

The end of the lease around June 2027. If you sign a lease for several years at the current price, the costs will stay, but turnover will keep falling; if the rent goes up, the already small profit may disappear. The €4,000 savings are one and a half months of costs. I suggest a red line: below €2,600 (one month). You are €1,400 above it; if nothing changes, in the worst case for 2027 a loss of up to €2,000 would cross it.

## What to do

- This week (you): for two weeks, write down the receipts by hour: how many receipts and how big the average receipt is. This will show whether customers are disappearing or purchases are getting smaller, and which of the 55 hours earn nothing.
- By 31 October (you): ask the landlord on what terms they would be ready to extend the lease; ask for a term of up to a year or lower rent.
- By 15 November (you): ask 10 regular customers what they now buy elsewhere, and add one or two of these goods. Also find out whether courier firms pay for parcel pick-up at your kiosk.
- By 1 February (you): give up the hours in which, according to the receipt data, the kiosk earns nothing.
- For advertising, in total no more than €300 and 3 hours a week.

## What to protect and what not to do now

I suggest this order, starting with what I would give up last: 1) savings above €2,600 and no debts; 2) income for your hours, not lower than last year's €2.10 an hour (so cutting hours is acceptable); 3) your health and time; 4) turnover growth, which I would give up first. If your order is different, tell me, and I will recalculate how long it is worth continuing with the kiosk.

Do not: do not sign a new long-term lease before March; do not take a loan for advertising or for renovating the kiosk; do not spend the savings below €2,600.

## When to check and what to expect

The first check on 2 November, based on the receipt data; after that, once a month, turnover versus the same month's figure last year, savings once a week. The decision on the lease by 31 March. If by then turnover is still more than 10% below last year's or the savings are below €2,600, we look at another place with more foot traffic, fewer hours, or closing.

If nothing changes: in 2027 turnover about €29,000–30,500 (a decline of 8–13%); with costs of about €31,000 that is a loss of €500–2,000, and in the worst case the savings fall to €2,000, below the red line.

If the plan works: the decline stops, turnover €31,000–34,000, and the profit per hour at least holds, because the working hours become shorter. Conditions: the new goods or the parcel point really bring in customers and the rent does not go up. This is an estimate, not a promise.

Questions:
1. How much is the monthly rent and what has the landlord already said about continuing?
2. How much of the €31,000 of costs does not change along with sales (rent, electricity, a helper)? This will decide whether this year's profit is closer to €2,000 or €5,400.
3. Is the kiosk your only income?
