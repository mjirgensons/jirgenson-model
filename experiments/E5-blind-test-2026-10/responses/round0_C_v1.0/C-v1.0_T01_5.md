Your shop earns in season and gives much of it back in winter. You cannot pay for the e-bikes from the account this year.

**Where you stand**

A season month leaves €3,300 after parts, fixed costs and your own pay. A winter month loses €3,700. Over a year that is +€23,100 and −€18,500, so €4,600 in total. The €12,000 for the bikes is 2.6 years of that.

**The greatest danger**

Around 10 February the account hits zero while rent and your mechanic's wage are still due. That happens even without the bikes; paying for them from the account at the end of October would bring it to early November. I propose a red line at €2,000, one month's rent, since below it the premises are at risk. Without changes you cross it around 24 January, so this is an October job.

**What to do**

- By 9 October, you: ask your bank for an €8,000 credit line for February to May. It costs nothing until used, and banks agree more readily while your account looks healthy. It covers the €6,200 gap plus a €1,800 cushion. Also ask about leasing the bikes, with payments from April.
- By 31 October, you: from November take €500 a month instead of €1,500, which saves €5,000. Start selling winter work: spring tune-ups paid in advance, winter servicing, bike storage. Aim for €1,000 a month more in sales; each €1,000 adds €700 to the account, so €3,500 over the winter. Together that is €8,500 against the €6,200 gap.
- On 2 March, you: decide on the bikes with the winter result in hand. To earn back €12,000 in one season, each bike must bring in about €245 a month for seven months (assuming the same 30% costs). First seasons often only just break even.

**What we protect first**

From last to give up to first: the shop opening in April with your mechanic and the premises; the account above €2,000; your own pay; the e-bikes are the first to go. This order is my proposal. If yours differs, tell me; if the bikes outrank your pay, your pay is cut further to fund a first batch.

**What not to do now**

Do not sign or pay a deposit on the bikes this month, and do not cut your mechanic's hours before the season; you need him in April.

**What to check, and when**

Every Monday: the account balance and the prepaid bookings sold. Full check on 2 November: October's real result against €3,300. If on 1 December the account is under €10,300, the plan is behind, and we deepen the cuts or draw on the credit line.

**Two forecasts**

If nothing changes: about €12,300 at the end of October, then down €3,700 a month, to a low of −€6,200 (overdrawn) at the end of March (−€4,800 to −€7,600 if winter sales run 10% higher or lower), back above zero around the end of May. If the plan works: the account bottoms at about +€2,300 at the end of March (+€900 to +€3,700 on the same ±10%), credit line unused. Condition: the pay cut and the extra sales happen.

I am assuming October brings the €14,000 average (each €1,000 less is €700 less in the account) and no lump-sum bills such as insurance or tax.

One question: what do you charge per day to rent a bike, and on how many days a month is a bike out in season? That decides whether the e-bikes pay back.
