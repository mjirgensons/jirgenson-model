Clearly, 55 hours a week while turnover is falling is hard. In short: marketing can stop the decline, but I do not see +30% next year, and decisions should not be based on it.

## What your numbers show

Turnover has been falling for the second year in a row by ~12% (€48 → 42 → 37 thousand), and this year it is about 10% lower: €25,000 for nine months gives roughly €33,000 for the year. I assume the last three months are average; if December is stronger, it will come to 34–35 thousand, and the conclusion does not change. The town has a quarter fewer people, and the kiosk's turnover has shrunk by almost a third since 2023. That is your strongest headwind, and advertising does not turn it around.

+30% on €33,000 is about €43,000: €10,000 more, or about €27 every day. (If you count from last year, it would be €48,000, the 2023 level.) And your time: €6,000 for 2,860 hours (55 × 52) is about €2 an hour. Even if +30% worked, the extra €10,000 would give at least €1,600 (16%, which is your current profit share; possibly more, because the rent does not go up), that is about €0.6 an hour. So more important than marketing is the question of what these hours give you.

## The biggest threat

The end of the lease around June 2027. If you do not talk it through in time, you may lose the place; if you sign a new long lease, you will tie yourself to a kiosk with falling turnover. The talks need to start in January, the decision must be made by March.

The €4,000 savings are about 8 months of what the kiosk brings you at the moment (~€500 a month). I suggest a red line of €2,500 (5 months): below it, one bad season or a rent deposit can empty the savings completely. The forecast does not cross this line as long as the kiosk makes a profit; it can be crossed by a rent increase or by money that goes into advertising.

## What to do yourself

- From Monday, 19 October, for two weeks count the receipts by hour and what people buy. This shows the empty hours that can be closed. Until 2 November.
- By 15 November: call 2–3 service providers that bring people in every day (for parcels, bills, tickets for local events), and find out the commission. Start with one. No more than €500 for experiments.
- By December: tell local employers, the school and the municipality about the new things in person; it costs only time.
- In January: talk to the landlord; ask for a shorter term or the right to end the lease with three months' notice.

## What to protect and what not to do now

I suggest this order, starting with what I would give up last: the savings and a life without debts; income for your hours; the kiosk itself; +30% is what I would give up first. If the kiosk matters more to you than the hours, tell me, and the emphasis will shift to the lease talks.

Do not: paid advertising and large stocks of new goods, a loan, a new long lease while there are no figures on the hours, and more than 55 hours.

## Two forecasts and a check

If nothing changes: in 2027 about €29–30 thousand (a decline of 10–12%), profit, if it follows turnover, about €4,500–5,000, that is below €2 an hour.

If the plan works: €33,000–37,000 (on average 35,000), if the new services outweigh the further decline, about €100 of turnover a week. This is not a promise.

Every Monday, write down the receipts, the turnover and the savings balance. Review on 30 November; if the new thing brings in less than €50 a week, we no longer spend on it. If the savings fall below €2,500, the experiments stop.

Two questions: 1) How much does the rent cost per month and what does the landlord say about extending? 2) How much of the turnover is newspapers, lottery and other goods, and how much of each euro stays with you?
