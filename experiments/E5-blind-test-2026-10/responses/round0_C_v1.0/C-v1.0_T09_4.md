Turnover cannot be raised by 30% next year with marketing, and I cannot promise that. The realistic target is to stop the decline, and the most important decision is not advertising but extending the lease in 8 months. Three years of decline and 55 hours a week are hard, but there is still time to prepare.

**Where you are**

Turnover is falling for the third year: –12.5% (2024), –12% (2025), and €25,000 in nine months means about €33,000 this year (–10%). If the end of the year is stronger, maybe €35,000; the conclusion does not change even with a 10% error. Growth of 30% would be about €43,000, more than in 2024. The town itself works against it: from 8,000 down to 6,000 residents and the biggest factory closed. That is stronger than any advertising.

Last year's €6,000 for roughly 2,860 working hours is about €2 an hour. Costs last year were about €31,000 (37,000 − 6,000), so about €2,600 a month; the €4,000 savings cover about one and a half months.

**The biggest threat**

The end of the lease around June 2027. A long extension while turnover is falling can eat up the savings. I suggest a red line: savings below €2,600 (one month of costs). At the moment you are above it, but close; in the worst case (costs do not fall) you would approach it around the end of the lease, so the decision must start being prepared already now.

**What to do**

This week, you yourself:
- Find the notice period and the extension terms in the lease. By 31 October, ask the landlord in writing about an extension for 6–12 months with the right to end it with 1–3 months' notice, or about lower rent.
- For two weeks, write down sales by hour and by product group (newspapers, lottery, the rest). This will show what earns money and which hours do not pay off; by shortening your working hours, you will get your time back.

By December, three cheap tests, one at a time with 3–4 weeks in between, to see what works:
- a service that brings people in: parcel pick-up, bill payment, tickets (first ask the partners about the commission);
- home delivery or a subscription for older customers;
- short messages with the week's offers in local Facebook groups.

For the tests, in total up to €300 (my suggestion, less than a tenth of the savings).

By March 2027 you need to decide: a short extension, moving to a place with more foot traffic or cheaper rent, or another source of income. The decision is yours, I will prepare the numbers.

**What to protect first and what not to do**

My suggestion for how to rank what to hold on to until the end (if it is different for you, tell me): first the savings and a life without debts, then enough monthly income, then your time and health; turnover growth I would give up first.

Do not: do not sign a long lease before reviewing the numbers, do not take a loan for growth, do not spend more than €300 on advertising, do not buy new equipment.

**What to measure and two forecasts**

Every Monday (the first on 19 October): the week's takings versus the same week last year and the savings balance. A bigger review on 15 January. If for three months in a row turnover is more than 12% lower than last year, we make the decision about the lease earlier.

If nothing changes: in 2027 about €29,000–31,000, profit before your pay from about –€2,000 (costs do not fall; the worst end) to about €5,000 (costs fall along with turnover).

If the plan works: illustratively assuming that the tests win back 5–10% of turnover (a guess, not a promise), about €31,000–34,000. The decline stops, but that is not enough for about €43,000. Condition: at least one service makes a profit and the rent does not get worse.

Questions that would narrow the forecast:
1. How much is the rent per month and what other fixed costs are there?
2. Is the €4,000 only the kiosk's reserve or all of the family's savings?
3. Is there other work or income you could switch to if the kiosk does not bring in enough?
