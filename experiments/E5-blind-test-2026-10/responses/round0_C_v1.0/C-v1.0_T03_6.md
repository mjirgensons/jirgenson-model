You have a working base: €18,000 in and €2,500 profit a month, a 14% margin (I take margin as profit divided by revenue). Each member brings €150 and costs €30 to serve, so each one adds €120 toward your fixed costs of €11,900. Against that, one move in the plan turns profit into loss, and several others cannot be paid for as written.

## Weak spots, most important first

**1. The 20% price cut.** At €120, each member adds only €90. With 120 members you would lose about €1,100 a month. You need 133 members to break even and 160 to earn what you earn now: a third more members just to stand still. Nothing in the plan shows that price is why people leave or stay away, and a cut is the hardest move to undo, because raising prices later loses members. It also fights your 25% margin goal: that needs about 145 members at today's price, and about 199 at the lower one.

**2. The tax arrears are filed as a wish.** "Sometime this year" is not a date, and unpaid tax is an obligation, not a goal you can drop when money is tight. €9,000 is 3.6 months of your entire profit; over 12 months it is €750 a month, 30% of what you earn now. Interest and penalties normally keep growing, and enforcement can follow. Contact the tax office this week and ask for an instalment plan in writing, before any deadline passes. Every spending goal below competes with this money.

**3. The cushion is thin, and the new studio is already across the road.** You make money from 100 members up, so I would treat 100 as your red line. At 120 you are 20 members (one in six) above it, and every member lost costs €120 a month. Losing 20 takes profit to about zero with the tax debt still standing. That is the nearest real danger, and growth spending should wait until it is covered. I don't know your account balance or how many members you have lost since it opened, so I can't say how close the line really is.

**4. Teachers, second room, app: costs with no figures.** All three add cost, and I can't size it without numbers. Every extra €1,000 a month of fixed cost needs about 13 more members at today's price (17 after the cut) to keep a 25% margin. I also don't know how many members your current room can hold. That one number decides whether goals 5 to 7 are needed now or can wait.

**5. Eleven goals, and most are not yet goals.** Eleven split your attention; three to five are usually enough for a year. Goals 1 and 2 are one thing seen from two sides (revenue is members times price), and neither has a number. Goals 3, 5, 6 and 7 are ways to reach a result, not results. Goals 10 and 11 have no number or date: how many hours a week, which rating by when. What remains as real goals is margin, member count, complaints, your hours and reviews, with the tax outside the ranking as a must-pay.

Goal 8 is well defined; name who counts the complaints. The price cut, the margin target, the hires and the second room pull against each other, and all of them against the tax payments. Tell me which of the four you would give up first and which last, and I will show how the numbers move.
