With marketing alone I do not promise +30% next year, and your own numbers show why. This winter's most important decision is the lease, not advertising. This week, start writing down the till takings by hour for two weeks (5 minutes a day): this will show which hours work.

**Where you are**

In 2026 so far, on average €2,780 a month; for the year it will come to about €33,000 (I assume average last three months; with a strong December up to €35,000). Turnover is falling for the third year: about 12% a year, this year about 10%. Last year €6,000 of profit for 55 hours a week is about €2.10 an hour. This year, if profit stays at about 16% of turnover, it will be about €5,300. The €4,000 savings are about nine months of what the kiosk now leaves you.

+30% on €33,000 is about €43,000, more than in 2024. If the decline continues, 2027 will be about €30,000, so the gap is about €13,000. The town has shrunk by a quarter, the factory is closed: that holds things back the most, and advertising does not overcome it, it only slows the decline.

**The biggest threat**

The lease ends around June. A new long lease with falling turnover would bind you even if the town kept shrinking, and against that there are only €4,000 of savings. I suggest a red line: the savings must not fall below €2,000 (about 4.5 months of the kiosk's profit; below that there is no longer time to find other income). The forecast does not cross this line if the savings are not spent.

**What to do**

"+30%" is a result, but marketing is only a method. What is in your hands is the working hours, the range of goods, the rent and small local events:

- This week: a record of takings by hour.
- By 1 December: ask 20 regular customers what they buy elsewhere, and try one service that brings people in (parcels, bill payments, coffee); find out the commission. Announce it on the door and in the local group. All together no more than €500 from the profit, not from the savings.
- By the end of January: based on the hourly record, cut the empty hours (my suggestion: no more than 45 hours a week) and start a conversation with the landlord: 12 months with an exit option and lower rent, referring to the fall in turnover. Also read the contracts with the newspaper distributor and the lottery operator: what they provide for if the kiosk is closed or moved.

**What to protect first**

I suggest this order, starting with what I would give up last: your living and savings above €2,000; your time and health; the kiosk itself; and growth, which I would give up first. If the kiosk is more important to you than your time, tell me, then we can give more ground on the rent.

Don't do now: do not sign a new multi-year lease, do not take a loan and do not buy large stocks until there are January data.

**What to measure**

Every Monday: last week's takings versus the same week last year and takings per hour. Every month: turnover versus €2,780 (at a +30% pace it would be about €3,600). Review on 1 December, decision on the lease by the end of February. If at the end of January the weekly takings are still more than 10% below last year's, then we drop a long lease extension and together consider a short term, moving or closing.

**Two forecasts**

If nothing changes: in 2027 about €30,000 (€28,000–32,000; the worst end if the decline speeds up to 15%), profit about €4,800 for the same 55 hours. If the plan works: €31,000–35,000 (the decline slows or stops, the new service adds €2,000–3,000 a year, this is an assumption), profit about €5,000–5,600 with about 45 hours. €43,000 is outside both ranges; it cannot be promised, these are estimates.

Questions: 1) how much does the rent cost and who owns the premises? 2) what does the turnover consist of (newspapers, lottery, other) and what is the commission for each? 3) how much a month do you need to get from the kiosk in order to continue?
