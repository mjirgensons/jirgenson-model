Your numbers show a profit of €2,500 a month, a 13.9% margin. The plan as written would turn that into a loss, mainly through the price cut. Here are the weak spots, most important first.

### 1. The 20% price cut costs more than you earn

At €120 per member, your 120 members bring in €14,400 instead of €18,000. Only €30 per member of your costs goes away, so you land at about −€1,100 a month, or −€13,200 a year. Even if your costs are 10% lower than you gave me, you would be near zero.

At the new price you would need 133 members to break even, 160 to get back to today's profit, and 199 for a 25% margin, which is 79 more than now. Without the cut, 25% needs about 145, which is 25 more. I put the red line at zero profit. The cut crosses it in the first month, so that is an alarm already ringing, not a plan.

The cut is a way, not a goal; the goal behind it is keeping members against the new studio, and you haven't said how many you have lost or what it charges. I am assuming your €15,500 includes your own pay and the €30 per member stays the same; if not, tell me.

### 2. The tax arrears have no date and no monthly amount

€9,000 is 3.6 months of today's profit. It is a legal debt, not something to trade against growth, yet it sits near the bottom of your list. "Sometime this year" is a wish: unpaid arrears usually collect interest and penalties, and tax offices can freeze a bank account, which would stop wages and rent at once. Over twelve months it is €750 a month, 30% of today's profit, the very money the cut removes. If you mean this calendar year, 11 weeks remain, about €6,000 of profit at today's pace.

### 3. Three growth moves add fixed costs before the members arrive

Two teachers, a second room and an app come with no cost or capacity figures, so I cannot say what the plan yields even if it works. Each €1,000 a month of added fixed cost needs about 12 more members to hold 25% at €150 (about 17 at €120), and you pay months before they arrive. With thin profit and open arrears, there is no cushion for a mistake. Can your current room and teachers hold more than 120 members? If so, the room and hires can wait.

### 4. Goals that cannot all be met

- More members and higher revenue are one goal at a fixed price. With the cut, revenue passes €18,000 only at 150 members.
- A 25% margin, the price cut and the growth spending pull against each other. Choose the cut and you need 199 members for 25%. Keep the price and you need about 145, but risk losing members to the new studio.
- Working less sits badly with an app, a room, hires and a tax debt, which all take your hours.

If you had to drop goals one by one, which goes first and which last? That order decides which side wins each conflict.

### 5. Eleven goals are too many, and some are not goals yet

Eleven goals split your attention; three to five are usually enough for a year. The cut, the app, the teachers and the room say how, not what. More members, higher revenue, working less and better reviews have no number or date. Complaints have no starting figure, and the same rate gives more complaints as you grow.

Smaller point: nothing says who checks each goal or when. Cash wants a weekly look, members and profit a monthly one.
