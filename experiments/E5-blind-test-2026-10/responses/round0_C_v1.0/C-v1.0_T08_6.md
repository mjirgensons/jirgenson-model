**What is going on**

You are profitable on paper but short of cash: your accountant counts an invoice when you send it, the bank when it is paid.

Your balance fell €9,000 in six months, to €13,000 (about €1,500 a month), while unpaid invoices grew by €13,000. Six months of €3,000 profit is €18,000; minus the €13,000 stuck in invoices, the bank should be up about €5,000, not down €9,000. A second hole of about €14,000 (roughly €2,300 a month) is not explained by the late customer. Drawings, loan repayments, equipment, VAT or an overstated profit could be it.

I am assuming sales of about €29,000 a month (expenses plus profit), so your biggest customer buys about €11,600. Moving them from 30 to 90 days ties up about €23,000; only €13,000 has shown up, so up to €10,000 more may come.

**The biggest danger**

Your €13,000 is half a month of expenses. I propose a red line at one month (€26,000): below it, one late payment forces a choice between wages and suppliers. You are already under it. The danger is your biggest customer's next invoices slipping again while the second hole stays open: the balance could fall to about €5,000 by mid-January, under a week of expenses, and wages would be at risk.

**What to do**

- By Friday, you: phone the customer's accounts department. Ask for a dated plan for the overdue amount and a part-payment this week, confirmed by email. EU business invoices carry statutory late-payment interest and at least €40 per invoice; mention it.
- From today, you: until they owe you less than about €12,000 again, ask 50% up front on their new jobs.
- By Wednesday 21 October, your accountant: a one-page list of every payment out of the bank in six months that is not in expenses. If it is your own drawings, trim them; that lever is yours. Ask also about VAT: in many countries it falls due on invoicing, before you are paid.
- This month, you: invoice the day the job is done, remind at day 31, phone at day 45, for every customer. Ask the bank now, while the balance is positive, for an overdraft limit you do not use unless needed.

**What to protect, and what to leave alone**

What I would protect first: your three people paid on time; then suppliers and rent; then the big customer, on shorter terms; then the €3,000 profit; your own withdrawals bend first. This is my proposed order; if yours differs, tell me.

Not now: new equipment, hiring, discounts for volume, an ultimatum to the big customer (losing 40% of sales would likely mean a loss), or a quick high-interest loan.

**Check and outlook**

Each Monday, check the balance in months of expenses, total unpaid invoices, and what the big customer owes. First check: Monday 19 October. No part-payment by 30 October, or a balance under €8,000, means that customer pays before delivery.

If nothing changes: about €5,000 to €15,000 by mid-January; €5,000 is the worst end. If the plan works: €22,000 to €25,000 by 31 January, assuming you recover €7,000 to €10,000 of the €13,000 build-up; that is still just under the red line. These are ranges, not promises. If the €14,000 was a one-off, add about €7,000 to both.
