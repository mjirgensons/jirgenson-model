In short: +30% (about €43,000) with marketing next year is not a realistic target. The town lost a quarter of its residents and its biggest employer, turnover is falling for the third year, and advertising here goes against the current. The real task is to stop the decline and reach the end of the lease with a calmly made decision. Three years of decline are hard.

## Where you are
Turnover: €48,000, 42,000, 37,000; this year about €33,000 (€25,000 in nine months; I assume the last three months are similar, if Christmas is better, up to €35,000). That is a decline of 10–12% a year. Last year's profit of €6,000 for 55 hours a week is about €2 an hour. Costs about €31,000 a year or €2,600 a month, so the €4,000 savings are 1.5 months. If +30% worked, that would be about €10,000 of extra turnover; assuming you keep 20–25 cents of each euro, that is €2,000–2,500 of profit a year (if 40 cents stay, about €4,000; the conclusion is the same). So the plan has to be cheap.

## The biggest danger
The end of the lease around June 2027. If you extend the lease for several years on the current terms or the owner raises the rent, the blow falls on your €4,000. I suggest two red lines: savings below €2,600 (one month of costs) and turnover for three months in a row more than 10% below the same month last year. The forecast does not cross the first line by June, unless a deposit or higher rent has to be paid. The forecast (–10…–12%) already touches the second line: it is not a limit but an alarm that is already ringing. So growth spending waits.

## What to do
This week, at no cost: for two weeks, write down turnover by hour. Hours in which nobody comes can be closed (if the contracts allow it); this raises income per hour faster than advertising.

By the end of November: discuss the lease with the owner, without signing anything. Ask for a 12-month term, lower rent or rent that follows turnover, and termination with 2–3 months' notice.

By the end of December: try one thing that brings people in every week: a parcel pick-up point, bill payment, ticket sales or newspaper delivery for older people (check your contracts first). One at a time, comparing with the same weeks last year. Cap: €300 and 3 hours a week taken from the empty hours.

By the end of February: choose between extending on better terms, moving to where people still go (by a shop, a stop) and a calm wind-down. The decision is yours; I can prepare a comparison with numbers.

## What to protect first
I suggest this order (it is mine, not yours): first I would give up the +30%, then some of the hours; the last thing I would give up is the €4,000 savings and the possibility to decide about the kiosk calmly, not under pressure from the lease deadline. If your order is different, tell me; for example, if the kiosk matters more to you than the savings, part of the money can be set aside for moving.

## What not to do now
Do not sign a long lease on the current terms, do not spend the savings on big advertising, equipment or stock, do not take a loan for growth. Also do not wind down the kiosk in a hurry: data are still missing.

## What to measure and what to expect
Every Monday: the week's turnover versus the same week last year, and the savings in months. The first check on Monday, 2 November. If any red line is crossed, we make the decision about the kiosk earlier.

If nothing changes: in 2027 turnover €29,000–30,000, profit €4,000–4,600. If the plan works (the decline stops, the empty hours are closed, a new service draws people in): €32,000–34,000, profit €4,800–5,400. There are no guarantees; one good month does not yet prove anything.

Questions: 1) How much does the rent cost per month and for how long would the owner be ready to extend? 2) How much of the turnover comes from newspapers, lottery and other goods, and how much of each stays with you? 3) Is the kiosk your only income?
