**Where you stand**

A season month leaves €3,300 after parts, fixed costs and your own pay (14,000 − 4,200 − 6,500). A winter month loses €3,700 (4,000 − 1,200 − 6,500). Over a year the shop nets +€4,600 on top of your pay, so the €12,000 for e-bikes is about 2.6 years of that. Your €9,000 covers 1.4 months of fixed costs. I am assuming October is an average season month, which puts you at about €12,300 on 1 November.

**The greatest danger**

The winter. By my proposed red line (one month of fixed costs, €6,500, below which rent and the mechanic's wage get hard to cover), you cross in mid-December, and the account is empty around 10 February. If October is weak (say €10,000 of revenue), that moves to mid-January. A 10% error in winter revenue is only about €1,400, so this holds.

The e-bikes cannot come from the account: €9,000 is less than €12,000, and paying at the end of October would leave about €300, so the account is empty in the first week of November.

**What to do**

This week:
- Do not sign or pay for the e-bikes. If the supplier wants a deposit by a date, ask to move the order to February or March.
- Ask your bank for an overdraft of at least €6,500 as a safety net. Banks say yes more easily while the account still shows €9,000.
- Decide your own pay for November to March. Leaving it out is worth €7,500, the biggest thing you control; halving it, €3,750.
- Sell prepaid winter servicing and spring tune-ups now, while customers are still bringing bikes in. Aim for €1,500 more revenue a month in winter.

This month: ask the landlord about lower winter rent and the mechanic about flexible winter hours, before touching anything you cannot get back.

**What we protect first**

My proposal, change it if your order differs: the shop opening in April with the mechanic and rent paid on time; then an account above €6,500; then your own pay; the e-bikes last. If you rank the e-bikes higher, they need a loan with payments only from April to October, and I would rebuild the plan around it.

**What not to do, and what to watch**

Do not buy the e-bikes from cash or on a loan with winter payments, and do not cut the mechanic yet. Look at the e-bikes again on 1 February. To pay back within one season, each bike must net about €170 a month over the 7 months; over two seasons, about €86.

Every Monday, check the balance against the path if nothing changes: €8,600 on 1 December, €4,900 on 1 January. Next review: 1 November. If the balance that day is under €10,000, or you fall €1,000 behind the path, the plan changes.

**Two forecasts**

If nothing changes: zero around 10 February, then €6,200 below zero at the end of March (the overdraft would just cover it), back above zero around the end of May.

If the plan works (no own pay in winter, €1,500 extra revenue a month): the account stays positive and ends March at about €6,500. With only €1,000 extra a month, about €4,800, under the red line, so the overdraft stays on standby. Tax or VAT falling in winter would come on top.
