The biggest problem: goal 3 (the price cut) undoes goals 2, 4 and 9, and goals 5–7 add costs the plan doesn't count. In order of importance:

**1. The 20% price cut works against your own numbers.**
Your variable cost is about €30 per member (€3,600 ÷ 120), which leaves roughly €11,900 of fixed costs. Today you make €2,500 a month, a 13.9% margin. A cut to €120 removes €30 from every existing member, €3,600 a month, before a single new member joins.

| Scenario | Revenue | Costs | Profit | Margin |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Today (120 members at €150) | €18,000 | €15,500 | €2,500 | 13.9% |
| €120, same 120 members | €14,400 | €15,500 | −€1,100 | −7.6% |
| €120, 150 members | €18,000 | €16,400 | €1,600 | 8.9% |

You would need 150 members (+25%) just to hold revenue flat, and you'd still earn less than today. The plan also assumes price is why people choose the new studio, and nothing in it shows that.

**2. The 25% margin can't coexist with the price cut, and it ignores goals 5–7.**
At €150 a head, 25% needs about 145 members (+25) with no new costs. At €120 it needs about 199 (+66%). Two teachers, a second room and an app all add fixed cost, and the plan gives no figure for any of them. Each extra €1,000 a month of fixed cost needs about 12 more members at €150 (about 17 at €120) to keep that 25%. It's also unclear whether "25% by December" means December alone or the full year.

**3. The tax arrears are unfunded and open-ended.**
€9,000 is 3.6 months of your entire current profit, and that same profit is supposed to pay for the app, the room and the teachers. "Sometime this year" has no monthly amount or start date, so it will drift to the end. It's also a debt that can grow with interest or penalties, so it shouldn't sit at the same level as a wish list.

**4. The app, teachers and room have no reason, cost or trigger.**
Is your current room full? If not, a second room adds rent and no members. If it is, that's your real growth argument and the plan should say so. For the app: what does it change for retention or revenue? For the teachers: hired before or after the members arrive? Hiring ahead of growth hurts margin in exactly the months goal 4 is measured.

**5. Eleven goals, one small profit.**
There's no priority order, timeline or budget per goal, and several conflict outright (3 vs 2, 4 vs 5–7, 9 vs 5–7). With about €2,500 a month to spend, the cash will decide the order for you.

**6. "More members" and "higher revenue" aren't goals yet.**
No number, no date. Goal 2 is simply the result of goal 1, and goal 3 shrinks it. Missing too is churn: how many members leave each month? Growing by 25 net could need far more than 25 new sign-ups.

**7. The baseline may be flattering.**
Does the €15,500 include a wage for you? If not, your true margin is below 13.9% and every threshold above rises.

**8. Goals 8, 10 and 11 have no starting point.**
How many complaints do you get now, and what rating do you have? "Work less" has no hours figure, and it competes with launching an app, opening a room and onboarding two teachers in the same year. Complaints should be per member too: 3 a month means something different at 120 members than at 200.

If you fix only one thing first, decide whether the price cut stays. Almost everything else depends on that answer.
