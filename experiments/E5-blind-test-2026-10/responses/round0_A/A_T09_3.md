In short: +30% with marketing alone next year is not a realistic target, and in my view it is not the right target either. Below I explain why, and what I would do instead.

**What the numbers show**

- Turnover has been falling three years in a row, by ~10-12% each year. €25,000 in the first nine months means about €33-35 thousand for the whole of 2026. If the trend continues, 2027 will be about €30 thousand.
- +30% on the 2026 level is about €43-44 thousand, so back to the 2024 turnover, while the town has a quarter fewer people. You would have to turn −10% into +30%.
- Turnover is falling at roughly the same pace as the number of residents (about −30% in three years versus −25% of residents in five). This is not a problem of too little advertising but a shrinking customer base, and advertising does not bring it back.
- €6,000 for 55 hours a week is about €2 an hour before taxes. Even if +30% worked, at a 25% margin (this is my assumption, check it on your side) an extra ~€10 thousand of turnover would give about €2,500 of extra profit. That is close to minimum-wage level for a very long working week.

So the real question is not how to grow by 30%, but: how to make the kiosk pay you acceptably, or how to stop it in time and without losses when the lease ends. The lease term is your real clock, and marketing should be a cheap test in support of this decision.

**What I would do**

1. **In the next 2-3 weeks, collect the data.** Sales by category (newspapers, lottery, the rest) and by day of the week/hour, comparing 2023 and 2026. You need to know exactly what is falling and when the kiosk is empty. And how many euros of each euro sold stay with you (margin), by category.
2. **In the next 3-4 months, run cheap tests**, in total no more than €300-500; keep the remaining €4,000 as a safety cushion. Ideas that can add real turnover in a small town:
   - services that bring people to you every week: parcel pick-up, bill payments, ticket sales (commissions and requirements must be checked with the operators);
   - home delivery of newspapers and subscriptions for older customers, who are your most loyal;
   - a range with a better margin: stationery for schoolchildren, postcards, hot drinks, snacks;
   - the local Facebook group and a clear sign at the kiosk about what you now offer, which costs almost nothing.
   
   For each test, write down how much extra turnover and profit it brings per month. Whatever doesn't work after 6-8 weeks, drop it.
3. **Cut your hours if the data support it.** If a large part of the day is almost without sales, shorter working hours raise profit per hour more than any advertising.
4. **Make the decision on the lease by month 5, not in the last week.** Write down a criterion already now: if the monthly turnover and profit after rent do not reach a specific level, do not renew the lease, or renew it only for lower rent and a shorter term. In a small town with a shrinking population, the landlord most likely has fewer alternatives than you, so start the talks in good time.

**A more realistic target for next year:** stop the decline, add +5-10% with the new services and raise profit per hour worked, rather than reaching €43 thousand of turnover at any cost.

To write a concrete 90-day plan with numbers, I would need: (1) the split of sales by category, (2) the margin, (3) the rent and whether the landlord is ready to negotiate, (4) whether you want to continue this business in the long term, or are also open to ending it if the numbers don't improve.
