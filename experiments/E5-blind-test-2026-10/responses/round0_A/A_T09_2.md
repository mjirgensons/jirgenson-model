In short: +30% with marketing in this situation is very unlikely, and even if it worked, it would not solve your main problem. I explain why, and what I would do instead.

**What the numbers show**

If the last quarter is similar to the quarters of previous years, 2026 will come out at about €33–35 thousand. Turnover has been falling by roughly 10–12% a year for the third year now, and that is on the same scale as the fall in the number of residents. So, most likely, what is missing is not advertising but buyers.

+30% of €34 thousand is about €44 thousand, which is higher than in 2024. If the trend continues, 2027 will be about €30–31 thousand, so you would have to find €13–14 thousand of extra turnover in a town that is losing residents. Advertising raises demand for newspapers and lotteries only a little: people already know the kiosk exists.

More important is your own work. €6,000 for about 2,800 hours a year is about €2 an hour, or €500 a month before taxes. If the extra €13 thousand brought 16–25% profit (16% is your current margin, 25% is an optimistic assumption), that would be +€2–3 thousand a year, roughly +€1 an hour. Even if reached, the target would leave you well below the minimum wage. In addition, rent and other fixed costs do not go down along with turnover, so the 2026 profit is most likely already below €6,000. Check the actual figure.

**What to put in place of the target**

Not +30%, but: stop the decline and increase profit per hour worked. If in 2027 you hold on to about €34 thousand, that is already roughly 10% better than the trend.

**Plan until the end of the lease**

1. **The next two weeks: measuring.** Break down turnover and profit by category (newspapers, lottery, everything else) and sales by hour and day. Most likely you will find hours (late evenings, Sundays) when there are almost no customers in the kiosk. If you cut 55 hours a week to, for example, 45, turnover may barely change, but your pay per hour will go up.

2. **November–December: keep the existing customers.** With such thin profit, discounts are dangerous: a 5% discount at 16% profit eats up a third of the profit. Other ways work more cheaply: newspaper subscriptions with home delivery for older people, arrangements with the school, the library and the pensioners' association, posts in the local Facebook group. €300–500 is enough for these tests.

3. **January–February: one service that brings people back repeatedly.** For example, a parcel pick-up point, bill payments, ticket sales, printouts or hot drinks. Before you start, find out how much you keep per transaction and how many transactions a day you need for it to pay off.

4. **Rent.** The lease ends in 8 months, and the landlord most likely does not have many other tenants. Ask for lower rent, a shorter term (6–12 months), an option to leave earlier, or rent that partly depends on turnover. Until the lease is extended, do not invest in repairs or equipment.

5. **Decision by March.** Set now the numbers by which you will decide, for example: if in January–February the monthly turnover is at least €X and profit per hour at least €Y, then I continue; if not, I plan an exit. You set Y yourself: what income is acceptable to you? The €4,000 savings are a safety cushion for the exit, not money for advertising. Closing the kiosk is also a decision, not a failure: a kiosk earning about €2 an hour does not have to be saved at any cost.

To turn the plan into concrete numbers, write: (1) the split of turnover by product group, (2) the monthly rent and other fixed costs, (3) what the kiosk sells or offers in addition to newspapers and lottery, (4) whether you want to keep the kiosk in the long term or could also close it.
