The rent here is not a catastrophe but simple arithmetic. The real task is to get the money out of the client, and you need to start today: the credit line only covers the next month and a half.

**The numbers**

- For the rent you are short 1,100 − 450 = €650.
- It is enough to take about €800 from the line (650 plus a small buffer). Interest: 800 × 12% ≈ €8 a month. That is nothing compared with the problems caused by late rent.
- €1,200 will remain on the line. That is roughly one next rent payment if the client never pays. So you cannot delay the escalation.

**What to do today, in order**

1. **Credit line (10 minutes).** Check that the line is active, that there is no withdrawal fee and no minimum amount, and how long the transfer takes. Draw about €800 today or tomorrow, not on the last day: weekends and bank delays happen. Ask whether you can repay early without a penalty. When the client pays, you will close the debt right away.

2. **Call the client.** Emails are being ignored, which means you need another channel. Call not only the contact person but also the accounts department or the finance department, or the person who signed the contract. The goal of the call is to find out the reason, not to ask "when will you pay". Possible reasons: the invoice did not arrive, it has not been approved, they are waiting for the acceptance certificate, there is no money, there is a complaint about quality. Get a specific payment date and ask them to confirm it in writing. If the problem is with only one invoice, ask them to pay at least the second one right away.

3. **On the same day, send a formal reminder.** Attach both invoices, state the amounts, the dates and the payment deadline, for example 5 working days. Refer to the clause of the contract on payment terms and the late-payment penalty. The tone is calm and businesslike, without emotions or threats. Send it to the contact person, the accounts department and, if there is one, to the legal address from the register; keep a copy for yourself. If you can, duplicate it by registered letter.

4. **Check the client itself.** Look in the company register and in the news to see whether the company is operating, whether there is a bankruptcy or a change of owner. If there are alarming signs, you have less time than it seems, and you need to act faster.

5. **Stop new work for this client**, if any is under way. Do not hand over new results and do not give access until the overdue amount is paid. First check the contract: suspension in case of late payment is usually allowed. Report this neutrally in the same email.

6. **Protect yourself through other clients.** Invoice for everything that has been done but not invoiced. Ask for faster payment of current invoices and an advance on new projects.

**If the client keeps silent**

- After 5–7 days: a letter from a lawyer or an official demand for payment.
- After 2 weeks: simplified recovery (a court order). In many EU countries this is inexpensive and can be done without an attorney.
- If you are in the EU, the late payment directive 2011/7/EU gives the right to statutory interest (the ECB rate + at least 8 percentage points) and a fixed compensation for costs of €40 or more. The details depend on national law, so check the rules of your country.

**What you should not do**

- Write angry emails or publicly accuse the client.
- Take quick loans at a high interest rate: you already have a cheap line.
- Pay the rent after the due date "and hope for the best".
