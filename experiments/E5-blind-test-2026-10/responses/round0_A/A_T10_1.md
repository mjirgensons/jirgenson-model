The situation is unpleasant, but not critical: you can cover the rent, and with the client it is time to move from emails to firmer steps. The order for today is as follows.

**1. Rent: close the question with the credit line**

You are short €650 (1,100 − 450). Taking €700 from the line for a month costs about €7 in interest (12% a year is roughly 1% a month). That is cheaper than any late rent payment and the stress. Just check the terms of the line: whether there is a withdrawal fee or a minimum payment. You can draw it closer to the payment date, not necessarily today. The remaining limit (about €1,300) together with your wife's salary is your cushion for at least another month.

**2. Client: change the channel of communication today**

- Call, don't write. Separately the person who ordered the work, and separately the accounts department. The goal is to find out the reason: the invoice got lost, it is stuck in approval, wrong bank details or order number, or they have no money. And to get a specific payment date.
- If they don't pick up, send an email to several people at once (the person who ordered the work, the accounts department, the manager) with both invoices, the amounts, the dates and how long they are overdue. Set a deadline: payment within 3–5 working days.
- Calmly mention the late-payment penalty. If you are in the EU, under Directive 2011/7/EU in B2B you are entitled to late-payment interest and compensation of at least €40 for each invoice (the details depend on the country and the contract).
- Offer an easy way out: pay one invoice right away, the second one next week. A partial payment is easier to agree on.

**3. Check whether the client itself is in trouble**

Look at the trade register and the news: is there a bankruptcy, a liquidation, complaints from other contractors. A month of silence is a reason to check. If there are signs, act faster and go to a lawyer straight away: positions in the queue of creditors are lost quickly.

**4. Lever: the work**

Put new tasks and the handover of results on pause until payment or a firm date. First look at the contract: whether there is a right to suspend work, whether the rights to the results pass only after payment. Don't switch off or delete anything in their systems: only stop new work.

**5. Safety net**

- Invoice other clients for everything that has been done and not yet invoiced, and ask them to speed up payment.
- Write out all payments for the next 4–6 weeks (taxes, insurance, loans) to see your real margin.
- Postpone everything you can by 2–3 weeks.

**If the silence continues**

After 5 working days — a formal demand with confirmation of delivery and a deadline of 7–10 days. Then a lawyer, simplified recovery of undisputed debts (in many EU countries this is inexpensive) or an agency that works for a percentage of the result.

For the future: an advance of 30–50%, staged invoices, a payment term of 14 days, a late-payment penalty in the contract, and no single client bringing in more than a third of your income.
