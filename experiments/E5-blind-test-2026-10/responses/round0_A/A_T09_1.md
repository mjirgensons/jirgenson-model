In short: with marketing alone, +30% in this situation is unlikely to work. Even if it did, it would not solve your main problem. Below are the numbers that show this, and a plan of what I would do instead.

## What the numbers say

- **Turnover is falling by about 12% a year** (€48 → 42 → 37 thousand). €25 thousand for nine months means about €33-35 thousand for the whole of 2026, depending on December. The decline continues, only a little slower (5-10%). If nothing changes, 2027 will be about €31 thousand.
- **+30% on this year is about €44 thousand**, so about 40% above the trajectory you are on now. That is almost the 2023 level, but there are a quarter fewer residents, and the main product, newspapers, is shrinking across the whole country. The decline matches what happened to the town, not anything you are doing wrong. Marketing can help people remember you, but it cannot create buyers in a town that is emptying out.
- **Even if the target worked, it would not be enough.** Last year €6,000 for roughly 2,860 hours (55 × 52) is about €2 an hour, or about €500 a month. If we assume your gross margin is 20-25% (this is my assumption, you need to check it), an extra €10 thousand of turnover gives €2,000-2,500 of profit, and that is before marketing costs. That is about €3 an hour, while the minimum wage is about €4.5 an hour gross. So "+30%" does not turn this work into something that pays off.

## So I would change the goal

Not "+30% turnover", but: **by the end of the lease (around June 2027), work out whether this place can be turned into one that pays you at least minimum-wage level for your time. If it cannot, leave without losses.**

Eight months without commitments is your advantage: you can try cheap things and decide with the numbers in hand. The €4,000 savings are a safety cushion, not a marketing budget. For experiments I would set aside no more than €300-500.

## Plan

**1. October-November: sort out the numbers (no cost)**
- Turnover and margin by group (newspapers, lottery, other goods) and by month.
- Fixed costs: rent, utilities, accounting.
- For two weeks, write down sales by hour. If 10 of the 55 hours bring almost zero turnover, cutting your working hours is the fastest way to raise your hourly rate.
- Lease: does it renew automatically, how far in advance you must give notice to end it, is there an exit point.

**2. December-February: 2-3 cheap tests**
- *Service point:* parcel pick-up and drop-off, bill payments, tickets, printing and copying. The commissions are small, but people come in and while they are there buy a newspaper or a lottery ticket.
- *Keeping existing customers:* newspaper subscriptions and home delivery for older people. They are staying in the town and are your most stable group of customers.
- *Seasonal goods:* calendars, greeting cards, school supplies in August.
- *Visibility:* a post in the town's Facebook group, a clear sign at the kiosk about everything you offer, a leaflet in letterboxes. Check what you are allowed to advertise when it comes to lotteries.
- Before each test, write down how much income a month it has to bring and by what date. If it doesn't, stop.

**3. February: decision**
- **A) Continue, but on different terms.** Lower rent, a shorter term, an exit option. For example, rent €100 a month lower is €1,200 a year, and that is almost half of the whole +30% effect. In a town that is shrinking, the landlord doesn't have many alternative tenants, so your position in the talks is not bad. Start the talks in March at the latest.
- **B) Turn it into a local service point**, if the tests justified it.
- **C) Wind down by the end of the lease:** sell off the stock, give up the newspapers and find a paid job. Full-time work at the minimum wage gives about €780 a month gross, and that is more than the current roughly €500 for 55 hours.

## So that the plan can be adjusted

3. Is the €6,000 before or after taxes, and does anyone else also work in the kiosk some of the time?
4. Where is the kiosk located: in the centre, by a school, a shop or a stop?

If you send these numbers, I will make an exact calculation (how much turnover you need for your hourly rate to reach the minimum wage) and a concrete month-by-month plan.
