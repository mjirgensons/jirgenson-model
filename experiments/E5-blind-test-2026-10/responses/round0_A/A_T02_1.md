**What to do, in this order**

1. **Call the inspector first.** Your 72 hours started yesterday, so they run out around the day after tomorrow, which is the same day as the repair. If the deadline falls mid-morning and the repairer comes in the afternoon, you could be late through no fault of your own. Ask for the exact deadline time. Ask whether a paid, dated repair order plus a re-inspection request keeps your permit alive. Ask how to book the re-inspection, what you may sell in the meantime, and what to do with the current stock. Write down the name, the time and the answer, and confirm it by email.

2. **Book the repair and pay the deposit, by card if you can.** That keeps your €1,900 in the account. Before you pay, ask:
   - What exactly was diagnosed? Was the compressor tested, or is it a guess?
   - Is €2,400 fixed, including VAT and labour?
   - What warranty comes with it?
   - Can the €1,400 balance be paid in two parts, or a few days after completion?
   - Is there a cancellation slot earlier than the day after tomorrow?

   Get the answers and a receipt in writing, because you'll show them to the inspector.

3. **Protect the stock.** Move it out of the 9°C unit today into coolers with ice, or into a borrowed fridge from a friend, a neighbouring restaurant or a rented chiller. Check it with a thermometer and keep a written log. Don't serve high-risk items (meat, fish, dairy, cooked rice, sauces) that have spent a long or unknown time above 4°C. A rough rule is more than about four hours. One food-poisoning case would end the business much faster than a €600 loss. Ask the inspector what they'd accept.

4. **Talk to your two part-timers today, not on payday.** Wages are legally protected, so you can't just delay them, but you can ask. Something like: "I'll pay €X on day 4 and the rest on day 5 or 6, when a customer deposit arrives. Is that OK?" Get a yes in writing, even as a message. People usually agree to a short delay when they hear it early and get a firm date.

5. **Chase the wedding deposit.** Check it is actually scheduled. Send a friendly reminder with your bank details and ask whether they could pay a few days early. Don't treat it as money until it's in your account. Also line up a plan B for the wedding, such as a rented refrigerated unit for the day, in case the repair slips.

6. **Fill what's left with cheap, short-term money.** In order: the repairer's payment terms (free), the staff agreement (free), a loan from family or a friend for about ten days, then a card or overdraft (a few days costs little). Avoid payday-style loans.
