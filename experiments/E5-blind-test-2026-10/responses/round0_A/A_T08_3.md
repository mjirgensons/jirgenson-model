You earned the money, but customers are holding €13,000 of it. That part you fix by collecting. The other €14,000 left through something that doesn't show up in profit. The usual suspects are owner withdrawals or dividends, loan or lease principal repayments, equipment purchases, VAT or tax payments, and bulk paper or stock purchases. Depreciation makes the gap slightly bigger, since it lowers profit without costing cash.

**Why it's urgent:** €13,000 covers only about two weeks of your €26,000 monthly expenses. The average burn (about €1,500 a month) looks gentle, but a payroll run plus a supplier bill landing before a customer payment can leave you short. If the 90-day pattern hasn't fully worked through your invoices yet, receivables could also keep climbing.

**This week**

1. **Phone the big customer (don't email).** Find out why: a dispute, their own cash trouble, or slow approvals. Get a firm payment date for the overdue invoices and ask for a part-payment now. Until the overdue amount is cleared, ask for a deposit on new work. They're 40% of your sales, so aim for a payment plan, not a fight. If you learn they're in financial trouble, treat the overdue amount as a possible loss, not a delay.
2. **Chase every other overdue invoice.** Call, don't just send reminders.
3. **Ask your accountant for a one-page profit-to-cash reconciliation for the six months.** It should list every outflow that isn't in the P&L. That finds the €14,000.
4. **Build a 13-week cash forecast.** One row per week: payroll, rent, suppliers, VAT, and expected receipts by the date you realistically expect payment, not the due date. It takes an hour or two and shows whether any week dips below zero.
5. **Hold what can wait.** Owner withdrawals, equipment and bulk buying should pause until the overdue money arrives.
6. **Ask your bank for an overdraft or credit line now.** It is much easier to get while your account still looks healthy.

**Over the next month**

- Take a 30-50% deposit on large jobs and move new work to 14-day terms.
- Set a credit limit per customer, and stop work when it's exceeded.
- State late-payment interest on your invoices. In the EU, businesses can usually claim interest and a fixed recovery fee on late B2B payments (check your country's rules).
- Reduce dependence on one customer. At 40% of sales, their payment habits set your cash position.

One caveat: this is based on your rounded numbers. The reconciliation in step 3 will give the exact figure for the gap.
