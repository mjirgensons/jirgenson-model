I can't give you an exact monthly profit or guarantee a payback period. The number that decides both is the second salon's monthly revenue, and nothing you've told me fixes it. Any "guaranteed" figure would be invented. What I can give you is the exact cost side, and what each revenue level means for payback.

**What is certain**
- Rent plus two technicians: €1,800 + €3,200 = €5,000 a month before a single client walks in.
- Everything else a salon carries (supplies, utilities, insurance, card fees, employer taxes on wages) is not in your numbers. I used €2,400. That makes total running costs €7,400, which is the first salon's €6,800 of costs excluding your pay, plus the €600 higher rent. Swap in your real figure if you have one.
- Break-even revenue is therefore about €7,400 a month. Below that, the salon loses money.

**Monthly profit and payback on the €28,000 fit-out (steady state)**

| Monthly revenue | Monthly profit | Payback |
|---|---|---|
| €7,400 | €0 | never |
| €8,000 | €600 | about 47 months |
| €9,000 | €1,600 | about 17.5 months |
| €10,000 | €2,600 | about 10.8 months |
| €11,000 (same as salon 1) | €3,600 | about 7.8 months |

Formula: payback in months = 28,000 ÷ (monthly revenue − 7,400). I did not charge your own pay to the second salon, since you are already paid from the first.

**Two things matter more than the table**

1. **Ramp-up.** A new salon rarely hits full revenue in month one. Every month below €7,400 adds its loss to the €28,000 you have to win back. For example, if the first 6 months average €5,000 of revenue, you lose about €2,400 a month, or €14,400, before you start recovering the fit-out. On the €9,000 row, payback then stretches from 17.5 months to roughly 32 months from opening.

2. **Funding.** The fit-out is €28,000 and your reserve is €15,000, so you are €13,000 short even if you spend the entire reserve. That would leave no cushion for ramp-up losses. The first salon's surplus after your pay is €2,200 a month, so closing the gap from that alone takes about six months. If you borrow the difference, interest lengthens every payback figure above.

**Bottom line:** don't plan on copying the first salon's €11,000 from day one. I'd use the €9,000 row as the plan and make sure you can survive the €8,000 row. If you can't fund the gap and the ramp-up without emptying the reserve, the risk is in the cash timing, not only in the payback number.
