**The short version**

The business itself is sound: 120 members at €150 leave you about €2,500 a month, a margin of about 14%. The plan is what puts it at risk. The 20% price cut and the 25% margin cannot both happen, and the €9,000 tax arrears are a debt to settle first, not a goal to rank against the others. You break even at about 100 members, so today you have a cushion of 20 members.

**What is wrong, most important first**

1. **The tax arrears have no monthly amount and no agreement.** "Sometime this year" is a wish. Tax debt usually grows with interest or penalties and can end in enforcement (rules differ by country; check yours). Many tax offices agree to instalments if asked before enforcement starts. Next step, this week: ask yours in writing for an instalment plan. €750 a month for 12 months would take 30% of today's profit. I read "this year" as the plan year; if you mean before 31 December, profit alone will not cover €9,000 in that time.

2. **The price cut turns your profit into a loss.** At €120, 120 members bring €14,400 against €15,500 of costs: −€1,100 a month, about −€13,200 a year, with nothing left for the tax office. Break-even moves from about 100 members to about 133, above the 120 you have; this holds even if the €3,600 of variable costs is 10% off. To keep today's revenue you would need 150 members. A cut only beats holding the price if the new studio would otherwise take more than about 30 of your 120 members; I see no sign of that in your numbers.

3. **Goals 3 and 4 contradict each other.** At €120, a 25% margin needs about 199 members, 79 more than now. At €150 with today's costs it needs about 145, which is 25 more (about two a month). That is arithmetic, not a promise, and it assumes you have room for them.

4. **Teachers, a second room and an app cost money before they bring members.** None has a price in your plan. Every €1,000 a month of new fixed cost needs about 8 to 9 more members at €150 just to stay level, while the margin is supposed to rise from 14% to 25%. The second room only makes sense if the first is full, and the plan does not say how full it is. These are ways to reach goals, not goals.

5. **Eleven goals split your attention.** Three to five are usually enough for a year. "More members" and "higher revenue" are one goal (members × €150). Members, working less and reviews have no number or date yet, so they are wishes. My reading, which you can change: five real goals once each has a number (margin, members, complaints, reviews, your own hours), one fixed obligation (the arrears) and four methods (price cut, app, teachers, room).

**The rest, one line each**

- Margin 25% by December: say whether December alone or the whole year counts, and whether the €15,500 includes your own pay; if not, the real margin is lower.
- Fewer than 3 complaints a month: fine, but there is no current number, and more members will push it up.
- Nothing in the plan warns you early if members are leaving; count cancellations monthly.

**Three checks to do first**

- This week: write to the tax office to ask for an instalment plan.
- Before any price decision: count how many members left or asked about price since the new studio opened.
- Give teachers, room and app a monthly cost, so each can be tested against the margin.

One question: if you had to give goals up one by one, which would go first and which last? In particular, would you drop the price cut or the 25% margin first?
