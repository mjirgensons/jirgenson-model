The €10,000 target helps with your second goal: if the biggest client stays at today's level, its share at €10,000 would be about 36%. For "stop depending on one client" I would draft the line as no client above about 30% of income; that is my proposal, so correct it if you mean something else.

**What to do this week**

Two steps cost nothing and keep your options open:

- Ask the departing client in writing for the exact date their work stops, and whether some work stays, for example checking and correcting the machine output. Companies often still want a human check for legal or customer-facing text, usually at a lower rate per word; it is worth asking.
- Talk to your biggest client about the next 6 to 12 months of work, and look up the end date and notice period of any contract with them.

Leave your employee's hours and pay, and any new spending, untouched until the dates and costs are clear.

**Five questions before I write the plan**

1. Dates. When exactly does the departing client's work stop? Does your biggest client have a contract, and if so, when does it end and what is the notice period? Has that client said anything about AI tools?
2. Money. What do you pay out each month (your employee, rent, software, the share you set aside for tax), and how much is in the account today? I am assuming the €10,000 is money coming in from clients, not what is left for you; is that right?
3. What goes first. If things got tight and you had to let go of things one at a time, which would you give up first and which last: the 12-month date for €10,000, independence from the biggest client, your employee's job, your own pay, your free time? My guess until you correct me: the date goes first, then independence from the biggest client, and last your employee's job and paying the bills.
4. Capacity and work. What kind of texts and which languages do you translate, and how many hours a week are free for you and for your employee today?
5. New clients. How many new clients did you win in the last 12 months, and how did they find you?
