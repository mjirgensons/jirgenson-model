Frankly: +30% next year is not a realistic goal, and an honest analysis cannot promise it. The more important task is a different one: stop the decline, get your hours back and arrive at the lease decision in June with numbers in hand.

**Where you are**

Turnover is falling for the third year: −12.5%, −12%, this year €25,000 in nine months, on an annual basis about €33,000 (−10%). I assume the last three months are the same; if they are 20% better over the holidays, the year will end around €35,000, still below last year. The +30% goal would mean about €43,000, more than in 2024, in a town that has lost a quarter of its residents. That is not the fault of marketing, and advertising will not bring the factory back. Even if it worked, each extra euro would leave you 15–30 cents (my assumption), so only €1,500–3,000 a year. Your €6,000 for about 2,860 hours a year is about €2 an hour.

**The biggest danger**

The end of the lease around June 2027. I assume the notice period is 3 months, so the decision day is around 15 March (check in the lease). If you extend for several years at the current rent, you will pay from a profit that without changes will be about €3,800–4,900 in 2027; if you then have to leave, the remaining rent will be covered by the €4,000 savings. Leaving too early, you lose the spot and about €3,000–4,000 of income for 8 months. Closing can also be right if you have a job with a normal wage, but decide that in March, with data.

**What to do**

- This week: read the lease — notice period, rent, whether it can be extended for a year.
- By 30 October: for two weeks write down the number of customers and the takings by the hour (till report or a notebook). If, for example, 15 of the 55 hours are almost empty (an illustration), you get back up to 780 hours a year, losing little turnover.
- In November: two tests, in total no more than €300 and 3 hours a week. The first: a parcel handout or bill payment point (usually brings people in and pays a small fee per item; terms differ, ask the service providers). The second: prepaid subscriptions for regular customers, they give money up front.
- In January: with the 2026 total, talk to the landlord about a one-year extension with the option to end it with 2–3 months' notice; in a small town an empty space is often worse for the landlord than lower rent.

**What we protect and what not to do**

My suggestion (if your order is different, tell me): the last thing I would give up is the €4,000 savings, with a red line at €3,000; the forecast does not cross it in the next 8 months as long as profit is above zero. Then your income per hour worked, then the kiosk itself, and the first thing I would drop is the +30% goal. Do not sign a multi-year lease now and do not close the kiosk before you have the lease numbers and the hours data; do not take a loan for growth and do not spend the savings on advertising.

**What to measure and two forecasts**

Every Monday: the week's takings against the same week last year (this year on average about €640) and the number of customers. Checks on 30 October, 15 January and the decision on 15 March. The tests help if the fall in takings against the same period last year is significantly smaller than this year's −10%; otherwise do not commit for longer than a year.

If nothing changes, in 2027 turnover is about €29,000–30,000, profit €3,800–4,900 before your pay, the same 55 hours. If the plan works (the decline stops, the empty hours dropped): €31,000–33,000, profit about €4,000–6,000, with fewer hours. This is not a promise.

Questions that would narrow the forecasts the most:
1. How much is the rent per month and the other fixed costs, and what is the notice period?
2. How is turnover split between press, lotteries and other, and how much of each euro stays with you in each group?
3. Is there another job or income you could move to if the kiosk does not hold up?
