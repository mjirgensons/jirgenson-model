You have been working against the current for three years now, and it is not your fault: the kiosk most likely follows the town. So, honestly: I do not promise +30% next year with marketing alone. I would suggest moving the goal to what you can change yourself: income per hour and a safe lease.

**Where you are**
The town has shrunk by a quarter (8,000 → 6,000), turnover since 2023 has shrunk by about a third (€48,000 → about €33,000), about 10–12% a year. I assume that the last three months of 2026 will be similar to the first nine (€25,000 ÷ 9 × 12); if you sell more at Christmas, the year may end around €36,000. Then +30% is about €43,000 (in the second case about €47,000), more than in 2024. Your income: €6,000 a year for 55 hours a week is about €500 a month, or about €2 an hour (I assume you work all year).

**The biggest danger**
The danger is the end of the lease around mid-June 2027. If you sign a long lease on the current basis, you take on a fixed payment for the whole term, but the kiosk will bring in less than now (forecast below). If you leave too early, you lose €6,000 a year and a spot that is hard to get back; the €4,000 savings would replace this income for only about eight months. The decision must be made by mid-March, and talks with the landlord must start in January; check the notice period in the lease: many leases require 1–3 months' notice, and then the decision must be made earlier.

Red line (my suggestion): turnover below €2,500 a month for two months in a row (about €30,000 a year); below that, a new long lease is too risky. If nothing changes, the forecast reaches this line close to the end of the lease, so it is already a signal, not a distant limit.

**What to do**
What we protect to the last (my suggestion): your health and time, then the €4,000 savings, then the kiosk income, then the kiosk in this location; the first thing to drop is +30%. If your order is different, tell me.
- This week: for four weeks, write down turnover by the hour and then drop the weakest hours. It is quick and reversible if you are wrong; income per hour grows more safely this way than with advertising.
- This week: write out the monthly turnover since 2023; it is an argument in the lease talks.
- By December: an eight-week test, no more than €200 and 3 hours a week: reserved newspapers and magazines for regular customers, a post in the local residents' group, a poster by the kiosk. Also look at an additional service that brings people in, for example parcel pick-up or bill payments (the terms depend on the provider, check).
- In January: ask the landlord for a 6–12 month extension or a lease that depends on turnover. Where there are fewer residents, it is usually not easier for the landlord to find another tenant.

Do not: do not spend the savings on advertising or stock, and do not sign a long lease before mid-March; while turnover is falling, growth spending only empties the reserve faster.

**What to measure and what to expect**
Every Monday: last week's turnover and hours worked. Once a month: turnover against the €2,500 line. Next check on 16 November. If turnover is below €2,500 for two months in a row, do not sign a lease for longer than six months and start preparing an exit.

If nothing changes: 2026 about €33,000 (up to €36,000), 2027 about €29,000–30,000 (worst end about €29,000), profit about €4,000–5,000 (if costs do not fall along with turnover).
If the plan works (fewer hours, cheaper lease): turnover holds at the €31,000–35,000 level, but income per hour grows. I do not consider +30% (about €43,000) a credible outcome; a forecast is not a promise.

Three questions that change the decision:
1. How much is the rent per month, and for how long does the landlord usually extend?
2. How much of each euro of turnover stays with you (press and lottery separately)?
3. What was turnover in October–December 2025?
