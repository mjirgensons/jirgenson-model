Nobody can honestly give an exact monthly profit or guarantee a payback period, and your figures give the second salon's costs, not its income. They do show that the €28,000 fit-out is almost twice your €15,000 reserve, and that payback runs from about 12 months to never.

**The numbers**

Your first salon leaves €2,200 a month after your €2,000 pay; the reserve is 1.7 months of expenses. The second salon costs €5,000 a month before the first client (rent €1,800, technicians €3,200). I assume €1,000 more for supplies and utilities, so it breaks even at about €6,000 revenue, 55% of the first salon's. I also assume slow filling: 40% of full revenue in months 1 to 3, 70% in months 4 to 6.

- Full revenue €6,000 a month: profit €0, start-up loss €16,200, payback never.
- €8,000: profit €2,000, start-up loss €9,600, payback about 25 months.
- €11,000: profit €5,000, start-up loss €4,800, payback about 12 months.

These are scenarios; payback includes start-up losses. An extra €500 of monthly cost (employer contributions on wages can add that; check yours) moves the middle case to about 33 months.

**The greatest danger**

The day you sign the lease: from then €6,000 a month falls due whether clients come or not, and monthly costs rise from €8,800 to €14,800. My proposed red line is a reserve under one month of expenses. Even with the fit-out fully financed, your €15,000 is then 1.0 month, right on the line; paying any of the fit-out from it crosses the line that day, an alarm already ringing. A 10% dip in the first salon (€1,100) halves the €2,200 that carries everything.

**What to do**

I have ranked what you protect: the first salon and your pay, then the reserve; the opening date gives way first.

The range narrows most with the first salon's ramp-up history, bookings per technician, and clients you can pre-book.
