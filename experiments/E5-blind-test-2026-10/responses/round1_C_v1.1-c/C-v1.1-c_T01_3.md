**Where you are**

The shop earns about €4,600 a year after your own pay (€118,000 revenue, €35,400 parts, €78,000 fixed costs), all of it from the season. A season month leaves about €3,300; a winter month loses about €3,700, so €18,500 over five months. With October at its average you will have about €12,300 on 1 November.

**The greatest danger**

Winter empties the account before spring. Losing €3,700 a month, you are down to about €1,200 at the end of January and at zero around 10 February, when rent and your mechanic's wage still fall due. By 31 March the hole is about €6,200, before any bike spending.

I propose a red line of €3,700 (one month of winter loss). Today's €9,000 is above it, but this path crosses it around 10 January: three months to act, but it is the default path, not a remote risk.

The €12,000 for bikes does not fit in €9,000, and a loan payment would fall in winter too.

**What to do**

- You, by 7 October: decide your pay for November to March. Pausing the €1,500 is the biggest step you control: +€7,500, turning the March hole into about +€1,300.
- You, by 15 October: ask the landlord for a lower winter rent, and review your mechanic's winter hours: €3,500 a month alone exceeds the €2,800 that all winter work leaves after parts. Rules depend on country and contract; check yours.
- You, by 31 October: sell winter work now: service packages, and deposits for next season's e-bike rentals. That brings cash and tests demand before you spend €12,000.

Rent, hours and sales together must find about €4,800: €2,400 to reach the red line on 31 March, and €2,400 cushion (a 10% revenue miss).

**What we protect first**

My proposal, from what you would give up last: the shop paying rent and wages on time, debt-free; then your own pay (paused, not lost); the spring e-bikes go first. If the bikes matter more than staying debt-free, tell me; the plan then becomes a financed purchase and a harder winter.

**What not to do now**

Do not order the bikes, pay a deposit or sign a loan before early March. Then buy only if cash on 1 March is above €3,700, the payment fits the winter and deposits show demand.

Buying earlier from cash puts 31 March near −€10,700, even with your pay paused. Waiting costs the bikes' income for every week they miss; to repay in one season ten bikes must net about €1,700 a month, in two seasons about €850, and a first season is often slower while customers learn the bikes exist.

**What to measure and when**

Every Monday, the account balance. Monthly, compare it with this path (pay paused): 1 December €10,100, 1 February €5,700, 1 March €3,500. A month starting €1,500 lower means cutting winter costs further that week. Next review: 1 November.

**Two forecasts**

If nothing changes: zero around 10 February, then about −€6,200 on 31 March (range −€8,600 to −€3,800, so negative either way), back above zero in May. If the plan works: about +€6,100 on 31 March (worst case +€3,700). Bike payback I cannot promise; the range narrows most with the day rate, rented days per bike, extra insurance and upkeep, and financing cost.

I assumed each month equals its season average and no tax or large bill falls in winter; one would deepen the gap by the same amount.
