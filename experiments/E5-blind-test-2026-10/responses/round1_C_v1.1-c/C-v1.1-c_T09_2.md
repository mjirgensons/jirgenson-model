In short: +30% next year with marketing alone is not achievable, and I would not put my €4,000 on it. More important than marketing is the lease end date. Three years of decline and 55 hours a week are a heavy load.

**Where you are**

Turnover is falling by 10–12% a year (48,000, 42,000, €37,000), and this year it will come to about €33,000 (€25,000 in nine months). Since 2023 turnover has fallen by about 31%, the population by 25%. The +30% goal means about €43,000, which is more than in 2024, and the town will not get back the residents who have left (at €35,000 the conclusion is the same too).

Profit last year was €6,000 for about 2,900 hours, which is about €2 an hour. This year's profit is a range, because I do not know how much of your costs fall along with sales: €2,000 if none, up to €5,000 if most. The €4,000 savings are about 1.5 months of costs (about €2,600 a month).

**The biggest threat**

The end of the lease around mid-June 2027. If you extend the lease for several years at the current price, you will tie yourself to payments that profit may not cover, a profit that is already only €2,000–5,000. The weakest point is the savings: I suggest a red line at 1 month of costs (about €2,600), you are at 1.5. In the worst 2027 scenario (a loss of about €2,000) the savings would fall to about €2,000 and cross this line around autumn, so the decision must be on 31 March, which leaves 2.5 months for the notice period.

Staying too long costs up to €2,000 of losses and another approximately 2,900 working hours a year, plus obligations to the landlord. Leaving too early costs €2,000–5,000 of profit a year and the chance to come to an agreement on the lease.

**What to do**

**Today (you).** Open the lease and write out the notice period and whether the lease renews automatically. In many places silence can extend the lease; check yours.

**By 23 October (you).** Write out 24 months of turnover by group: press, lottery, other.

**In November and December (you).** One trial at a time, 4–6 weeks, to see what attracts customers: parcel handout (in many places a fee is paid for it) or press delivery to a school, a care home and workplaces.

**By 31 December (you).** Ask the landlord in writing for an offer: 6–12 months, lower rent or the option to end early. In a shrinking town landlords usually give way, because empty premises bring no income, but that is not guaranteed.

**What to protect and what not to do**

The order is my suggestion; if yours is different, tell me. I would hold on the longest to: safety (no debts, savings not below €2,600), then your health and hours (no more than 55 a week), then the kiosk as a source of income. The first thing I would give up is +30%. If the kiosk is more than income to you, I would put it higher.

Do not sign a lease for several years and do not spend more than a few hundred euros on advertising. Advertising has to be paid from profit, not turnover: if a quarter of each euro stays (my assumption), an extra €10,000 would give only about €2,500.

**Check and forecasts**

Every Monday: number of receipts and turnover for the week (the number of receipts is an earlier signal); once a month: turnover against the same month last year, and savings in months. Next check on 16 November. If savings fall below €2,600, we stop all spending except rent and stock. If on 31 March the landlord offers only the current terms, we assess handing over or closing the kiosk.

If nothing changes: 2027 turnover about €29,000–30,000, result from −€2,000 to +€4,000. If the plan works: the decline stops, turnover €33,000–35,000, result as this year (€2,000–5,000). This is not a promise, but depends on the town and the result of the trials.

How much is the rent per month and what does the landlord say about continuing? Do you also spend the savings on living costs?
